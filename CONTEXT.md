# Save7 OS

Internal ops platform for Save7, a student-run organisation operating across university branches. This glossary defines the roles, org units, and navigation concepts that gate access throughout the app.

## Language

**Save7 OS**:
The product — this platform, live at `os.save7.org`, commissioned by Save7's Exco from the user and Gilbert Lieb jointly. **One system, one name**, before and after the restructuring currently being designed: there is no second product, and nothing in this glossary describes a rival to it. Where a sentence needs to separate the system as it stands from the model being designed for it, that is a difference of **version, not of system** — say "Save7 OS today" and "the target model" rather than reaching for a second name.
_Avoid_: Save7 Operations OS, Save7OS, the OS, Gilbert's system, Nerve Centre (which names the effort, not the product)

**Nerve Centre**:
The planning effort that designs Save7 OS's organisational structure, and the repo it lives in — its wayfinding map, the decision tickets under it, and the spec they become. Names **work, never software**: Nerve Centre holds no application code, and nothing a volunteer signs into is ever called this. So "the Nerve Centre role model" means the model this effort designs, while "the Save7 OS role model" means the one the product carries. Named after the retired Coda nerve-centre document that hub content is drawn from; spelled British, like the rest of this glossary.
_Avoid_: Nerve Center, the Nerve Centre app / platform / system, a second Save7 OS

**Branch**:
A Save7 chapter at one university (e.g. UCT, Stellenbosch, Wits, UP, UJ), and the **entry point** through which a volunteer joins the organisation. A volunteer recruited at a branch works under that branch's team, and every project they take on stays under that branch's Branch Management regardless of where the project itself runs. A branch is not compulsory — a volunteer recruited outside any university has no branch (see Volunteer). Open-ended table managed by National Exco alone — added, edited, or deleted outright, on the Operations Hub's Branches page; no admin status confers this. Carries a separate active/inactive status reflecting current activity/momentum, not whether it still exists — an inactive branch is still fully editable, just flagged as needing a push to get moving again.
_Avoid_: location, chapter, campus

**National Exco**:
Org-wide leadership seats — CEO, COO, CGO (Chief Growth Officer — see CGO), CFO. Full access to every hub across every branch and department. Provides overall oversight, alignment, and accountability across both the Branch and Department systems. Authors Department Head MOUs directly; reviews and signs off on Branch-Manager-authored Project Lead MOUs — routed to them by the General Liaison. Super Admins by default and irrevocably. Exco assigns every seat in the organisation — Exco seats, General Liaison, Department Heads and Branch Management, the Exco seats themselves and the Advisory board moving only by **Resolution** — grants or revokes Super Admin status, adds branches and departments, and deletes a volunteer account outright — all of it worked from the **Users** hub, which Exco open with the General Liaison. **For now an Advising member of the Advisory board holds these acts beside Exco and opens Users too** (see Advisory board), though no Exco seat moves except by Resolution. Exco also hold every branch-level power across every branch, and every Project-level power across every Project: they edit any Project's eligibility, its status, its overview, its project tracker and the ladder it names, write to its log, and they alone remove a Project Lead where no successor is taking over, the MOU sign-off having been theirs. No one outside Exco, whatever admin tier they hold, can create or alter an Exco seat or override an Exco decision. The four seats hold the **same access** — each sees and does everything Exco can — and the same acts, save for a short list reserved to the CEO alone (see CEO) and **editing the Operations Hub's own content**, which the CEO and the COO hold with the General Liaison while the CFO and CGO read it (see Operations Hub). **The Finance Hub is not divided that way**: all four seats post to the ledger, mark Claims paid, record Section 18A certificates, set Budgets and keep its settings alike — and so does the General Liaison, who writes the books beside them (see General Liaison) — and what makes it the CFO's is that its national duties name the CFO (see CFO). Where the CEO seat is vacant, those acts fall to the remaining Exco acting together — save approving a Resolution, which waits for the new CEO, the Resolution appointing them excepted (see Resolution); there is no second-in-line and no deputy. In the OKRs Hub, Exco alone writes the Mission & vision, the Strategy and the Pillars, and may author an Objective onto any unit — national, any branch, any Department. Their weekly meeting is the one meeting the platform records (see Meeting); Exco read every Meeting and the whole Action Point tracker, may log a meeting themselves as backstop to the General Liaison, and may tick any Action Point done.
_Avoid_: leadership, management, executives, exec team, CMO (no longer an Exco seat — see Department Head)

**CEO**:
The National Exco seat carrying the greatest authority in the organisation. Holds the same access as the other three seats, plus a short list of acts that are the CEO's alone — an **open list**, added to as the model grows, presently: **approving a Resolution**; **returning an Advisory board member from Observing to Advising**; and **naming a successor** when stepping down. One more act the CEO holds without holding it alone: **editing the Operations Hub's own content**, shared with the COO and the General Liaison and not held by the CFO or CGO. Everything else National Exco holds, all four seats hold equally — including assigning seats, granting Super Admin, adding branches and departments, deleting an account, and inviting an Advisory board member to a meeting. Most of what makes the CEO the CEO happens off the platform, and the model says so rather than manufacturing powers to justify the title.

**Does not leave by removal in the ordinary way.** A CEO **steps down** by Resolution — their own act, carrying their own approval — which may name their successor; naming one installs them, and reaches the CEO seat only, every other seat staying National Exco's collective appointment. A CEO who will not go is **removed** by a Resolution approved by the **remaining National Exco instead of by the CEO** — a majority of the filled remaining seats, never fewer than two — — the single Resolution the CEO does not approve, and the only reason the seat has any involuntary exit at all. Where no successor was named, the remaining Exco appoint the next CEO by Resolution, carried the same way, rather than by ordinary seat assignment, every movement of this seat being a Resolution matter.

Shown first and labelled wherever the Exco seats are listed, so that the authority is visible to everyone and not only to the person holding the buttons.
_Avoid_: chief executive, president, chair, head of Exco, managing director, boss

**COO**:
The National Exco seat the **Operations Managers report to** — the operations line runs from each branch's Operations Manager to the COO, as the finance line runs from each Finance Manager to the CFO — and the owner of the **Operations Hub**. Holds the same access as the other three Exco seats, and one act the CFO and CGO do not: **editing the Operations Hub's own content**, which the COO shares with the CEO and the General Liaison — and with it the backstop over every **Event**, worked from that hub's Events page. Beyond that, what the seat adds is that **national operations duties sit with the COO by name** — approving what Operations Managers submit, chasing an Agreement past its renewal date — rather than with National Exco as a whole, since a duty sits with whoever is explicitly named and otherwise with the lowest level that can act on it — and holding Exco's authority over operations is not by itself a reason for all four seats to owe it. Along with the Branch Manager and the Operations Manager, funnels volunteers with a specialised interest toward the matching Department.
_Avoid_: head of operations, operations admin (Save7 OS today's job title for an account carrying finance permissions, not this seat)

**CFO**:
The National Exco seat the **Finance Managers report to** — the finance line runs from each branch's Finance Manager to the CFO with no national Finance Department between them — and the owner of the **Finance Hub**. Holds **no finance permission the other three Exco seats lack**: all four write there alike. What the seat adds is that **national finance duties sit with the CFO by name** — Claims approved and awaiting payment, Claims a Finance Manager cannot decide (their own, those with no branch, and every Claim of a branch with no Finance Manager yet), Spend requests awaiting a decision, Section 18A certificates outstanding, a Budget period lapsed with no replacement, and the budget plan of a Project with no home branch — rather than with National Exco as a whole, holding Exco's authority over money being no reason for all four to owe it. Whether the CFO is a **co-signatory** is a matter of the bank mandate, not of the seat (see Co-signatory).
_Avoid_: finance admin (Save7 OS today's role carrying every finance write, which no seat in the model inherits whole), treasurer, head of finance

**CGO**:
Chief Growth Officer — the National Exco seat that **covers funders**: the organisation's relationships with the people and bodies who fund it, worked closely with the CFO on the money those relationships bring. Holds the same access as the other three Exco seats and **no act they lack** — covering funders is a field of responsibility, not a permission, and the Finance Hub is written by all four alike. Like the CFO, reads the Operations Hub's own content rather than editing it.
_Avoid_: CMO (no longer an Exco seat), head of growth, fundraising lead

**Advisory board**:
Retained Exco capability, held by people who have served on National Exco. Not an honorary title and not a consulted body with rights of its own — an Advisory board member keeps working access to the organisation, in one of two states. **Advising**, the default, is full edit across every hub, every branch and every department — everything a sitting Exco member does day to day — and, **for now, the acts the model would otherwise keep for the sitting Exco as well**: assigning seats, granting or revoking Super Admin, adding branches or departments, deleting a volunteer account, and opening the **Users** hub. They are kept because every member of the board has sat on National Exco and has held these acts before, and because the board carries much of the organisation's work at present. They are due to be revisited. An Advising member never holds the CEO's own acts, and never moves an Exco seat or the board's own membership, which happen only by Resolution. Like the CFO and CGO, an Advising member reads the Operations Hub's own content rather than editing it. Stepping down surrenders a seat at Exco's table, the CEO's acts with it where it was the CEO's; the working access stays. **Observing** is read everything, change nothing. An Advising member opens the **Users** hub, where access to Save7 OS is decided, beside the sitting Exco and the General Liaison; an Observing one does not. Close to Super Admin in shape, but reached by seat rather than by admin status and so not identical to it — the Meetings page of the Operations Hub's Compliance section is read by the advisory board and explicitly not by Super Admins. A member may step themselves down to Observing at any time; returning to Advising needs the CEO. Entry is never automatic — a Resolution places a retiring Exco member on the board, so an Exco member removed for non-performance does not reach it. Membership has no expiry but is not irrevocable: a Resolution removes it. Closed to anyone who has not served on National Exco. Members hold no standing place in Exco's meeting — any Exco member invites them and the General Liaison routes the invitation — but they read its archive: the record, not the room. They do not read the Action Point tracker (see Meeting, Action Point). Underneath the seat they remain a Volunteer with their original branch, like everyone else.
_Avoid_: board, board of directors, advisory council, emeritus, former Exco (names the history, not the seat), advisory role

**General Liaison**:
Single org-wide seat, distinct from National Exco. The routing point between Exco and everyone reporting up to it: Branch-Manager-authored Project Lead MOUs pass through them on the way to Exco sign-off, Department Heads' **Department reports** wait on their Dashboard until opened, and any Exco-discussion requests (including meeting invites) go through them too. Full hub access across every branch and department, same as National Exco, but doesn't author or sign off MOUs — and **writes the books** beside National Exco: posts to the ledger, decides and pays Claims, records Section 18A certificates and keeps the Finance Hub's settings, as Save7 OS has it (`0163`), while every act reserved to Exco stays Exco's. May author an Objective or Key Result onto any unit — national, any branch, any Department — though this is no longer exceptional, OKR authorship having been opened to everyone holding a seat or Project Lead status; the seat's organisation-wide reach across every *hub* remains its own. Neither extends to the Mission & vision, the Strategy or the Pillars, which are Exco's alone. Owns the lifecycle of volunteers who have no branch — acceptance, dormancy, deactivation, reactivation — since there is no Branch Management to hold it, with Exco as backstop, and helps a branchless volunteer with no Department author their Project Lead MOU. **Records each Project Lead MOU** the moment Exco sign it off — the platform record that gates Project creation, one Project per accepted MOU (see MOU), with Exco as backstop — on the MOUs page of the Operations Hub. **Edits the Operations Hub's own content** alongside the COO and the CEO, the two seats working closely enough that the General Liaison was given the COO's edit there. **Keeps the record of National Exco's meetings** — logs the Meeting, writes its Agenda Points, raises the Action Points off them, and works the organisation-wide Action Point tracker; Exco can do all of this too, as backstop. **Opens the Users hub** with National Exco: invites people in without the course, may answer any access request and answers every branchless one — and gives a **Funder** its sign-in or takes it away — but assigns no seat, grants no Super Admin and deletes no account.
_Avoid_: registrar, secretary general, secretary, exco liaison

**Users**:
The hub where **access to Save7 OS** is granted, changed and taken away, opened by **National Exco, the General Liaison and, for now, the Advisory board while Advising** (see Advisory board), and nobody else — not Super Admins, not an Observing member of the board, not Branch Management. It holds **User approval**: one page in four tabs — **Pending**, every **access request** not yet answered; **Approved**, everyone who is in, with their basic details and the **switches** on their account; **Rejected**; and **Deleted** — with a search across all of them. From here National Exco and the General Liaison **invite** someone in without the course (see Volunteer) and may answer any access request in the country, though a branch's own are its Branch Management's to answer, from Branch Centre, and a branchless person's the General Liaison's. **The switches** on an approved account are five, each flipped only by its owner: **seats**, **Super Admin** and **delete**, National Exco's and, for now, an Advising member's, a seat being given on the person and, every seat having one holder, giving one already held naming its holder and taking it off them (a handover, never a second holder); **active / deactivated**, the person's Branch Management's, or the General Liaison's where they have no branch, with Exco on anyone; and **branch**, moving someone when they change university, Exco's and the General Liaison's, since a move sits above either branch. Department membership and Project Lead status are not switches — the first is the Head's, the second comes from an MOU record. A **rejected** request may carry a reason, shown to the person, who may ask again and lands in Pending afresh. **Deleted** is a record — who, when, and by whom — of an account that is gone for good and cannot be restored, or deleting would be deactivation under another name. It is the end of an account, **not a ban**: a deleted person signing in again has no account and may ask for access as anyone may, the model having no *blocked* state — a refusal is a rejection with a reason. **An invited person arrives already approved**: sending the invitation is the approval, so they never wait in Pending, and their row shows who invited them. It skips the course and nothing else — the invitation goes to a Save7 email address the inviter has arranged, everyone signing in on the same kind of account. **An invitation is a standing approval of that address, not a credential**: the registration link points at the **Sign-in page** carrying the invited address, which the page fills in, so it may be forwarded harmlessly (all it does is fill in an address; signing in still takes the account behind it) and nothing about it expires or is used up — the first sign-in on the address claims the approval. Until then it shows on the **Approved** tab as *Invited — not signed in yet*, with who sent it, and National Exco or the General Liaison may **withdraw** it; that is not deleting an account, none yet existing, and it leaves no record, since inviting the address again undoes it. An invited person arrives **branchless**, owned by the General Liaison, until the branch switch moves them — unless they were already waiting *Pending*, when the branch they named stands. The invite form refuses an address that is already approved, and an address with a *Pending* access request is simply approved by the invitation, as asked. Beside User approval sits a **Funders** page, the same two seats' alone: every **Funder** with a sign-in, the internal note kept on each, and the acts of **giving a funder its sign-in**, deactivating it and reactivating it — a funder never being a row in User approval, holding no seat and making no request. Replaced the **Admin Dashboard**, whose contents were split by what each actually was — access came here, the master volunteer tracker went to the Operations Hub, the branch list to its Branches page and the department list to the Departments directory.
_Avoid_: Admin Dashboard (retired), admin, admin panel, control panel, back office, user approval (the page inside it, not the hub)

**Sign-in page**:
The one screen a person sees before they are signed in, and the first thing anyone reaches. It holds a **single sign-in**, and one line for anyone who has not begun — the course is at learn.save7.org. **No role buttons**: a person cannot choose to be a Branch Manager at the door, Exco assigning every seat and one **Shell** being built from whatever seats a person holds. No sign-up, no "I have an invitation" button (an invitation is a link to this page), and no external-volunteer door: everyone who takes work through the platform is a member, and anyone helping from outside is organised offline by a member. No funder door either: a **Funder** signs in outside Save7 OS, never here. **An account is only ever on a Save7 address**, but the door takes the address a person did the course on, Save7 or personal, because asking for access happens there (see Access request). What follows a sign-in is decided by **the state of the account behind the address**: an approved one reaches the **Dashboard**; an address with no account reaches **Ask for access**, which checks the course first and then offers the **access request**; a *Pending* request shows a waiting screen; a personal address whose request was approved shows the Save7 address the account now lives on and says to sign in with that; a *Rejected* one shows its reason and a way to ask again; a deactivated account reaches one plain screen naming who reactivates it; a deleted one counts as no account. **Every screen says only what is true, and nothing emails anyone** — so the waiting screen says to sign in again to check.
_Avoid_: landing page (names the **Dashboard**, the surface a person reaches *after* signing in), login page, welcome page, sign-up page (nothing is signed up for here — that is the course at learn.save7.org), role door

**Access request**:
A person's ask to be let into Save7 OS, which they may send **only once they are eligible** at learn.save7.org — being eligible lets nobody in by itself. Names **their branch, or none**, chosen by them, **and carries nothing else** — name and address come from their account, and there is no free-text box — the branch being what routes it: to that branch's Branch Management, answering from the Branch Managers hub or their Dashboard, or to the General Liaison where they chose none; National Exco and the General Liaison see every one in the **Users** hub and may answer any. Waits in *Pending* until **approved** or **rejected**, a reason optional on a rejection, and never lapses. While *Pending* the person sees the branch they chose, when they asked and who will answer, and is told plainly that nothing will email them; they may **withdraw** it, as with a Department or Project request, but not edit it — a different branch is a withdrawal and a fresh ask. A *Rejected* request shows its reason, and the person may **ask again**, naming a different branch if they like: a new *Pending*, the old rejection staying in the Rejected tab. Where they are not eligible, the person reaches no form at all. **It is asked from the address the course was done on**, because that is the one address learn.save7.org knows. That may be a Save7 address or a personal one: learn.save7.org takes any Google account, and nobody is given a Save7 address before the course. **Approving a request from a personal address names the Save7 address the account will live on**, arranged before the approval is given. Who creates that address is not yet settled. As with an invitation, the approval is a standing approval of that Save7 address, and the first sign-in on it claims the account. Until then it shows on the **Approved** tab as *Approved — not signed in yet*. The personal address is never an account. It stays only on the request, which shows both addresses. **What counts as eligible is learn.save7.org's to say**: a rule its own admin sets and versions, at present a finished Beginner Level rather than the whole course, and a Save7 address is never proof of it on its own. Save7 OS reads that answer **at the moment of asking**, keeps no copy of it and never checks it again, so a rule tightened while a request is *Pending* leaves the request standing: eligibility gates asking, not acceptance. Whoever answers sees the name, the address, the branch and when it was asked, **and nothing of the course**, since every request has already passed it and scores never leave learn.save7.org. Stands as a duty, aggregated per branch, on its Branch Management. The one way in besides it is an invitation, which skips the course (see Volunteer).
_Avoid_: submission, application, sign-up, registration (registering at learn.save7.org comes first and is not this); *finished the course* for what lets a person ask (the test is learn.save7.org's eligibility rule, which may ask for less than the whole course)

**Learner**:
A person on the course at learn.save7.org. **Learn's, not Save7 OS's**: nothing in Save7 OS shows a learner, counts them or lists them by branch — learn.save7.org records no branch, and a learner names one only in an access request. Save7 OS first sees a learner when they ask for access, and learns one thing about them then: whether they are eligible. A learner who never asks is never seen; one approved becomes a **Volunteer** and stays a learner at learn.save7.org. The two records meet only once, when the person asks, on the address the course was done on. After that they need share nothing, since the account may live on a Save7 address learn.save7.org has never seen.
_Avoid_: volunteer, applicant, recruit (for anyone who has not been approved)

**Super Admin**:
The only admin status there is — a flag on a person, not a seat of its own, and **not tied to holding one**: Exco may grant it to any member, a plain Volunteer included, since it confers nothing to change. Confers **read** access to every hub across every branch and department — with **two carve-outs**, neither of which a Super Admin reads: the **Meetings page** of the Operations Hub's Compliance section, and the **Users** hub. Maintaining the site is not a reason to sit in on Exco's deliberations, nor to decide who gets in, so both are reached by seat — National Exco and the General Liaison, the advisory board for Meetings and, while Advising, for Users — and never by admin status. It confers no edit rights, which still come from the underlying seat. The "Super" is measured against National Exco rather than a lesser admin: a Super Admin sees everything and changes nothing structural. One who is not Exco cannot grant or revoke Super Admin, assign any seat, add a branch or department, create or alter an Exco seat, or override an Exco decision. Exco hold it by default and irrevocably, and the Tech Department Head holds it by seat, since maintaining the site requires seeing everything; otherwise granted at Exco's discretion — to seats already carrying organisation-wide responsibility such as the General Liaison, or to a member with no seat whose work needs the whole view, such as one who maintains the site. Account upkeep is not theirs: it is done in the Users hub, which a Super Admin does not open. For the same reason a Super Admin reads a Resolution's outcome but not its reason.
_Avoid_: admin, superuser, root, owner

**Branch Manager**:
Runs one branch. Edit access to their own branch across hubs; read-only access to other branches — **money excepted**: of the Finance Hub they reach **Your money**, where their branch's Claims show by name, and they write nothing in the books, the finance line running from the branch's Finance Manager to the CFO. They do act on a **Spend request** from their branch whose form names no lead on the platform, approving or declining it as its lead before it goes to a decision. Authors MOUs (responsibilities/expectations/deliverables) for that branch's Project Leads — routed through the General Liaison for Exco review and sign-off. Appointed by Exco. With the Operations Manager and Finance Manager, accepts new volunteers onto the platform — a power that comes with the seat itself, not from any admin status. Authoring a branch volunteer's Project Lead MOU is the Branch Manager's alone, and it is their whole part in making a Project Lead: the status is conferred by the record of Exco's sign-off, not by any branch seat (see MOU). Branch Management own their branch's volunteers for the whole of that lifecycle: reviewing dormancy, deactivating, and reactivating. Deleting an account outright is Exco's, and for now an Advising member's. Any one of the three, acting alone, may author an Objective or a Key Result — no quorum, and not confined to their own branch, since OKR authorship is the one thing in the model that is not unit-scoped.

**Operations Manager**:
Runs one branch's operations team. Branch-scoped, same edit-own/read-others pattern as Branch Manager. Appointed by Exco; shares Branch Management's powers over their branch's volunteers — see Branch Manager. **Reports directly to the COO** — the operations line runs to the COO the way the finance line runs from each Finance Manager to the CFO — while working alongside their branch's Branch Manager. Oversees all volunteers in the branch, all Project Leads, and new-volunteer onboarding. **Overseeing a Project Lead is looking after them as a person** — they are one of the branch's volunteers — and reading their Projects as any seat-holder does; it confers nothing over the Project, which is its Lead's. Drafts, offline, the branch's **Agreements** with outside organisations and its **SOPs**, and briefs the branch's volunteers from them; never drafts a Project Lead MOU, which is the Branch Manager's alone. Along with the Branch Manager and COO, funnels volunteers with a specialised interest toward the matching Department.

**Finance Manager**:
Runs one branch's finance function. Reports directly to the CFO — there is no national Finance Department standing between them. Appointed by Exco; shares Branch Management's powers over their branch's volunteers — see Branch Manager. **The one branch seat that decides money**, and only over their own branch: once seated, they **decide their branch's Claims** — approve, reject or query a fuel claim or a Branch spend line, approving a spend line posting it to the ledger — **review the budget plan** of every Project homed at their branch, accepting it or returning it with a reason, and **decide their branch's Spend requests below a threshold** set when the first Finance Manager is appointed. Until a branch has one, all of that is the CFO's. They do not mark a Claim paid, since the money leaves the organisation's account and no branch holds one, never decide their own Claim, and post nothing to the ledger by hand. Of the Finance Hub they reach **Your money**, where their branch's Claims show by name, and they **decide from the Finance Hub and their Dashboard**, never from a hub of their own: **there is no Branch Finance Managers hub** (see Branch Centre), and they reach the Branch Managers and Branch Operations Managers hubs as one of Branch Management. **Money duties sit with them alone**, not with Branch Management jointly.
_Avoid_: branch function head, finance head, department head, branch lead

**Branch Management**:
The three branch seats **collectively** — Branch Manager, Operations Manager, Finance Manager — one set per branch. Not a seat of its own and not a fourth appointment: the name for the three acting as the branch's leadership, which is how the model refers to them whenever the point is the branch owning its people rather than one manager's particular remit. They accept volunteers — answering their branch's **access requests** from the Volunteers page of the Branch Managers hub or from their Dashboard, while National Exco and the General Liaison see every request in the Users hub — and own their branch's volunteers through dormancy, deactivation and reactivation. They do **not** grant Project Lead status — the Branch Manager alone authors a branch volunteer's MOU, and the status comes from the record of its sign-off (see MOU). Over a Project homed at their branch they hold no write at all: the branch owns the person, the Lead owns the work. **Money is not held jointly**: the branch's Claims and budget plans are its Finance Manager's alone, or the CFO's while the branch has none. Any one of them puts their branch's **Events** on, from the Branch Managers hub, with no approval. Addressable as a unit — an Action Point may be assigned to a branch's Branch Management, which lands on all three.
_Avoid_: branch trio (the previous term, renamed), trio, branch leadership, branch exec

**Department**:
A national/organisation-level portfolio for specialised work: Media, Tech, Research, Education. Open-ended list managed by National Exco alone — Departments are added and edited on the Departments directory, and **never removed**: unlike a branch, a Department is not deleted, and it is not retired the way a Pillar is. A Department is a standing place the organisation's specialised work is handed out from, so dropping one is a restructure for Exco to decide off the platform, not an act on the directory; a mistaken add is fixed by renaming it. **Creating one takes only a name.** It arrives with *No Head yet*, "what it is for" left blank for its Head to write, and anyone may be switched on as its Head afterwards. Marking a Department **under construction**, and lifting the mark, is **National Exco's alone**. A **second supervisory axis**, not a label: joining a Department places a volunteer's work in that portfolio under its Department Head, while their branch's Branch Management continues to own them as a person. Distinct axis from the Branch system — operates directly under Exco, not under a Branch Manager. A volunteer may belong to several Departments at once, one interest not excluding another; each Head's authority reaches only their own portfolio's work, so two Heads never contend over the same task. Finance is not a Department — see Finance Manager.

**Joined by asking, and admitted by its Head.** A volunteer asks to join from the Department's front page, sees the request pending there and may withdraw it; the Department Head approves or declines, a reason optional, and someone declined may ask again. A request **never lapses** — it stands on the Head's Dashboard until answered, since a request that quietly expired would let a Head refuse someone without ever saying no. The Head may also **invite** a volunteer, who must **accept**: membership places a person's work under somebody, and that cannot be agreed on their behalf. Branch Management has no say — the branch owns the person, the Head owns the work — and the funnelling the Branch Manager, Operations Manager and COO do toward a matching Department is advice that points a volunteer at the Departments Hub, never an act on the platform.

**Leaving, removal and the inactive flag.** A member may leave at any time. The Head may **remove** a member — for someone who should not be there — who may then ask again. **Inactive** is the state between, for someone who has gone quiet and is expected back: still a member, still able to enter the Department's hub and read it, unable to write content, and cleared by the Head alone. Membership survives a change of seat and a deactivated account alike. **A Department with no Head** shows as having none and is **closed to requests and invitations**, there being nobody to answer them — the rule a Project with no Lead follows. Its members stay and may leave, requests already waiting pass to the next Head, an inactive flag stays until one arrives, and nobody carries a duty for it. National Exco do not admit members in a Head's place: admitting someone is taking on their work.
_Avoid_: hub (a Department is an org unit; Hub is a nav section — a Department typically owns a Hub, but they aren't the same concept); retired, archived, deleted (a Department is never removed)

**Department Head**:
Owns, grows, and organises one Department at national scale. MOU authored directly by Exco (no Branch Manager in the loop). Keeps their Department's **Department tracker**, which follows their MOU's KPIs and targets. Accounts for their Department upward in **Department reports**, written from its hub and owing no date, which reach Exco through the General Liaison (see Update), and routes any Exco-discussion requests through the General Liaison. Owns their Department's **hub**, which sits under Departments rather than at the top level — the Media Department Head owns the Media Hub, absorbing the former CMO/Marketing remit, which is no longer a separate Exco seat or department. Holds real authority over their Department's work: sees what its members are doing, assigns it, and has a say in it; onboards them with the tools the portfolio needs, advises them, and mobilises them. That authority stops at the work — **the Department Head owns the work, Branch Management owns the person**, so acceptance onto the platform, dormancy review, deactivation and reactivation are never theirs. They may mark a member inactive *within the Department*, which is a per-Department status and touches nothing about the account — its one consequence is to suspend that member's write over content in the Department's hub (see Volunteer). **The two axes do not reach into each other**: marking someone inactive within a Department never feeds their Branch Management's dormancy review, and dormancy at their branch never marks them inactive in a Department — a volunteer may be quiet in one and busy in the other, and letting either switch the other off would have one supervisor judging the other's half of the person. The one exception is **deactivation**, which belongs to the account rather than to either axis: a deactivated account can do nothing, but its Department memberships survive it and return on reactivation, the Head seeing that member as deactivated and unable to undo it. **Holding the seat makes them a member** of their Department, doing everything its members do — entering its hub, writing content, opting in to its work — so they cannot be marked inactive, nobody in the Department sitting above them (a Head who goes quiet is Exco's to reassign), and they do not leave it, stepping down from the seat instead; someone who stops being Head stays an ordinary member unless they leave. What the Head holds beyond membership is its **management**, which lives on their **Dashboard** rather than in the Department's hub: volunteers waiting to join, the invitations they have sent, the member list and the inactive flags. They admit, invite, remove and mark inactive from there (see Department). Puts their Department's **Events** on, from its front page. Authors Objectives and Key Results for any unit rather than only their own Department, OKR authorship being the one thing in the model that is not unit-scoped; the Mission & vision, the Strategy and the Pillars remain Exco's alone. **One Head per Department, but a person may head several** — the authority being scoped to each Department separately, heading two needs nothing but two of everything. The seat sits alongside any other: an Exco member, the General Liaison or a Branch Manager may head a Department too. Where a branchless volunteer in their Department seeks Project Lead status, the Head works the MOU up with them and passes it to the General Liaison. The Tech Department Head additionally carries Super Admin by seat.
_Avoid_: finance manager, branch lead

**Project Lead**:
Not a distinct account type — a status/upgrade on a Volunteer, granted via an MOU authored at the lowest supervising level above them and signed off by Exco, and **conferred on the platform by the record of that sign-off** (see MOU) — held from the moment the record exists, before any Project does. Owns the resulting Project, which they create from the Projects Hub; until they do, the unused record sits on their Dashboard as a standing duty. A **standing** status, not a time-boxed one: it lasts as long as the Project does. Held **once per accepted MOU** — a person holding three signed-off MOUs may create three Projects and no more, which is what the MOU record on the platform exists to enforce (see MOU). Sees the volunteers working under them and their Tasks; admits, invites and removes them, assigns them Tasks and owns the columns of the Project's Board (see Project Team); edits the Project's eligibility, its status and the Pillars, Objectives and Key Results it names; writes its overview, its project tracker and its attached documents, and submits its budget plan for review; and keeps its log of Updates; and puts the Project's **Events** on, from its page. Joining a Project Team confers nothing — only an accepted MOU makes a Project Lead. **Ends three ways:** a successor signs an MOU for the Project — which may be the same MOU the outgoing Lead hands over, run through the normal route; **National Exco remove the Lead outright**, the sign-off having been theirs; or the Project is **completed or cancelled**. Whoever authored the MOU does not revoke the status. Carries **OKR authorship**: writes Objectives and Key Results for any unit, on the same terms as Branch Management and Department Heads — the one place the status reaches outside its own Project — held **while the status is held and not a moment after**, so leading a Project once does not confer organisation-wide OKR write for life. A Project that is Inactive or On ice changes none of this: those statuses report momentum and carry no permission, or nobody would ever set them. Completing or cancelling one does end the status for it, and with it the OKR authorship it carried unless its holder leads something else — which is why National Exco may set those statuses too, so that a Project is not kept open just to keep a permission.

**Project**:
An initiative owned by a Project Lead, born when a volunteer's idea is written up as an MOU and signed off by Exco — so there is no Project waiting on a sign-off, and nothing before *Active*. Carries one **status**, set by its Lead or by National Exco: **Active**, where every Project starts; **Inactive**, dormant — it has lost momentum and needs a push, the same signal a Branch carries; **On ice**, parked on purpose to be picked up later, a decision rather than a stall; and **Completed** or **Cancelled**, which are final. Only the final two carry any consequence.

**Not time-boxed, but able to end.** A Project runs until its Lead or Exco say it has finished, and many never will; nothing about a date closes one. A completed or cancelled Project is closed to requests and invitations, and its Board, team and Updates become read-only — save that its Lead may still attach its **reflection and close** document. The platform stamps the date it ended. Ending one **closes whatever was still waiting** — requests to join and invitations, each person seeing that the Project ended rather than their ask quietly vanishing, and every open Task, which leaves its owners' Dashboards and stays on the Board as left undone. Only National Exco reopen one, to correct a mistake. Its MOU stays used, so running the same thing again takes a new MOU and a new Project. Its **home is its Lead's branch**, read off the Lead rather than chosen, and null where the Lead is branchless; a Project left with no Lead keeps the home its last Lead gave it until a successor arrives, its people and its history still being there. It attaches to no Department: a Department Head already owns their members' work wherever that work happens, and a tag would add a second claimant without adding a power.

**Created by its Lead**, through a form in the Projects Hub that appears only to someone holding an unused MOU record, once the MOU behind it is signed off and recorded — one Project per accepted MOU. The form sets a name, what the Project is for, the MOU it comes from — chosen by its code from the Lead's own unused ones — and its **eligibility**: the set of branches whose volunteers may join, plus whether branchless volunteers may. One branch in the set is a branch-scoped Project; every branch is an open one. KPIs and targets are not set here — they live on the MOU, and tracking them is the work of a project tracker. A Project carries a **start date** and an optional **target end date**, both set by its Lead and both informational: nothing is computed from them, so no Project is ever shown as late or as having slipped.

**Names the ladder it serves** — any number of Pillars, Objectives and Key Results, all optional, and a Project that names none is unremarkable. Set on the creation form and changed at any time by its Lead, with National Exco as backstop. The choice **cascades**: naming a Key Result carries in its Objective and that Objective's Pillar, naming an Objective carries in its Pillar. Because these are sets rather than single slots, naming a Pillar alongside an Objective standing on a different one is not a contradiction — it is a Project serving two things. The link is **inert**: it moves no progress bar, since an Objective's figure stays the derived mean of its Key Results, and it confers nothing on the Key Results named — no ownership, no acceptance, no duty. Deleting a named Objective or Key Result, or retiring a named Pillar, simply drops it from the set; a Project pointing at something never blocks its deletion, and the Project shows as unlinked, which everyone can see. **The same commitment may legitimately appear twice** — as a Key Result on the ladder and as a KPI in the project tracker — and the link is what makes the duplication visible rather than what prevents it: the two answer to different documents, the ladder and the MOU.

Carries a log of **Updates**, kept by its Lead.

Every Project is visible to every member of the organisation, whether or not they may join it: eligibility gates joining, never seeing. Every member reads its **page** — its **overview** (what it is for, its goals and its audience), its Lead, its status, its eligibility stated as it applies to the reader, the ladder it names, its Updates, its team, its **Events**, and its **MOU code**, which opens the signed MOU only for those allowed to read it (see MOU). Its **Board** of Tasks is read only by its team and its Lead, and by the seats that read everything: the invitation is open, the working surface belongs to the people doing the work. Its **project tracker** and **attached documents** are read by its Lead, its team and anyone holding a seat — except the **budget plan** and the Project's **spend**, which only the Lead, the seats that read everything and the plan's reviewer — its home branch's Finance Manager, or the CFO, who reviews it from their Dashboard — see. The overview, the tracker and the documents are written by the Lead alone, with National Exco as backstop.

**A Project with no Lead** — its Lead removed by Exco and no successor signed off — shows as having none and is **closed to requests and invitations**, there being nobody to answer them. Its team stays, and may leave; requests already waiting pass to the successor; its status falls to Exco; and nobody carries a duty for it, since nobody owes a successor — a Project nobody will lead is Exco's to cancel.

**Deleting** a Project is National Exco's alone and corrects a mistake — a Project created against the wrong MOU, or created twice — returning its MOU record to unused. It is never a way of ending one; that is what Completed and Cancelled are for.
_Avoid_: programme, campaign, initiative (use for the idea before it is signed off, not the Project), time-boxed (a Project ends when someone says so, not at a set term), pending, planned (there is no Project before its MOU is signed off)

**Project Team**:
The volunteers helping on a Project, plus the Project Lead who owns it. Team members remain ordinary volunteers under their own Branch Management; sitting on a team grants no status and no authority over the Project. Its **one write** is on the Project's **Board**: a member writes their own Tasks, moves cards and ticks their own Tasks done, while the Lead also assigns Tasks to any member, edits or deletes any, and owns the columns. An assigned Task needs no acceptance — joining was the handshake, and a member who will not do it says so or leaves. The team is visible to every member of the organisation, each member with an optional **role** on it — *Logistics*, *Social media* — set by the Lead, which labels what they do there and grants nothing.

**The Project Lead is the only gate.** An eligible volunteer asks to join from the Project's page, sees the request pending there and may withdraw it; the Lead approves or declines from the same page — their own Branch Management has no say, since the branch owns the person and the Lead owns the work. A decline may carry a reason and need not, a Lead choosing their team owing nobody an account of it; someone declined may ask again. A request **never lapses**: it stands as a duty on the Lead's Dashboard until answered, because a request that quietly expires lets a Lead refuse someone without ever saying no. The Lead may also **invite** a volunteer, who must **accept** — being added to a team silently creates an expectation of work nobody agreed to — and the invitation stands on the invitee's Dashboard until they accept or decline it, the Lead being free to withdraw it. **An invitation never overrides eligibility**: the set is what everyone reads as who may join, so a Lead who wants one volunteer from another branch adds that branch and answers its requests. Waiting requests and invitations are seen only by the Lead and the person concerned. There is **no cap** on a team's size or on how many join from one branch; the Lead's approval already does that job.

A member may **leave** at any time and the Lead may **remove** one — deliberately unlike a Key Result's owners, who have no exit, because membership grants nothing there is any reason to make sticky. Narrowing a Project's eligibility **does not eject** anyone already on the team: the set governs new joins, and removing someone is a named act, not a side effect of editing a list.
_Avoid_: project members, crew, squad

**MOU**:
An offline, signed agreement between a role and its supervising structure (Branch Manager ↔ Project Lead, Exco ↔ Department Head) setting responsibilities, expectations, deliverables, and KPIs/targets.

A Project Lead MOU is authored at the **lowest supervising level above the applicant** and routed upward through the General Liaison to Exco for sign-off; where the applicant already sits at or above a level, that level is skipped. So: a branch-onboarded volunteer's MOU is authored with their **home** Branch Manager whatever the project's reach, and Department membership does not divert it; a branchless volunteer in a Department works it up with that Department Head; a branchless volunteer in no Department works it up with the General Liaison; the General Liaison authors their own and goes straight to Exco; an Exco member authors their own and presents it to the rest of Exco. Not a UI workflow: the drafting, the review and the sign-off stay offline. A Project Lead MOU's KPIs and targets are followed in its Project's **project tracker**, never in the OKRs Hub. A Department Head's MOU is kept in the **Operations Hub**, which holds every signed MOU, and its KPIs and targets are followed in that Department's **Department tracker** — likewise never in the OKRs Hub. Not to be confused with an **Agreement**, which Save7 makes with an outside organisation even where the paper is titled a memorandum of understanding.

The platform nonetheless holds a **record** of each signed-off Project Lead MOU — who holds it, and when Exco signed it off — entered by the **General Liaison**, with Exco as backstop, at the moment of sign-off, on the **MOUs** page of the Operations Hub's Compliance section, which keeps every signed MOU — Department Head MOUs beside Project Lead ones. **The record is what confers Project Lead status** — no branch seat grants it. It is entered either **unattached**, making a new Project possible, or **against an existing Project**, making its holder that Project's successor Lead, which is how a handed-over MOU is recorded. The record does not run the drafting, the review or the sign-off; it exists because a Project is created from an accepted MOU and one MOU makes exactly one Project. KPIs and targets stay on the document, not in the record.

Each record carries a **code** — `#MOU-0042` — which the platform issues at entry: unique, never reused and never typed, so that no two can collide. It is how a Project names the MOU it comes from, and how the paper and the record point at each other. A Lead's creation form offers their own unused codes and no one else's. The **signed copy is uploaded to the record** by the General Liaison as part of entering it, and is required, since a record with no document asserts a sign-off nobody can check. It is read by its holder, whoever authored it with them, the General Liaison, National Exco and the seats that read everything; every other member sees the code on the Project's page and cannot open it. A successor's record carries a code of its own — the Project shows its current Lead's, the earlier ones staying listed for those readers. The General Liaison or Exco may **withdraw** a record no Project has been created from, which is how an idea dropped before its Project exists comes off its holder's Dashboard.

A **Department Head MOU** has a record of the same shape — a code, its holder, the Department it covers, the date Exco signed it off and the required signed copy, entered by the General Liaison with Exco as backstop — with one difference: **it confers nothing**. The Head's seat is assigned by National Exco in the **Users** hub; the record is the file, not the key. Its signed copy is read by the Head, National Exco, the General Liaison and the seats that read everything. A successor Head signs a new MOU and gets a record of their own, the earlier ones staying listed, and the **Department tracker** names the code it follows. **The MOUs page lists every record to everyone who reads the Operations Hub** — code, holder, kind, sign-off date and the Project it became — while only a record's own readers open its signed copy.

**Agreement**:
A signed agreement between **Save7 and an outside organisation** — a hospital, a university, another NGO — whatever the paper calls itself: the Tygerberg Life Pod transplant-shadowing memorandum of understanding is an Agreement, not an MOU. Drafted and negotiated **offline**, by a branch's Operations Manager for that branch's arrangements or nationally; the **signed copy** is kept on the **Agreements** page of the Operations Hub's Compliance section, which groups them by partner. An Operations Manager **submits the draft** from their own hub for the COO's approval **before it is signed** — approval after a signature could undo nothing — and the signed copy is then filed against the approved draft without a second approval; a draft may be returned with a reason, and never lapses. Names its **partner organisation**, the **branch** it serves or none where it is national, and may carry a **renewal date**: once that passes it shows **overdue, in red, on the COO's Dashboard**, and clears when the renewed copy is filed or the Agreement is marked ended. The Operations Manager does the renewing, offline, but cannot file it, so the red sits with the COO, who chases them. A renewal date of its own, rather than a warning computed ahead of an end date, keeps the one rule the model already has: a dated duty goes red when its date passes. **Read in full, signed copy included, by everyone who reads the Operations Hub** — there is no confidential Agreement, since a Project Lead who cannot read the terms they work under cannot keep them. An approved draft whose partner has not yet signed shows on the Agreements page as **Not yet signed**, on nobody's Dashboard, because the signature is the partner's to give and no duty on our side could discharge it; a hub editor files the signed copy when it comes, or marks it **abandoned**. A hub editor marks an Agreement **ended**, and an ended or abandoned one stays listed, never deleted except to correct a mistake. It confers nothing on the platform and gates nothing, which is what separates it from an MOU — an MOU is internal, sits between a role and the level above it, and its record makes a Project Lead.
_Avoid_: MOU (the paper may carry that title; in this glossary an MOU is internal), contract, partnership, deal

**SOP**:
A **standard operating procedure** — Save7's written way of doing something that recurs, such as running a campus stall or conducting a hospital visit. Drafted by Operations Managers and the COO and kept in the **Operations Hub**, read by everyone holding a seat or Project Lead status. An Operations Manager **submits** one from their own hub in Branch Centre; it waits as a duty on the **COO's** Dashboard — one card however many are waiting — until **approved**, which publishes it, or **returned with a reason**, and it never lapses. The CEO and the General Liaison may approve one too, but the duty is the COO's. A **returned** one stands on the Operations Manager who submitted it until they resubmit or withdraw it, so that a return is not lost on someone who never looks — the same holding for a returned Agreement draft. **Not visible to plain Volunteers**: an SOP is for the people who run the work, and an Operations Manager briefs their branch's volunteers from it rather than handing it over.
_Avoid_: policy, guideline, playbook, manual, procedure document

**Resolution**:
A formal decision of National Exco that **moves a seat at the top of the organisation**, held on the platform in the Resolutions page of the Operations Hub's **Compliance section**. The list is **closed**: appointing an Exco member, removing one, an Exco member stepping down, placing a person on the Advisory board or taking them off it, a CEO stepping down (which may name a successor), removing a CEO who will not step down, and appointing a CEO where none was named. Everything else Exco does — adding a branch, ending a Project, adopting a policy — stays a direct act of any of the four seats, and needs no Resolution. One Resolution concerns **one person**, save the successor a CEO names, and may move them more than one way at once — stepping down and joining the Advisory board are one Resolution with one approval. **Approval is the act**: the seat moves the moment it is approved, and the Users hub refuses these movements by any other route. **Raised** by any Exco seat, by the General Liaison, or by the person it concerns; anyone else asks the General Liaison to put it to Exco, and the Advisory board raises none. **Approved by the CEO alone** — whatever deliberation precedes it happens in the room, and the record states the outcome — with one exception: a Resolution **removing or appointing a CEO** is approved by a **majority of the filled remaining Exco seats, never fewer than two**; with fewer than two in post, the platform cannot carry it. The person a Resolution concerns has no part in approving it unless it is their own stepping down. While the CEO seat is vacant, every other Resolution waits for the new CEO. A Resolution is a structured record, not an uploaded document — its kind, the person and seat it concerns, who raised it and when, a written **reason**, and the approval or rejection with its date; there is no signed copy, the approval on the platform being the signature. It moves **Draft → Awaiting approval → Approved, Rejected (with a reason) or Withdrawn**; awaiting approval it is a duty on the CEO's Dashboard, and it never lapses. An approved Resolution is **never edited**: only a later Resolution reverses it. **Its outcome** — who, which seat, what moved, when — is read by everyone who reads the Operations Hub; **its reason** by National Exco, the General Liaison, the Advisory board and the person it concerns, and not by Super Admins; a draft or a rejected Resolution by National Exco, the General Liaison and the person it concerns. The **Resolutions page** is their register, newest first and filterable by person and kind, and where those allowed to raise one start it; each reader sees there exactly what the readership above gives them, and approval is given from the CEO's Dashboard. It may cite the **Agenda Point** it was taken under, which then links to it; it is never an Action Point and creates none. Unlike an MOU, which is offline and describes a working relationship, a Resolution is held on the platform and changes who holds what.
_Avoid_: motion, vote, minutes, decision (too general — a Resolution is the recorded instrument, not any decision taken), board resolution (the same instrument under another name — there is no separate board passing its own)

**Volunteer**:
The base layer every account holds. There are **two doors** into Save7 OS (below), and everyone who comes through either is a Volunteer, every seat and status layering on top of it — Branch Management is a Volunteer holding that seat, and Project Lead and Super Admin layer the same way. Recruited at one branch, or at none. A volunteer normally enters through a branch and may transfer to another if they change university — a move National Exco or the General Liaison make; a volunteer recruited outside any university has no branch at all — a small population in a student-driven organisation, owned by the General Liaison instead of Branch Management, and following the same paths into Departments and Projects as anyone else. Has a **Dashboard** — the same surface every member of the organisation has, not a consolation for holding no seat — plus the **OKRs Hub** and **National Centre** with everything beneath it: the **Projects Hub**, the **Departments Hub** and the front page of all four Department hubs, what lies behind one opening to them when they join it. So the ladder from day-to-day work up to the mission is visible to the people doing that work, every Project is visible to everyone who might join one, and every Department is too. **Operations and Branch Centre do not appear in their nav** — save that holding **Project Lead status** opens the Operations Hub to them, to read — and of the **Finance Hub** they reach only **Your money**: their own Claims, their own Claude seat and their own documents. **A plain Volunteer holds exactly five writes anywhere in the model.** The first is **owning a Key Result** — its progress, their own acceptance of it, its health, its done tick, its Updates and its supporting units, on that Key Result alone. The second is **membership of a Department**, which carries write over **content** in that Department's own hub and nothing else — never its membership, never its permissions, never another Department's hub — because the Department axis exists to place a volunteer's work under a Head, and a media volunteer with no way to publish a template cannot do the work they joined to do. That write is what the per-Department **inactive** status suspends. The third is **membership of a Project Team**, which carries write over that Project's **Board** — writing their own Tasks, moving cards, ticking their own Tasks done — and nothing else about the Project, for the same reason: a team member who cannot put a card on the board cannot do the work they joined for. The fourth is **lodging their own Claims** — a fuel claim, or a Branch spend line for their own branch — and withdrawing one while it is still pending; once it has been decided, changing it is a finance correction rather than theirs, since the person driving to a hospital event is exactly who is owed the money. The fifth is **organising an Event** they have been named organiser of and have accepted — editing it, cancelling it and removing people signed up to it — on that Event alone, because the student running the stall is who knows when it is full. Everywhere else, write comes from a seat. Can simultaneously belong to a branch, be a member of one or more Departments, sit on one or more Project Teams, and hold Project Lead status for one or more initiatives — these are not mutually exclusive. Accounts are not created on the platform: a prospective volunteer signs up at learn.save7.org on any Google account and, once **eligible** there, may **ask for access** from that same address, a Save7 email address being arranged for them only when they are approved — being eligible lets nobody in by itself; the request waits, *Pending*, until it is *accepted* onto the platform by hand, by their branch's Branch Management. The **second door** is an **invitation**, for the people Save7 wants in **without the course**: National Exco or the General Liaison send someone a registration link, and the course is the one thing it skips. **A door is a way in, never a role**: both arrive at the same **Sign-in page**, and what differs is the state of the account behind the address. Graduating does not end volunteering — accounts persist, and deletion is a genuine last resort rather than routine cleanup, reserved to Exco and, for now, the Advisory board while Advising: an account costs eligibility at learn.save7.org and a Save7 email address to obtain, or an invitation from the top of the organisation, so destroying one is not a branch-level act. **Contribution is recorded as work owned and done, never as time spent** — nothing on the platform measures duration, and no hours are logged, verified or counted anywhere in the model, and nobody's attendance at an Event is recorded. What a volunteer has contributed is legible from the Key Results they own, the Projects they sit on and the Action Points they close; proof of that contribution to somebody outside Save7 is written by a person, offline. **There is one kind of volunteer.** Nobody takes work through the platform without being a member, so there is no lighter outside tier: a student who has not started the course, a parent at a drive or a company's staff on a CSR day helps offline, organised by a member.
_Avoid_: using *volunteer* for Save7 OS's separate `volunteers` tier (personal Google account, no `people` row, volunteers.save7.org). That tier dissolves into this one when the two account kinds merge.

**Funder**:
An organisation outside Save7 — a corporate CSR partner or a key donor — given a **read-only sign-in** to see a scoped view of Save7's work: the Projects published to funders, with their milestones and figures. **Not a member**: holds no seat, has no Dashboard, makes no access request and never appears in User approval; its sign-in is not a Save7 address, and one funder never sees another. *CSR* is why a company funds, not a thing in the model. **National Exco and the General Liaison** give a funder its sign-in and take it away, on the Funders page of the **Users** hub — the **CGO** covering funders as a relationship, not as a power of its own. They alone also choose what funders see: a Project is **published to funders** by them, never by its Lead, showing a Project outside Save7 being an organisational decision. Nothing else is published — no Meeting or Agenda Point of National Exco's reaches a funder. What a funder reads is reached from outside Save7 OS and is not designed here.
_Avoid_: stakeholder, CSR stakeholder, sponsor, partner; *donor* for a funder (a donor gives money and never holds a sign-in)

**Hub**:
A named, role-gated section of the site's navigation. **Six sit at the top level** — OKRs, Finance, Operations, **National Centre**, **Branch Centre** and **Users** — and nine sit beneath the two Centres (see their entries). **Where a hub sits on screen is deliberately not part of the model** — top nav, sidebar group or anything else is a rendering decision, left to the work that builds the Shell. What the model fixes is which hubs exist, how they nest, who may open each, and what state each is in.

**Nesting runs three levels along two spines and nowhere else** — National Centre → Departments → a Department's hub, and Branch Centre → a seat's hub. Everywhere else the nav is one level deep: a hub's internal pages are pages within it, not hubs and not sub-hubs, and a **section** — a named group of pages inside one hub, such as the Operations Hub's **Compliance** section and the Meetings page within it — is not a hub either. Compliance was a top-level hub until it was folded into Operations. **A Department's hub is reached under Departments and never stands at the top level**, whatever that Department's size or reach — Media carried a top-level hub in the pre-Departments nav and loses it for the same reason branch Media Head went, the Departments layer having arrived after that nav was drawn.

**A hub that holds only other hubs has no page of its own**, because that is a menu rather than a place — true of National Centre and Branch Centre, and of neither anywhere else. **Departments is the counter-case and holds a page deliberately:** a directory of the four Departments, what each is for and what it is working on, and how to join one. Nothing else in the model tells a volunteer the Departments exist. See Departments Hub.

A hub is hidden entirely from a role's nav if that role can't access it, rather than shown disabled. **A page inside a hub follows the same rule**: one the reader cannot open is left out of the hub's pages rather than shown locked — a Department's members do not see its Reports page at all. **A link to such a page** — sent by someone who reads it — opens on a plain notice that it is not a page the reader opens, rather than pretending there is no such page: the nav stays uncluttered, but nobody is misled about whether something exists, and the notice shows nothing of what the page holds. A Dashboard is not a hub either, and nor is an **Event's page**, which sits outside every hub (see Event). **Open to every member of the organisation, Volunteers included:** the **OKRs Hub**, since a ladder nobody can see fails at its only job; the **Projects Hub** — which the user refers to informally as *All Projects* — since every member is a potential member of every Project open to them and a directory that hides what you cannot join cannot tell you what Save7 is running; **National Centre, Departments and all four Department hubs** — every Department is visible to everybody through its **front page**, and what lies behind that page is for those who belong to it (see Departments Hub); and the **Finance Hub**, of which a plain Volunteer reaches only **Your money**, since everyone may be owed a reimbursement and nobody without a seat needs the books. **A page inside a hub may be gated more tightly than the hub itself** — the Meetings page of the Operations Hub's Compliance section, which a Super Admin does not read, and everything behind a Department's front page, which only its members, its Head and the seats that read everything reach: for Media, its content scheduler, tracker, tasks and calendar. What a member may *edit* there still comes from their seat, save for the five writes a plain Volunteer holds (see Volunteer): owning a Key Result, content in a Department they belong to, the Board of a Project whose team they sit on, lodging their own Claims, and an Event they organise.

A hub may be **under construction** — it appears in the nav for whoever may reach it and its page says it is not built yet. See that entry; it is not a property of hubs alone. **Tech and Education carry it**, behind their front pages; Research does not, its **Research board** being what lies behind its front page.
_Avoid_: top nav, sidebar section, tab (each names a placement the model does not fix); sub-hub (a nested hub is a Hub like any other)

**National Centre**:
A Hub holding the **Projects Hub** and the **Departments Hub**, and nothing else. It has no page of its own — clicking it opens what is under it. A **grouping in the navigation, not an organisational unit**: nobody heads it, it confers no permission, and it makes no claim that what it holds is national — the Projects Hub is the loosest fit of all, a Project's home being its Lead's branch. Open to every member, Volunteers included, because both its children are. Named against the alternative of leaving Projects and Departments at the top level, to shorten the top row; the cost is that both sit one click further away for the members who use them most.
_Avoid_: Nerve Centre (the effort and its repo, never a surface in the product), National Exco (a body of seats, not a place in the nav)

**Projects Hub**:
The hub holding every Project, under National Centre and open to every member: a **directory**, and each Project's **page** with its Board and its project tracker. The directory is one flat list, searched by name and filtered by **home** branch, by **status** — Active and Inactive Projects showing unless the reader asks for those On ice, Completed or Cancelled — and to the Projects **open to the reader**; not grouped, and not filtered by Pillar, since a Pillar in the OKRs Hub already lists the Projects serving it. A Project's page is read by everyone, its Board by its team, its tracker by its team and anyone holding a seat (see Project). It holds **no money** for anyone but a Project's Lead and the seats that read everything; spend against a Project is the Finance Hub's to show. It holds no MOU records: those are entered on the Operations Hub's MOUs page, which keeps every signed MOU, and a Project's page shows only its MOU code.
_Avoid_: All Projects (the user's informal name for it), project portfolio, projects page

**Project tracker**:
A Project's built-in record of how it is doing against its MOU — one per Project, a page of it in the Projects Hub, reached by its Lead from their Dashboard too. Holds **KPIs**, each a current figure against a target; **milestones**, each named and dated with a done tick; **deliverables**, planned against actual; and a **budget plan**, begun from a template, filled in line by line and **submitted for review**, waiting on the reviewer's Dashboard and opened from the Projects Hub, where it is accepted or returned with a reason (see Budget) — revisable and resubmittable, every submission kept. The Lead adds their own KPIs and milestones, and writes all of it, with National Exco as backstop. **Nothing in it is computed or chased**: an overdue milestone shows its date and nothing goes red, nothing reaches a Dashboard, and no check-in is kept. What the tracker does not hold is an **attached document** — a file the Lead uploads to the Project — for motivation and sustainability, the risk register and stakeholders, and the reflection and close sign-off. Not the MOU: the MOU sets the targets and the tracker follows them.
_Avoid_: tracker sheet, full proposal, check-in, Ops Mentor (Save7 OS today's second writer of the tracker, with no counterpart in the model)

**Department tracker**:
A Department's record of how it is doing against its Department Head's MOU — the counterpart of a Project's **project tracker**, one per Department and kept from one Head to the next. A page of the Department's hub, reached by its Head from their Dashboard too, and **live regardless** where the hub is *under construction*, like the front page, since it follows the Head's MOU rather than the Department's unbuilt work. Holds **KPIs**, each a current figure against a target; **milestones**, each named and dated with a done tick; and **deliverables**, planned against actual. **No budget plan** — a Department has no budget in the model. Read by the Department's members and every seat-holder; written by the Head alone, with National Exco as backstop. A new Head's MOU sets new targets and the new Head revises the tracker to them; with no Head it stays readable, Exco alone may write it, and nobody is sent a duty. **Nothing in it is computed or chased**: nothing goes red and nothing reaches a Dashboard. The Head's **Department reports** account against it but **stamp nothing from it**, sitting beside it in the hub as a Project's Updates sit beside its project tracker — a KPI is already a figure against a target and needs no evidence pinned to it. Not the MOU: the MOU sets the targets and the tracker follows them.
_Avoid_: MOU KPIs in the OKRs Hub, Key Result (a Key Result's progress is a claimed 0–100 figure, not a figure against a target), Department report (the Head's account upward, an Update; the tracker is what it accounts against)

**Departments Hub**:
The hub holding the four Department hubs, under National Centre and open to every member — and, unlike the two Centres, holding a page of its own: a **directory** of the Departments. Each Department's row gives its name, and whether it is under construction; its **Head**, or *No Head yet*; **what it is for**, a short standing description written by the Head with National Exco as backstop; **what it is working on**, which is read rather than written — the Department's own Objectives from the OKRs Hub, and a way into the Key Results naming it as a supporting unit — since a hand-written account goes stale and the ladder already holds the answer; when it last reported (see Update); and the reader's own standing in it: a member, inactive, a request pending, or none. Membership is not managed here; that is the Head's, from their Dashboard.

**Every Department's hub opens on a front page** — its signpost, open to every member, and built for every Department whether or not the rest is. It carries the directory's row at full size, the way in for someone who is not a member (asking to join, or closed where there is no Head), and whatever the Department offers everyone: its **Events**, and for Media, its brand kit, templates, coverage log, photo and video library and the Content Request intake; for Tech, the **Feature ideas** box. **Behind it is the Department's own work**, which its members, its Head and the seats that read everything **enter** — National Exco, and the Advisory board while Advising, writing there as backstop to the Head; Branch Management does not enter, the Head owning the work. On an under-construction hub the notice is what lies behind the front page, so a Head runs their Department from the front page, their Dashboard and the OKRs Hub until the rest is built. The hub's **Reports** page — its Department reports — is gated more tightly than either: the Head writes it, and only they, the General Liaison and National Exco read it. The member list is on no page of the hub: the Head holds it, and fellow members see one another only on the work they share.
_Avoid_: Departments group (it holds a page, so it is more than a group), department list (Exco add Departments on this directory, but it is a page for every member, not an admin list), lobby, landing page

**Operations Hub**:
The **COO's** hub, national in scope: where Save7 keeps track of its people, its branches and its paperwork. Holds the **Volunteers** page — the **master volunteer tracker**, one row per volunteer in every branch, giving their branch, seats, Departments, Projects and Project Lead status, their **Last seen** and **Last sign-in**, filterable by how far onboarding has got and by **Activity** band, each row opening that person's **Profile** and carrying **the same switches as the Users hub**, each reader seeing only those they own on the rows they own — Branch Management deactivate and reactivate their own branch's people from here as well as from Branch Centre, flipping a switch being an act on a person rather than an edit to the hub; the **Branches** page — where National Exco add, edit and delete branches — one row per branch with its Branch Management, its active/inactive status, how many volunteers it has and how many await acceptance, and its latest **branch report** with the date it was written, so that a quiet branch is visible without anything going red; the **Events** page — the nation's Events, put on here, and a list of every Event in the organisation, where the backstop over them works; the **SOPs**; and the **Compliance** section — **Resolutions**, **Agreements**, the **MOUs** page holding every signed MOU, **Templates** (an MOU template for each kind of MOU, the SOP template, indemnity and consent forms — downloaded by every reader of the hub, kept by its editors, and owing nothing), **Meetings**, and an **Incident log**, under construction.

**Read by everyone holding a seat or Project Lead status**, and by no plain Volunteer — an Operations Manager briefs their volunteers rather than handing them the hub. **Some pages are gated tighter**: the master tracker and Profiles are read by National Exco, the General Liaison, the Advisory board, Super Admins, every Branch Management seat (every branch, not only their own) and the Department Heads, and not by Project Leads; Meetings by National Exco, the General Liaison and the Advisory board; a Resolution's reason by those and the person it concerns (see Resolution); the Action Points page by National Exco and the General Liaison only; a signed MOU by those its record names. **Its own content is edited by the COO, the CEO and the General Liaison, and nobody else** — not the CFO or CGO, not an Advising member of the Advisory board, not Branch Management. Acts the model gives to someone keep their owner wherever their page sits: the General Liaison keeps the Meetings record, enters MOU records and runs the lifecycle of branchless volunteers from the master tracker, and National Exco's collective acts stay theirs. Branch Management read here and act on their own branch's volunteers from Branch Centre. An Operations Manager reaches the hub by **submission**: what they draft goes to the COO from their own hub in Branch Centre, and their branch reports are written there and shown here.
_Avoid_: ops hub, operations dashboard, Compliance Hub (folded into this hub as its Compliance section), events calendar (the Events page here holds the nation's Events and the backstop's list, not a calendar for volunteers to sign up from — see Event)

**Finance Hub**:
The **CFO's** hub, national in scope, holding Save7's **one ledger** — a single set of books in which a branch is a dimension on a line, never a book of its own, and a line with no branch is the organisation's. **Save7 OS's finance build carried across whole**, the model deciding who holds each power and the build deciding what the records are and how they move. Its pages, as Save7 OS draws them: **Overview** — cash on hand, income against spend, the action queue, each branch's Budget burn; the **Financial tracker** — the ledger itself, Budget against actual, and where a manual outgoing payment awaits its co-signatures; **Fuel claims**; **Branch spend**; **Spend requests**; **Claude seats** — subscriptions Save7 pays for on people's own accounts and recovers from them unless it funds the seat outright; **Section 18A** — the tax certificates issued to donors, and the donors themselves; the **Receipt vault**; the **Asset register** — what Save7 owns, who holds it and when it was last verified; and its **settings** — the organisation's details, opening cash, the fuel rate, the financial year, the certificate issuer, **Budgets** by category and by branch, the bank accounts and who may co-sign. And **Your money**, the one page group every member reaches: **My claims**, **My branch spend**, **My Claude seat** and **My documents**. Budget plans are not reviewed here but from the reviewer's Dashboard (see Project tracker).

**Opens to every member, gated by page.** A plain Volunteer reaches Your money and nothing else. **Whoever writes the books** — **National Exco**, the Advisory board while **Advising**, and the **General Liaison** — reads and writes all of it alike; **Super Admins** and the Advisory board while **Observing** read all of it and write none. **Branch Management** reach Your money, where their branch's Claims show by name, and write nothing in the books; a seated **Finance Manager** decides their branch's Claims, budget plans and smaller Spend requests. A Project's **Lead** reads its budget plan and its spend. Approving a fuel claim and paying it are kept apart, and a Branch spend line posts when approved and is **paid out** later if somebody paid for it themselves; paying is open to whoever writes the books, the claimant included, the decision being the check — but **nobody decides their own Claim**. A record a flow has posted is never deleted as a side effect of deleting something else, and **nothing here blocks spend**: a Budget reports, a missing co-signature flags, and neither refuses.
_Avoid_: the books (one page of it), money hub, finance admin (Save7 OS today's role, not a place)

**Claim**:
Money a person asks to be reimbursed or accepted as spent — a **fuel claim**, the distance driven at the fuel rate frozen onto the claim so that a later change never restates it, or a **Branch spend** line, which carries its receipt. **Lodged by the person themselves**, as *pending*, and withdrawable by them only while it is pending — save that whoever writes the books may file a fuel claim for somebody not on the platform, under the name they give. Decided — approved, rejected, or **queried**, a query going back to the claimant to answer before the decision is taken — by **whoever writes the books**, the duty naming the CFO, and once a branch has a **Finance Manager** by them for that branch, the CFO keeping a Finance Manager's own Claims and those with no branch. **Nobody decides their own.** An approved fuel claim is then **paid**, which posts its line to the ledger. An approved Branch spend line posts the moment it is approved, the purchase being the spend; one paid on somebody's own card is then **paid out** when Save7 pays them back, which posts nothing more. Paying is open to whoever writes the books, the claimant included. A Branch spend line belongs to **a branch or the organisation**, may name the **Project** it was for, which is how a Project's spend reaches the Finance Hub, and may name the **Spend request** it carries out — one with none is flagged, never refused. Nothing caps a Claim: the decision is the check on each one and the branch's Budget reports the total.
_Avoid_: reimbursement request (the user's informal term), team spend (Save7 OS's older name, where a team means a branch), expense, invoice

**Spend request**:
Approval asked for **before** money is spent, where a Claim comes after. Asked for on a **Google Form** — the one way in, for volunteers and staff alike, so nobody needs the platform to ask — whose sheet Save7 OS pulls in one way, never writing back. The requester is a **name and an email** as typed, never matched to a person. It waits first on its **lead** — the person on the platform whose email the form names, else the Branch Manager of the branch named, else the General Liaison — who approves or declines it; only then on a **decision** by whoever writes the books, the CFO by name, or by the branch's Finance Manager below a threshold once one is seated: approved, possibly for less than was asked, declined with a reason, or queried. The money is then spent and logged as Branch spend lines naming the request; spend over the approved amount is **flagged, never refused**. The requester hears the outcome from their lead. Kept on the Finance Hub's **Spend requests** page.
_Avoid_: purchase order, pre-approval, the form (one way in, not the request)

**Co-signatory**:
A person who may sign a **manual outgoing payment** on the ledger, two signatures from two different people being what authorises one. **Held by the person, not by any seat**, mirroring who the bank mandate names — which a seat cannot express — and granted or withdrawn by National Exco alone. A payment awaiting its signatures still counts in the books and is flagged rather than held back; the duty to sign it stands on every co-signatory who has not, shared the way a Department's members share an unanswered Content Request, and clears at the second signature.
_Avoid_: approver, signatory (the bank's word, for the mandate this mirrors)

**Budget**:
An amount allotted to a **branch** or to a **category** of spend for a **period** — by default the organisation's **financial year**, which starts in a month kept in the Finance Hub's settings (March today) and is a separate thing from the fixed March-to-February tax year Section 18A runs on, though any period may be set and two for the same target never overlap. **Set by whoever writes the books**, in the Finance Hub's settings, and read by the branch's Finance Manager and Branch Management for their own branch. **Reports and never blocks**: spend over it shows, and no Claim is refused because of it. **Never follows a Season**, an irregular chapter declared by Exco being the wrong clock for money. A Project's money is a separate view rather than a Budget: its **budget plan**, submitted from its project tracker, is reviewed by its home branch's Finance Manager, or by the CFO by name where it has no home or the branch has no Finance Manager, from their Dashboard, and **accepted or returned with a reason, never left to lapse** — and an accepted plan is the Project's budget, shown against its spend. Budgets, category Budgets and Project budget plans are three views of the same money and are never added together. A Department has no Budget of its own: its spend is booked to the organisation under a category until it holds money of its own.
_Avoid_: allocation, budget line (one line of a Project's budget plan)

**Branch Centre**:
A Hub holding two seat hubs — **Branch Managers** and **Branch Operations Managers**. **There is no Branch Finance Managers hub**: the Finance Hub is the one central place for money, Save7 OS's build carried there whole, so a hub of the Finance Manager's could only point at it — and a hub that only points elsewhere is a menu rather than a place. No page of its own, the same as National Centre. It is the branch counterpart at the top level, and its existence is what lets "National" in National Centre name an axis instead of describing contents loosely. **The Operations Hub and the Finance Hub are national** — the COO's and the CFO's — and hold no branch half for Branch Centre to take: each seat hub holds that seat's own work for its branch, distinct from both, and no seat hub keeps a **second set of books**.

**The Branch Managers hub** holds what Branch Management holds jointly, as well as the Branch Manager's own: **Volunteers** — the branch's **access requests** awaiting an answer, then its roster, which is the master volunteer tracker filtered to the branch and carrying the active/deactivated switch rather than a second list; **MOUs** — the branch's Project Lead MOU records, read-only: who holds one, whether it is used, and the Project it became, the Branch Manager having authored them offline and the General Liaison entering them; **Events** — the branch's Events, put on from here by any of its Branch Management; and **the branch at a glance** — its Projects, its Objectives and its latest branch report, read from the hubs that hold them and writing nothing. The joint work sits in the Branch Manager's hub without making it the Branch Manager's: the Operations Manager and the Finance Manager answer the same access requests **from their Dashboard**, and flip the same switches from the master tracker. **The Branch Operations Managers hub** holds the Operations Manager's **submissions** — SOPs and Agreement drafts sent to the COO, each visible until approved, or until withdrawn or resubmitted after a return — and the branch's **branch reports**. Onboarding progress is a filter on the master tracker, not a page here. **A Finance Manager's money duties are worked from the Finance Hub and their Dashboard** (see Finance Manager), never from Branch Centre. **Who opens it.** Branch Centre is **gated at the hub**: like the **Users** hub, it is hidden from anyone who can open none of the hubs under it, so no plain Volunteer, Project Lead or Department Head sees it by that standing alone. A **Branch Management** seat opens **both seat hubs of its own branch**, and no other branch's — reading across branches stays on the Operations Hub's master volunteer tracker and Branches page. The seats that reach every hub across every branch — National Exco, the General Liaison, the Advisory board and Super Admins — reach every branch's Branch Centre by their own entries, may do there what those entries already give them, and see it **one branch at a time**, exactly as that branch's seat holders do: there is no combined view, the Operations Hub being that.
_Avoid_: Branch Management (the three seats acting jointly over a branch's people — an authority, not a place in the nav)

**Under construction**:
A **declared state, not an omission**: the thing exists in the model, appears wherever it belongs, and says plainly that it is not built yet. It is what lets the platform show the **whole shape of the organisation** while the content arrives afterwards, and what stops *"not yet decided"* from rendering to a member as *"does not exist"*. On a **Hub**, it means the hub is in the nav for whoever may reach it and its page carries the notice rather than content — carried today by **Tech** and **Education**. Research carried it until its **Research board** was placed behind its front page. On a Department's hub the mark is set and lifted by National Exco alone, and the notice sits **behind its front page**, which every Department has whether built or not — so joining, the directory's account of it and the Head's management of its members are live in a Department whose own work is not.

It has a **second job, on the effort rather than the product**: it is the tag for anything whose build may land **either in this map or after the merge**, that being a question deliberately left open rather than answered early. Anything under construction is therefore **parked, never closed** — it is in scope, and the only open question is when. This is what separates it from work ruled out of scope, which does not come back.
_Avoid_: coming soon, TODO, out of scope (a scope ruling, and it never returns — under construction is in scope and merely unbuilt)

**Shell**:
The frame around whatever page a person is looking at — the navigation, and the chrome that says who they are and what they can reach. **There is one Shell, and every person gets it**: what appears in it is assembled from the seats and statuses that person holds, so someone holding several seats sees the union of what those seats reach, and a plain Volunteer sees their Dashboard, the OKRs Hub and National Centre with everything beneath it. There is no second frame chosen at sign-in, and **navigating never changes what a person is** — opening a page shows that page and nothing else. This is the one thing the model requires of the Shell; everything else about it, placement included, belongs to the work that builds it.
_Avoid_: admin view, member view, the admin half / member half (the model has no halves)

**Mission & vision**:
Save7's standing statement of what it exists to do — the top rung of the OKR ladder, written by National Exco as free-form prose. **Standing, never seasonal**: Exco edit it in place and the platform keeps no chapters of it, because a mission that accumulated versions would be a Strategy. It is the fixed thing each Season's Strategy moves against, which is what lets a member read the hub as *this is who we are*, then *this is what we are doing about it now, and what we did before*. **One write-up, not two fields** — mission and vision are one statement here.
_Avoid_: mission statement, purpose, values, mission & strategy (the old fused layer — see Strategy)

**Strategy**:
How Save7 pursues its Mission & vision right now, written by National Exco as free-form prose. **One Strategy per Season, and the Season is what chapters it**: a new Strategy is written when Exco declare a new Season, and it changes as the scope of what the organisation becomes changes. Sits below the Mission & vision and above the Pillars — Exco define the Pillars from both — but **holds nothing**: no Pillar, Objective or Key Result hangs off a Strategy, so a Season turning over moves nothing beneath it. **Not several named strategies standing side by side**; that shape is a Pillar's, and two rungs of the same shape one above the other would be a redundant rung.
_Avoid_: strategic plan, roadmap, strategic priority, pillar, mission & strategy (the old fused layer — see Mission & vision)

**Season**:
A chapter of Save7's **Strategy**, declared by National Exco. Has **no fixed length** — not a calendar year, an academic year, or a cycle of any set duration; a new Season begins when Exco decide there is a new Strategy to write. Scopes the Strategy **and nothing else**: the Mission & vision stands above it, and Pillars, Objectives and Key Results carry no Season and never roll over, so there is no archiving, carry-forward or closing-off anywhere beneath it. Seasons accumulate rather than overwrite and all stay visible, so that any member can see how the organisation has changed over time; the latest is marked current, and past Seasons stay editable — nothing is frozen.
_Avoid_: cycle, term, year, phase, quarter, sprint

**Pillar**:
An organisation-wide focus area defined by National Exco from the Mission & vision and the current Strategy — standing examples: Public awareness, Help hospital systems, Help patients, Nimble operations. A **theme, not a commitment**: a Pillar has no owner, no Key Results and no progress of its own, and exists so that every Objective can be seen to serve something. Several stand at a time. Standing, not seasonal — revised in place rather than rewritten each Season. Retiring one **archives** it: removed from the list new Objectives may choose from, still displayed for Objectives already tagged to it. **Displays the Projects serving it**, rolled up from every rung beneath — the one piece of content a Pillar carries, and a directory rather than a commitment, so it remains true that a Pillar has no Key Results and no progress of its own.
_Avoid_: theme, focus area, goal, category, strategic priority

**Objective**:
A unit's commitment, standing on exactly one Pillar — the tag is mandatory, and an Objective that fits no Pillar means Exco needs a new Pillar, not that it should go untagged. Belongs to a **branch**, a **Department**, or the **nation**; the three are structurally identical and differ only in what they hang off. Belongs to a unit, never to a person, so **no Objective has an owner**: it displays **who created it**, and accountability sits one layer down, on its Key Results' owners. A branchless volunteer needs no Objective of their own — they reach work by being made an owner of a Key Result. Written by anyone holding a seat or Project Lead status, **for any unit** — authorship is not confined to the unit you supervise, which makes the OKRs Hub the one place the edit-own/read-others ceiling does not apply, with the creator stamp carrying the accountability that scoping would otherwise enforce. Plain Volunteers do not write Objectives. **Bigger than any one champion**, which is what separates it from a Project, born bottom-up from one volunteer's MOU. Carries a **progress** bar of its own — the unweighted mean of its Key Results' progress, **derived and uneditable**, because an Objective has no owner to set it. Unowned Key Results count at zero, so an Objective whose work nobody has taken reads low however well its owned Key Results are going; that sag is the point, not a defect, since it puts the organisation's work-discovery signal one layer up where the people who can act on it are looking. Carries **no health status**: health is a reading an owner makes, and an Objective has none. **Displays the Projects serving it** — those that named the Objective itself and those that named any of its Key Results, which roll up. The list is a directory and nothing more: a Project named here changes no figure and owes the Objective nothing. May be put **on ice** — parked on purpose, to be picked up later — which is the one status an Objective carries, a decision about a commitment rather than a reading of one. Its **creator** ices or thaws it while they still hold a seat or Project Lead status, and so may **National Exco**, the **General Liaison** and the **Advisory board** while advising; never a Super Admin, who writes nothing. Icing records **who, when and a short reason**, all mandatory, and thawing clears them — only the current icing is kept, the Updates being the history. **No date to come back to**: like a Project's, on ice is a status, and nothing calls it back. Its Key Results go on ice with it (see Key Result). While iced it **leaves every current view** — its Pillar's list, the supporting-units filter, and the Pillar's Projects unless a Project also names something live — and stands only on the OKRs Hub's **on-ice page**, reached by a link from the hub, listing iced Objectives with their Key Results beneath, grouped by Pillar; its progress holds where it was. Projects that named it keep the link, shown on ice, but none may name it afresh. Its authors may still reword or delete it and add or remove Key Results and owners, so that it can be made ready to thaw. **A Key Result is never iced on its own** — an owner who wants one parked pauses it.
_Avoid_: goal, OKR, initiative, target, project; for on ice — archived (a retired Pillar's word), frozen, shelved, paused (a Key Result's health)

**Key Result**:
One of the several commitments that move an Objective's needle; the Objective is met when its Key Results are. Written by the same people who write Objectives — anyone holding a seat or Project Lead status — who then attach its **owners** from a drop-down of anyone in the organisation, themselves included. Owners are a **flat set**: one is usual, several are allowed, none is primary, and there is no co-owner concept. A Key Result may also sit **unowned**, displayed as such, which is how work advertises that nobody is doing it. **Any member may add themselves** as an owner of **any** Key Result — unowned or already owned, in any unit, plain Volunteers included — and **taking it is the act**: nobody approves it, the existing owners have no say, and a self-added owner's acceptance is given by the joining itself. The check is after the fact: anyone holding a seat or Project Lead status may remove an owner, as for any owner. **Unowned** is found through a filter in the OKRs Hub open to every member; nothing puts it on anybody's Dashboard save its creator's, once its target date passes. Carries a **progress** bar, an **acceptance** per owner, a **health** status, an optional **target date**, a **done** tick, a log of **Updates**, and its **supporting units**.

**Progress** is one figure from 0 to 100 that an owner slides. It is a **claim, not a measurement**: nothing computes it and nothing can contradict it, which is what makes the latest updates the evidence behind it rather than a diary beside it. Key Results differ wildly in kind — a plan either written or not, a session held twice a month, a satisfaction percentage held above a threshold — and one sliding claim is the only figure that fits all of them and stays comparable across them.

**Acceptance** is the handshake between one owner and the work. It is held **per owner**, never per Key Result, because it cannot be given on anybody's behalf: attaching a new owner to a Key Result others have accepted leaves that person unaccepted, and the Key Result displays how many of its owners have accepted. There is **no decline** — not accepting is the decline, visible as such, and only someone holding a seat or Project Lead status can remove an owner. Nothing times out.

**Health** is one of *On track*, *At risk*, *Off track* or *Paused*, shared across every owner with the last write standing; two owners who disagree settle it in the latest updates, not in the schema. *Paused* suspends the target date. Moving health to *At risk*, *Off track* or *Paused* is saved together with an Update saying why (see Update), which is what keeps a pause loud.

A **target date** is optional — the standing commitments that never end take none, and none is imposed by a Season, which scopes nothing below the Strategy. A Key Result whose date has passed shows **overdue, in red**, on its owners' Dashboards, exactly as an Action Point does, and nothing carries it forward. The **done** tick is what clears the red: set by an owner, it puts progress at 100 and takes the Key Result off the Dashboard while leaving it on the ladder. There is deliberately **no *Missed*** — an owner must not be able to clear their own red by declaring failure — so a Key Result that was genuinely missed stays red until somebody holding a seat rewords, redates or deletes it.

An **unowned** Key Result has no acceptance and no health, its bar is pinned at zero and immovable, and it can never be ticked done. Where it carries a target date that passes, the red falls to whoever created it.

An owner sets exactly those six things — progress, their own acceptance, health, the done tick, the Updates and the **supporting units** — and may not reword, reassign or delete the Key Result, nor remove themselves as an owner **unless they added themselves** — a commitment you took you may put down, one you were given goes back through whoever gave it; all of which belong to anyone holding a seat or Project Lead status, **none of whom may set the first five**. Supporting units are the one field both sides write, because the person who discovers that help is needed is the one doing the work, and a tag that obliges nobody hands an owner nothing over the unit named. Nobody overrules an owner's reading: a Key Result claiming *On track* whose log shows no recent Update is visibly stale, and staleness is a better signal than a drop-down two people are fighting over.

A Key Result that **cannot move until another unit acts** is said in the fields that already exist — health *At risk*, that unit named in supporting units, and a dated Update saying what is stuck. There is deliberately **no *Blocked*** health value: recording that a unit is blocking something raises the question of what that unit owes and when it clears, which is precisely the obligation supporting units do not carry — and since *Paused* stops the clock, a *Blocked* that did the same would let an owner clear their own red by pointing at somebody else.

A Key Result displays the Projects that have named it; those Projects also appear on its Objective and on that Objective's Pillar.

A Key Result whose Objective is **on ice** is iced with it: it leaves its owners' Dashboards, an overdue one stops being red, and its creator's two standing duties are suspended — icing raises nothing on anybody. Its owners keep their acceptance and their readings, which icing does not overwrite — it is not *Paused* imposed from outside — but cannot change any of their six fields until it is thawed. Thawing brings back whatever still applies, a date that passed while it was iced turning red at once until somebody holding a seat redates it. On ice and *Paused* are deliberately not the same word twice: *Paused* is an owner's reading of one Key Result, on ice an author's decision about a whole Objective.
_Avoid_: task, action point (discrete and binary with a mandatory date, where a Key Result is a standing commitment carrying progress with an optional one — the dates do not distinguish them, the progress does), KPI, target, milestone

**Supporting Units**:
The parts of the organisation whose help a Key Result needs — **never the people doing the work**, who are its owners. A **readout, not a claim**: naming a unit obliges it to nothing, queues nothing, creates no request to accept or decline, and puts nothing on anybody's Dashboard. The model had already settled that a unit cannot owe anything — an Action Point's owners are people, or a branch's Branch Management, while Departments and National Exco are explicitly not assignable as groups — and a Department Head's authority over their members' work is not by itself a reason for them to owe something here (see Duty). Where the answer really is *"Tech should do this"*, the model already has the instrument: attach someone from Tech as an **owner**.

Optional and multi-select, drawn from: each **Department**, each **branch**, each branch's **Branch Management** as an entry of its own, and **National Exco**. A branch and its Branch Management are separate entries because needing a branch's volunteers and needing its three managers are different asks. **Not** in the list: individuals (that is what owners are), a Department Head separately from their Department (the Head is already who the Department reaches), the **Advisory board** (retained working capability, not a body with rights of its own to be leaned on), and a **Project** — a Project already names the ladder it serves, under its Lead's control, and a tag pointing the other way would give one pair of things two overlapping links.

Set by whoever wrote the Key Result **and by its owners**. Displayed on the Key Result and available as a **filter in the OKRs Hub, open to every member**, read there being flat; it does not roll up to the Objective. A tagged unit is never told — it finds out by looking — so the field records who the work leans on rather than starting anything.
_Avoid_: team (Save7 OS already uses it for a branch, and a Project Team is people), team tag, stakeholder, collaborator, assignee, contributor

**Update**:
One dated entry in a log — a **title**, a **date** and a body of free text. The title is always present, even when it says no more than *General check-in*, so that a log renders one way rather than two; a notable entry titles itself accordingly. **Authored, never derived**: nothing writes an Update for anyone, and no automatic history of changes is kept beside the log — what happened between two Updates is unrecorded, the Update being the record. Neither title nor body may ever be saved empty. Its author may **edit** it, which shows it as *edited*, but **nobody deletes one** — it is evidence; it goes only with whatever it hangs off, which is deleted for errors. An author who loses the right to write there leaves their Updates standing, signed. **Nothing is owed on a cadence and nothing chases**: no Update is ever late, nothing goes red, and the log shows when it was last written to — that age, and no threshold turning it into a badge, being the signal.

**One mechanism, used in four places.** A Key Result carries a log of Updates written by its **owners**; a Project carries one written by its **Lead**, with National Exco as backstop; a **branch** carries one written by its **Operations Manager** — its **branch reports**, the operations line's account to the COO, written from the Branch Operations Managers hub and shown on the Operations Hub's Branches page; and a **Department** carries one written by its **Head**, with National Exco as backstop — its **Department reports**, the Head's account upward to Exco, kept on a *Reports* page of the Department's hub. The two kinds of report are shaped alike: each owes no date and nothing goes red when one is late, the Branches page and the Departments directory showing when each last reported. A new branch report sits on the **COO's** and the **CEO's** Dashboards until each has opened it; a new Department report sits on the **General Liaison's** alone, the seat that routes Heads to Exco and carries into the room, as an Agenda Point, whatever is worth raising. Department reports are read by the Head, the General Liaison and National Exco only — an account given upward, not a noticeboard for the Department's members — and the Reports page is built for every Department, under construction or not, like its front page.

**On a Key Result, each Update stamps the reading it was written against** — the progress and health as they stood — so the log pairs every claim with its evidence. Moving health **to** *At risk*, *Off track* or *Paused* **is saved together with an Update** saying why; moving back to *On track* and sliding progress need none. No new Update reaches anybody's Dashboard — not the creator's, the other owners' or a supporting unit's: like supporting units, it is found by looking. Besides each Key Result's own log, newest first, the OKRs Hub has a **Latest updates** page: every Key Result's Updates across the organisation, newest first, filtered as the rest of the hub is — by unit, Pillar, Objective and supporting unit — and read flat, by every member. Project Updates and reports do not appear in it. An iced Key Result's log is frozen with its other owner fields; nothing rolls over, so the logs are the organisation's memory. A Project Team member does not write to a Project's log — sitting on a team grants no authority, and a log anyone may post to is a discussion thread rather than a record; a team member's own work surfaces through the Key Results they own.

**The weight differs with what it hangs off, and deliberately so.** On a Key Result, progress is a claim nothing computes and nothing can contradict, which makes the Updates the **only evidence** behind the bar. A Project has no progress bar, so its log is a **record** — what happened and when — carrying no evidential load; a branch's is the same.
_Avoid_: check-in, note, comment, diary, latest updates (the log is of Updates; "latest updates" names the display, not the thing), monthly report, interval update (neither kind of report has a cadence)

**Dashboard**:
The surface a person lands on when they sign in — **everyone has one**, whatever they hold, it is always where they land (nobody chooses another landing page), and it is the control centre for that account. **Per seat**, so what appears on it follows the person's duties — a Volunteer's and an Exco member's are the same surface carrying different things — yet **nothing on it is designed for a particular seat**: each hub declares the duties it raises and who is the lowest level that can act on one, and a Dashboard shows whatever falls to its person. Distinct from a Hub, which is organisational and shared; a Dashboard is personal.

**Seven sections, always in this order**, the same on every Dashboard and rearranged by nobody. A section with nothing in it is hidden, save two: *Workflow* says so when it is empty, and *My Projects* points an empty one to the Projects Hub.

1. **Workflow** — a heading, not a term of the model (what sits in it is a Duty): every Duty the person owes, raised or standing, in **one list**: overdue first by how long overdue, then soonest due, then undated standing duties by age; each labelled by the seat or source it arrives through, never grouped, and filterable by seat without the filter becoming a mode. It holds their open and overdue Action Points (overdue in red), **every Key Result they own**, the work assigned to them on a Project, **their dated Tasks**, and what they must act on by virtue of a seat — **volunteers waiting to join a Project they lead** or **a Department they head**, **an invitation to join a Project or a Department, or to organise an Event**, awaiting their answer, and **an accepted MOU they hold with no Project yet**. The COO's also carries the **SOPs and Agreement drafts submitted for approval** and any **Agreement past its renewal date**; the COO's and the CEO's carry **branch reports they have not yet opened**; the General Liaison's carries **Department reports they have not yet opened**; each Branch Management seat's carries its branch's **access requests awaiting an answer**, one row for the branch, answerable from the row; the Tech Head's carries the **Feature ideas awaiting a decision**, one row for them all, answerable from the row — National Exco's instead while Tech has no Head; an Operations Manager's carries **an SOP or Agreement draft of theirs returned with a reason**. An Action Point or a Key Result is ticked done here by its owner.
2. **Announcements** — every Announcement still in force. Never above *Workflow*, and never a duty.
3. **What you run** — a Department Head's **management** of each Department they head: its member list and inactive flags, and the invitations they have sent, run from here rather than from the Department's hub, and repeated for each Department they head. The only section about what a seat runs rather than what the person owes or is.
4. **Waiting on others** — what the person has started and someone else must act on: **any Claim of theirs still waiting on Finance**, any **request to join a Project or a Department they are still waiting on**, **any Content Request they submitted that is still live**, **any Feature idea of theirs still pending**, and the standalone Action Points they raised, with who has not yet accepted. Nothing here is owed by them; it shows where each stands.
5. **Your events** — **every Event they have signed up to** until its date passes, and **the upcoming Events they organise** with how many have signed up.
6. **My Projects** — a section, not a page of its own, listing the Projects they lead and, apart from them, the Projects whose team they sit on, each by name, status and their place on it, linking to its page and Board and, for its Lead, its tracker; it shows no progress, milestones or money and repeats none of their duties, holds only Projects they belong to whatever seats they hold, gathers ended Projects into a collapsed *Finished* group, and for someone on none says so and points to the Projects Hub with *Open to me* on
7. **You in the organisation** — who they are across the organisation: branch, seats, Departments and any inactive flag in one; Projects are not repeated here. The Shell names only the person and their branch, so this is the one place their seats are listed.

**Nothing else goes on a Dashboard**: no MOU status, no links or shortcut cards into hubs (getting around is the Shell's), and nothing from outside the organisation's own records. For many plain Volunteers the Key Results they own will be the only thing in *Workflow*, which is the same reasoning that opened the OKRs Hub to them.
_Avoid_: home page, my work, personal hub, landing page (it is the landing page, but the term names the surface — the screen *before* sign-in is the **Sign-in page**)

**Announcement**:
A notice from **National Exco** to the whole organisation, shown in the *Announcements* section of every Dashboard until the expiry its author sets, and then gone. **Never a duty**: it carries no due date and asks nothing of anyone — if Exco needs something done, that is an Action Point with an owner. Org-wide only; branch- and Department-scoped announcements are not modelled, and nothing is sent anywhere — it is found on the Dashboard.
_Avoid_: notice, news, broadcast, notification

**Profile**:
One page per person showing **who they are across the organisation** — their branch, their seats, their Departments, the Project Teams they sit on and the Projects they lead, with their **Last seen**, **Last sign-in** and **Activity** band — opened from the **master volunteer tracker** in the Operations Hub or from the **Users** hub, and read only by **those who read that tracker**: National Exco, the General Liaison, the Advisory board, Super Admins, every Branch Management seat and the Department Heads. Not a Dashboard: a Dashboard is what a person sees of their own duties, a Profile what the people who look after volunteers see of them.
_Avoid_: account, user page, bio, member card

**Last seen**:
The last time an account **opened Save7 OS** — any use of the platform, whether or not it began with a fresh sign-in. Recorded for every account, and the one input to its **Activity** band. A moment, not a duration: nothing is added up from it, so it sits with the rule that the platform measures no time spent. Shown on the **Profile** and as a column on the **master volunteer tracker**, beside **Last sign-in**.
_Avoid_: last login, last active, last activity

**Last sign-in**:
The last time a person **actually signed in** — proved who they are at the **Sign-in page** — as distinct from **Last seen**, since a person who stays signed in can use the platform for months without signing in again. Shown beside Last seen on the **Profile** and the master volunteer tracker, **for information only**: it sets no band, gates nothing, and a wide gap between the two is ordinary and flagged nowhere.
_Avoid_: last login, last seen (a different moment)

**Activity**:
A band **computed from an account's Last seen and nothing else** — never judged by a person, deliberately unlike a Branch's or a Project's status, which someone sets by hand. Three bands, fixed in the model: **Recent**, seen within 30 days; **Quiet**, 31 to 120 days; **Dormant**, over 120 — wide enough that a summer break does not turn the organisation dormant every February. No other signal is folded in: the work a person owns and closes is already legible on their Profile, their Key Results and the Boards, and weighting signals would make the band a judgement again. Carried by **every account**, seat holders included, so a dormant Branch Manager is as visible as a dormant volunteer; a deactivated account, or an invitation not yet claimed, carries none, each already showing its own state.

**Informational, and nothing more.** No band gates sign-in, hides anyone or deactivates anyone, and none is a **Duty**: a dormant account puts nothing on any Dashboard, since a duty that only deactivation could clear would push its owner to deactivate. Shown as a column and filter on the **master volunteer tracker** — and so on a branch's roster in the Branch Managers hub, which is that tracker filtered — and on the **Profile**, read by whoever reads those; not in the **Users** hub, which decides access rather than reading health, and not on the person's own Dashboard. The **dormancy review** that Branch Management, and the General Liaison for branchless volunteers, owe their people is a look at the Dormant band when they choose — after which they reach out offline, deactivate, or leave it be. Deletion stays National Exco's last resort, and graduating is never a reason for any of it. **Independent of a Department's inactive flag**: neither feeds the other.
_Avoid_: active / inactive (the account switch, a Branch's and a Project's status, and a Department member's flag), engagement, health (a Key Result owner's reading), activity level

**Duty**:
Anything a person owes — the single word for what a Dashboard lists, covering an Action Point raised on someone, work assigned to them on a Project, and an obligation falling to them by virtue of a seat. Two kinds. A **raised duty** is a stored record with owners and one done status, ticked off by an owner; Action Points, assigned Project work, **a dated Task** and **an owned Key Result** are raised — whether the owner was attached by someone else or **added themselves**, which makes no difference to what they owe — a Key Result carrying a target date goes overdue in red exactly as an Action Point does, and its done tick is what clears it. A **standing duty** is not stored and not addressed to anyone — it is computed from a state somebody is responsible for, so it appears wherever that responsibility sits, disappears when the state does, and has nothing to tick. It **aggregates**: a branch with three volunteers awaiting acceptance produces one duty, not three, and a Project Lead or a Department Head with three volunteers waiting to join produces one duty, not three — the same shape, one level down. An invitation to join a Project or a Department stands on its invitee the same way until answered, and an accepted MOU with no Project yet stands on its holder until the Project is created or the record is withdrawn. Whoever creates a Key Result carries two of them — owners who have not yet accepted, and Key Results left unowned past their target date — kept separate rather than merged, because one asks them to chase a person and the other to find one. That is also why joint responsibility needs no machinery — Branch Management's three seats each see the same standing duty because the state is theirs collectively, any one of them acting clears it for all three, and no assignment, claiming or division of labour is required. A duty sits with the **lowest level that can act on it**, being explicitly named overrides, and holding the authority to do something is never by itself a reason to owe it. Vocabulary of the model, not of the interface: nothing on screen says "duty".
_Avoid_: obligation, commitment, responsibility, to-do, item, task (assigned Project work is one kind of duty, not the word for all of them)

**Meeting**:
A recorded sitting of **National Exco**, the only meeting the platform keeps — weekly, though nothing depends on the cadence. Carries a title, a date and **attendance**, and holds Agenda Points. Attendance may include **guests** from elsewhere in the organisation, attached by Exco or the General Liaison; being named on attendance grants a guest no access to anything — not the minutes, not the agenda, not the record. Logged by the General Liaison, with Exco as backstop, and kept on the **Meetings page of the Operations Hub's Compliance section**. Read by National Exco, the General Liaison and the advisory board; explicitly **not** by Super Admins. **Corrected in place, never silently**: the General Liaison or Exco may edit a Meeting at any time, each edit stamped *edited* with who and when, and its earlier versions stay readable to the Meeting's readers — minutes being the record people come back to argue about. There is no confirming of minutes. **Deleting** a Meeting corrects a mistake — logged twice, or against the wrong date — by the General Liaison or Exco, and is refused while any Action Point still hangs off it, so that no owner's duty vanishes with it. Branch, Department and Project meetings are not modelled.
_Avoid_: minutes (a Meeting holds them rather than being them), session, sitting

**Agenda Point**:
One item of business at a Meeting — a heading plus a free-text note of what was discussed or decided. Several per Meeting, each separate. Holds zero or more Action Points; an Agenda Point that produces none is ordinary, since plenty is settled in the room.
_Avoid_: agenda item, topic, minute

**Action Point**:
A piece of work somebody owes, with **owners**, a **due date**, a free-text **note** of what is wanted, and one **done** status. Owners are one person, several people, or a branch's **Branch Management**, which resolves to its three members at the moment of assignment. Shown **overdue**, in red, once the due date passes; nothing carries it forward and nothing copies it — it stays outstanding until it is done. Reaches its owners on their Dashboard wherever they sit in the organisation, including people who may read nothing else about where it came from.

**Raised off an Agenda Point, or standing alone — and nothing else.** A Meeting's Agenda Point is its one optional parent; there, the General Liaison raises it with Exco as backstop, and whether an owner outside Exco sees the Agenda Point behind it is a per-Action-Point choice made by whoever raised it. **A Key Result is not a source**: work on a Key Result is reached by owning it, and a discrete job that relates to one is a standalone Action Point. A **standalone** one is raised by **anyone holding a seat or Project Lead status, on any member**, themselves included, and carries a **raised by** stamp — openness backed by visibility, as in the OKRs Hub. A further source would be a new optional parent, a deliberate change rather than a new row.

**Accepted per owner when it stands alone**, as a Key Result is: there is no decline, not accepting being the visible refusal; an owner added later starts unaccepted, Branch Management's three accept one by one, raising one on oneself is accepted at once, and it goes red on its date whether accepted or not. Who has not accepted is shown to whoever raised it and on the tracker, and is nobody's duty. An Action Point raised off a Meeting carries National Exco's authority and asks no acceptance.

**Ticked done** by an owner from their Dashboard, or by whoever raised it — who also changes its date and owners and cancels it, keeping that over what they raised after losing their seat or status, overseeing one's own Action Points being no seat power — with the General Liaison and every Exco member as **backstop** from the tracker. An owner may not drop themselves. The change reads the same everywhere at once. **Cancelled** with a reason when the work is no longer wanted, leaving its owners' Dashboards with the reason shown and staying on the tracker marked cancelled; **deleting** one corrects a mistake, by whoever raised it or the backstop, and is never a way of cancelling.

**The Action Points page** of the Operations Hub's Compliance section is the organisation-wide tracker: every Action Point from every source, outstanding and overdue at a glance, filterable by person, read and worked by National Exco and the General Liaison only. The Meetings page lists each Meeting's own. Whoever raised a standalone one sees what they raised from their Dashboard. On an owner's Dashboard every Action Point sits in **one list** with their other duties, labelled by where it came from, never grouped by source.
_Avoid_: task (now a term of its own — a Task hangs off a parent and has an optional due date, where an Action Point stands alone and its due date is mandatory), to-do, follow-up, item

**Board**:
A named set of columns holding cards, moved between them by hand. **One mechanism, several domains** — the project board, the **Research board** and the Media Hub's **content tracker** are instances of it, and what sits on a board is domain-specific: Tasks on a Project's, **Studies** on Research's, accepted Content Requests on Media's. Columns belong to the board rather than to the model, which fixes only that a card sits in exactly one column at a time and that moving it is somebody's deliberate act, never a computed one. A card may carry a **blocked** flag with a free-text note; it stays in its column and reads as blocked. The flag **never names a unit** — naming one would raise the question of what that unit owes and when it clears, which is precisely the obligation the model declined to create when it refused a *Blocked* health value on Key Results.
_Avoid_: kanban, swimlane, pipeline, workflow, column (names one part of it)

**Task**:
A piece of work somebody owes **under a parent** — a Project, or an accepted Content Request — carrying **owners** that default to whoever created it, an optional **due date**, a **done** status, and an **importance** of one to three set by its creator alone, which orders the list and does nothing else. Shows its creator's name. Appears on its owners' Dashboards **when it carries a due date**, and not otherwise: an undated Task is a note to self rather than a duty.

A Task under a Project may be left **unowned**, marked as such on the Board, and **any member of the Project Team may add themselves to any of its Tasks** on the same terms as a Key Result — taking it is the act, no approval, and a self-added owner may leave. The reach stops at the team: somebody outside it joins the Project first, through its Lead, and only then takes work. There is no advertising a single Task to the organisation at large.

**Renders differently in different places and is one concept regardless** — a card on a Board under a Project, a list item under a Content Request. In the Media Hub **nobody assigns a Task**: a member opts in to a Content Request and then writes their own, so creator and owner coincide by construction, and importance is that member's ordering of their own work.
_Avoid_: action point (which has a mandatory due date and stands alone or hangs off an Agenda Point, never a Project or a Content Request), to-do, ticket, card (names the display, not the thing)

**Study**:
A piece of research the **Research Department** is carrying out — a literature review, an audit, a survey, an interview study and the like — and the card on its **Research board**, the Board behind Research's front page. **Not a Project**: it needs no MOU and no sign-off, confers no Project Lead status and has no project tracker, so that deciding to research a topic is not gated by the machinery an initiative needs; a Study that grows into one becomes a Project the usual way and may point at it. Holds its **question**, in PICO form where that fits; its **kind**; its **methodology**; its **ethics** — not needed, pending or approved, with the approval letter attached; its **stage**, which is simply its column; a running **log** of what was found; its **documents**; and its **team** and **lead**.

**Opened by any member of the Research Department**, who becomes its lead. Its **team** mirrors a Project Team: everyone on it belongs to Research, and the lead is the only gate onto it. **The team writes the Study** — its fields, its log, its documents, and moving it between columns — and the rest of Research reads it; this is the shape a Research member's write over content in their Department's hub takes. A document is removed by whoever added it or by the Head. **The columns are the Head's**, with National Exco as backstop, and the Head and National Exco are backstop over every Study. Read only behind the front page — by Research's members, its Head and the seats that read everything — since datasets and ethics files may carry participants' data.

**Ethics is recorded, never enforced**: no column refuses a Study whose ethics is not approved, a literature review needing none and the board's columns meaning nothing to the model. A lead who leaves Research, is deactivated or goes quiet leaves the Study standing, its team still writing it, and the **Head names a new lead** from the team; nothing goes red and nobody is sent a duty. A Study **ends by reaching the last of the Head's columns** — *Published* or *Abandoned*, say — and is never deleted by its team, its log and ethics record being worth keeping; the Head or National Exco may delete one entered by mistake. **It reaches no Dashboard**: its members find it in Research's hub.
_Avoid_: research project (a Project is a different thing), research question (the question is one field of a Study), paper

**Open work**:
Work a member may take up without anyone deciding they should — **a way of reading what the model already holds, not a thing of its own**: unowned Key Results, unowned Tasks within one's own Project Teams, accepted Content Requests a Media Department member may opt in to, Projects open to them, and Events open to them with places left. Nothing is posted as an opportunity and nobody advertises one, so there is one list of the work rather than two that disagree. Taken by **doing**, never by asking. Never lapses. **An Action Point is never open work** — it is always raised on someone.
_Avoid_: opportunity, vacancy, job board, sign-up (the act of joining an Event, one kind of open work rather than the word for all of it), hand-raise

**Content Request**:
An ask for something to be made — the Media Hub's intake, open to **every member of the organisation** through the hub's Content section, which is an invitation rather than a workspace. Carries a tagline, a description, the **date wanted**, a type (post, reel, long-form) and the **channels** it is for (the website, Facebook, YouTube, Instagram, TikTok, LinkedIn and so on).

**Answered, never left to lapse.** It stands as a standing duty shared across the Media Department's members until one of them **accepts** it or **declines** it with a reason; any member may answer, and answering clears it for all of them. **National Exco may also answer one**, as backstop to the Head, without it ever standing on them as a duty. A request that quietly expired would let the Department refuse somebody without ever having to say no.

Accepting sets the **scheduled publish date** — the Department's promise, kept alongside the requester's date wanted rather than overwriting it, so that a date which has slipped stays visible. The accepted request becomes a card on the **content tracker** (Not started, In progress, Done, Published) and may carry Tasks. Members **opt in** freely to work on one — no approval, the Head's power to mark a member inactive within the Department being gate enough — and the opted-in members plus the Department Head are who may move its card.

**Overdue** once its scheduled publish date passes with the card short of Published: red on **everyone opted in**, or on **whoever accepted it** where nobody has — the same fallback the model uses for an unowned Key Result whose target date passes. **Published is set by hand** after posting; the platform posts to no channel and integrates with none.

The requester follows their own request from their **Dashboard** rather than from the hub, whose operational pages they cannot open, and it clears when the content is published. **A declined request stays there with its reason until the requester dismisses it**, since a request that vanished on being declined would tell them no without saying why. A member of the Media Department may also **create content directly**, which enters the tracker already accepted with no requester attached.
_Avoid_: brief, job, ticket, suggestion (a Feature idea is a different thing), content idea (names the invitation, not the record)

**Feature idea**:
Something a member wants Save7 OS to do — the platform's own suggestion box, and the one record in the model about the platform rather than the organisation's work. **Filed by any member of the organisation**, plain Volunteers included, on the **Tech Department's front page**, which is live although Tech is under construction; it carries a title and, optionally, what they mean by it. **Every member sees every one**, with its state and the decision's note, so the same idea is not filed five times and a refusal is not asked again blind.

**Decided by the Tech Head**, with National Exco as backstop — and by National Exco alone while Tech has no Head: filing an idea places nobody's work under anybody, so unlike joining, the box does not close without a Head. Three states: **pending**, **approved**, **declined**; a decline carries a **reason**, an approval may carry a note. The filer may **withdraw** one while it is pending. **Deleting** is the decider's and corrects a mistake — a duplicate, a misfire — never a way of saying no. **Approving is a decision, not a card**: the idea becomes work in Tech's own work behind its front page, which is under construction, so until that is built an approval is a complete answer on its own. It never lands on a Project, a Project being born of an MOU.

On the **Dashboard**, the waiting ideas sit as one row on the decider's *Workflow*, and a pending idea in its filer's *Waiting on others* until it is decided or withdrawn; the outcome stays readable on the front page.
_Avoid_: suggestion, feature request, ticket (an issue on a tracker), Content Request (Media's intake for something to be made)

**Event**:
Something Save7 puts on at a place, on a date — a campus stall, a donor-registration drive, a hospital visit. Carries a **title**, a **date** with an optional **end date** for one running over several days, optional start and end times, a **place**, the **unit it belongs to** — a **branch**, a **Project**, a **Department** or the **nation** — **one organiser**, and an optional **cap** on how many may sign up. Not an Action Point or a Task — nothing is owed and nothing is ticked done — and not a Meeting, which is National Exco's alone and keeps attendance and minutes.

**Put on the calendar by whoever answers for its unit**, with no approval: any one of a branch's Branch Management for that branch, a Project's Lead for that Project, a Department's Head for that Department, and the COO, the CEO or the General Liaison for the nation. Those three are also the **backstop**, editing or cancelling any Event.

**Lives with its unit — there is no Events hub.** An Event is put on and listed on its unit's own surface: a branch's in the **Branch Managers hub**, a Project's on its **page**, a Department's on its **front page**, and the nation's on the Operations Hub's **Events** page, which also lists every Event for the backstop. **No list shows a member every Event**: a member finds Events on their **Dashboard** — those open to them in their **Open work**, those they have signed up to, those they organise — and on the surfaces of their own units. **An Event's page sits outside every hub**, so no hub's gating closes it to the volunteers it needs: any member who reaches it reads it — its details, its organiser and who has signed up — and signs up there if the switch allows. **Separate from the Media Hub's content calendar**, which plots scheduled publish dates — the Media Department's promise, not a happening — and the two are not linked: an Event that wants coverage gets it through a Content Request, like anything else.

**The organiser** defaults to whoever created the Event; the creator may name any member instead, who must **accept** — a handshake cannot be given on anybody's behalf — the invitation standing on them until answered and never lapsing. The organiser edits the Event, cancels it and removes people signed up to it; the creator and the backstop keep their edit. For a plain Volunteer that is one of their five writes (see Volunteer).

**Signing up is the handshake**: a member signs up, and may withdraw, with no approval; the organiser may remove someone. Who may sign up is one switch the organiser sets — **its own people** (the branch's volunteers, the Project's team, the Department's members, and for a national Event everyone), the default, or **anyone** — and narrowing it removes nobody already signed up. A capped Event closes when full and reopens when someone withdraws. **Lowering the cap below those already signed up removes nobody**: the Event stays closed until withdrawals bring it under the cap. Whoever reads the Event reads who has signed up. An Event a member may still sign up to is part of their **Open work**. A member who reaches an Event they may not sign up to reads it all the same, and **its organiser, named on the page, is whom they ask**; the model adds no request to join an Event.

**Nothing is recorded after the day** — no attendance, no no-show — since the model measures no time spent and an attendance record is the first step towards a log of hours; the sign-up list stays readable on the past Event and nothing is counted from it. A **moved** Event keeps its sign-ups, each of them seeing the new date. A **cancelled** one stays on its unit's surface marked cancelled, with its reason, and leaves its sign-ups' Dashboards with the reason shown. **Deleting** an Event corrects a mistake and is never a way of cancelling one.

**On the Dashboard**: everyone signed up sees it until its date passes, and the organiser sees their upcoming Events with how many have signed up against any cap — **two sections of their own**, *Events you're signed up to* and *Events you organise*, while an Event open to them sits in **Open work** among the Key Results, Tasks and Projects there, never in a separate agenda or calendar of Events. A cancelled Event a member signed up to shows at the top with its reason. Neither is a duty — nothing to tick, nothing red — and a shortfall never becomes one, "not enough people" being a reading rather than a state any one person can clear. The one duty an Event raises is the invitation to organise it, standing on its invitee. Nothing is shared across Branch Management.
_Avoid_: opportunity (Save7 OS today's word for an open task card), shift, activity (an account's Activity band), RSVP (use sign up), Events Hub (briefly a hub of its own; an Event lives with its unit)
