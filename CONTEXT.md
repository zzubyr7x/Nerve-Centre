# Save7 OS

Internal ops platform for Save7, a student-run organisation operating across university branches. This glossary defines the roles, org units, and navigation concepts that gate access throughout the app.

## Language

**Save7 OS**:
The product — this platform, live at `os.save7.org`, commissioned by Save7's Exco from the user and Gilbert Lieb jointly. **One system, one name**, before and after the restructuring currently being designed: there is no second product, and nothing in this glossary describes a rival to it. Where a sentence needs to separate the system as it stands from the model being designed for it, that is a difference of **version, not of system** — say "Save7 OS today" and "the target model" rather than reaching for a second name.
_Avoid_: Save7 Operations OS, Save7OS, the OS, Gilbert's system, Nerve Centre (which names the effort, not the product)

**Nerve Centre**:
The planning effort that designs Save7 OS's organisational structure, and the repo it lives in — its wayfinding map, the decision tickets under it, and the spec they become. Names **work, never software**: Nerve Centre holds no application code, and nothing a volunteer signs into is ever called this. So "the Nerve Centre role model" means the model this effort designs, while "the Save7 OS role model" means the one the product carries. Named after the retired Coda nerve-centre document that hub content is drawn from; spelled British, like the rest of this glossary.
_Avoid_: Nerve Center, the Nerve Centre app / platform / system, a second Save7 OS

**Branch**:
A Save7 chapter at one university (e.g. UCT, Stellenbosch, Wits, UP, UJ), and the **entry point** through which a volunteer joins the organisation. A volunteer recruited at a branch works under that branch's team, and every project they take on stays under that branch's Branch Management regardless of where the project itself runs. A branch is not compulsory — a volunteer recruited outside any university has no branch (see Volunteer). Open-ended table managed by National Exco alone — added, edited, or deleted outright; no admin status confers this. Carries a separate active/inactive status reflecting current activity/momentum, not whether it still exists — an inactive branch is still fully editable, just flagged as needing a push to get moving again.
_Avoid_: location, chapter, campus

**National Exco**:
Org-wide leadership seats — CEO, COO, CGO (Chief Growth Officer — field of responsibility still TBD), CFO. Full access to every hub across every branch and department. Provides overall oversight, alignment, and accountability across both the Branch and Department systems. Authors Department Head MOUs directly; reviews and signs off on Branch-Manager-authored Project Lead MOUs — routed to them by the General Liaison. Super Admins by default and irrevocably. Exco alone assigns every seat in the organisation — Exco seats, General Liaison, Department Heads and Branch Management — grants or revokes Super Admin status, adds branches and departments, and is the only seat that can delete a volunteer account outright. Exco also hold every branch-level power across every branch, and every Project-level power across every Project: they edit any Project's eligibility, its active/inactive flag and the ladder it names, write to its log, and they alone remove a Project Lead where no successor is taking over, the MOU sign-off having been theirs. No one outside Exco, whatever admin tier they hold, can create or alter an Exco seat or override an Exco decision. The four seats hold the **same access** — each sees and does everything Exco can — and the same acts, save for a short list reserved to the CEO alone (see CEO). Where the CEO seat is vacant, those acts fall to the remaining Exco acting together; there is no second-in-line and no deputy. In the OKRs Hub, Exco alone writes the Mission & strategy and the Pillars, and may author an Objective onto any unit — national, any branch, any Department. Their weekly meeting is the one meeting the platform records (see Meeting); Exco read every Meeting and the whole Action Point tracker, may log a meeting themselves as backstop to the General Liaison, and may tick any Action Point done.
_Avoid_: leadership, management, executives, exec team, CMO (no longer an Exco seat — see Department Head)

**CEO**:
The National Exco seat carrying the greatest authority in the organisation. Holds the same access as the other three seats, plus a short list of acts that are the CEO's alone — an **open list**, added to as the model grows, presently: **approving a Resolution**; **returning an Advisory board member from Observing to Advising**; and **naming a successor** when stepping down. Everything else National Exco holds, all four seats hold equally — including assigning seats, granting Super Admin, adding branches and departments, deleting an account, and inviting an Advisory board member to a meeting. Most of what makes the CEO the CEO happens off the platform, and the model says so rather than manufacturing powers to justify the title.

**Does not leave by removal in the ordinary way.** A CEO **steps down** by Resolution — their own act, carrying their own approval — which may name their successor; naming one installs them, and reaches the CEO seat only, every other seat staying National Exco's collective appointment. A CEO who will not go is **removed** by a Resolution approved by the **remaining National Exco instead of by the CEO** — the single Resolution the CEO does not approve, and the only reason the seat has any involuntary exit at all. Where no successor was named, the remaining Exco appoint the next CEO by Resolution rather than by ordinary seat assignment, every movement of this seat being a Resolution matter.

Shown first and labelled wherever the Exco seats are listed, so that the authority is visible to everyone and not only to the person holding the buttons.
_Avoid_: chief executive, president, chair, head of Exco, managing director, boss

**Advisory board**:
Retained Exco capability, held by people who have served on National Exco. Not an honorary title and not a consulted body with rights of its own — an Advisory board member keeps working access to the organisation, in one of two states. **Advising**, the default, is full edit across every hub, every branch and every department — everything a sitting Exco member does day to day — minus the acts reserved to the sitting Exco: assigning seats, creating or altering an Exco seat, granting or revoking Super Admin, adding branches or departments, and deleting a volunteer account. Stepping down surrenders the power to decide who runs Save7 next; it surrenders nothing else. **Observing** is read everything, change nothing. Close to Super Admin in shape, but reached by seat rather than by admin status and so not identical to it — the Meetings section of the Compliance Hub is read by the advisory board and explicitly not by Super Admins. A member may step themselves down to Observing at any time; returning to Advising needs the CEO. Entry is never automatic — a Resolution places a retiring Exco member on the board, so an Exco member removed for non-performance does not reach it. Membership has no expiry but is not irrevocable: a Resolution removes it. Closed to anyone who has not served on National Exco. Members hold no standing place in Exco's meeting — any Exco member invites them and the General Liaison routes the invitation — but they read its archive: the record, not the room. They do not read the Action Point tracker (see Meeting, Action Point). Underneath the seat they remain a Volunteer with their original branch, like everyone else.
_Avoid_: board, board of directors, advisory council, emeritus, former Exco (names the history, not the seat), advisory role

**General Liaison**:
Single org-wide seat, distinct from National Exco. The routing point between Exco and everyone reporting up to it: Branch-Manager-authored Project Lead MOUs pass through them on the way to Exco sign-off, and Department Heads' interval updates and any Exco-discussion requests (including meeting invites) go through them too. Full hub access across every branch and department, same as National Exco, but doesn't author or sign off MOUs. May author an Objective or Key Result onto any unit — national, any branch, any Department — though this is no longer exceptional, OKR authorship having been opened to everyone holding a seat or Project Lead status; the seat's organisation-wide reach across every *hub* remains its own. Neither extends to the Mission & strategy or the Pillars, which are Exco's alone. Owns the lifecycle of volunteers who have no branch — acceptance, dormancy, deactivation, reactivation — since there is no Branch Management to hold it, with Exco as backstop, and helps a branchless volunteer with no Department author their Project Lead MOU. **Records each Project Lead MOU** the moment Exco sign it off — the platform record that gates Project creation, one Project per accepted MOU (see MOU), with Exco as backstop. **Keeps the record of National Exco's meetings** — logs the Meeting, writes its Agenda Points, raises the Action Points off them, and works the organisation-wide Action Point tracker; Exco can do all of this too, as backstop.
_Avoid_: secretary general, secretary, exco liaison

**Admin Dashboard**:
Where organisation-wide administrative work is done — seat assignment, the Branch and Department lists, the master volunteer tracker — rather than a section of the hub nav, mirroring the way a Volunteer has their own dashboard instead of hub access. Reached by National Exco and Super Admins.
_Avoid_: admin hub, admin panel, control panel, back office

**Super Admin**:
The only admin status there is — a flag layered on a seat, not a seat of its own, and never held by a Volunteer. Confers **read** access to every hub across every branch and department — with **one carve-out: the Meetings section of the Compliance Hub**, which a Super Admin does not read. Maintaining the site is not a reason to sit in on Exco's deliberations, so that surface is reached by seat (National Exco, the General Liaison, the advisory board) and never by admin status. It confers no edit rights, which still come from the underlying seat. The "Super" is measured against National Exco rather than a lesser admin: a Super Admin sees everything and changes nothing structural. One who is not Exco cannot grant or revoke Super Admin, assign any seat, add a branch or department, create or alter an Exco seat, or override an Exco decision. Exco hold it by default and irrevocably, and the Tech Department Head holds it by seat, since maintaining the site requires seeing everything; otherwise granted at Exco's discretion, in practice to seats already carrying organisation-wide responsibility such as the General Liaison. Organisation-wide account upkeep that no Branch Management covers falls here.
_Avoid_: admin, superuser, root, owner

**Branch Manager**:
Runs one branch. Edit access to their own branch across hubs; read-only access to other branches. Authors MOUs (responsibilities/expectations/deliverables) for that branch's Project Leads — routed through the General Liaison for Exco review and sign-off. Appointed by Exco. With the Operations Manager and Finance Manager, accepts new volunteers onto the platform and grants Project Lead status — powers that come with the seat itself, not from any admin status. Branch Management own their branch's volunteers for the whole of that lifecycle: reviewing dormancy, deactivating, and reactivating. Deleting an account outright is Exco's alone. Any one of the three, acting alone, may author an Objective or a Key Result — no quorum, and not confined to their own branch, since OKR authorship is the one thing in the model that is not unit-scoped.

**Operations Manager**:
Runs one branch's operations team. Branch-scoped, same edit-own/read-others pattern as Branch Manager. Appointed by Exco; shares Branch Management's powers over their branch's volunteers — see Branch Manager. Oversees all volunteers in the branch, all Project Leads, and new-volunteer onboarding; along with the Branch Manager and COO, funnels volunteers with a specialised interest toward the matching Department.

**Finance Manager**:
Runs one branch's finance function. Branch-scoped, same edit-own/read-others pattern as Branch Manager and Operations Manager. Reports directly to the CFO — there is no national Finance Department standing between them. Appointed by Exco; shares Branch Management's powers over their branch's volunteers — see Branch Manager.
_Avoid_: branch function head, finance head, department head, branch lead

**Branch Management**:
The three branch seats **collectively** — Branch Manager, Operations Manager, Finance Manager — one set per branch. Not a seat of its own and not a fourth appointment: the name for the three acting as the branch's leadership, which is how the model refers to them whenever the point is the branch owning its people rather than one manager's particular remit. They accept volunteers, grant Project Lead status, and own their branch's volunteers through dormancy, deactivation and reactivation. Addressable as a unit — an Action Point may be assigned to a branch's Branch Management, which lands on all three.
_Avoid_: branch trio (the previous term, renamed), trio, branch leadership, branch exec

**Department**:
A national/organisation-level portfolio for specialised work: Media, Tech, Research, Education. Open-ended list managed by National Exco alone, extendable the same way Branch is. A **second supervisory axis**, not a label: joining a Department places a volunteer's work in that portfolio under its Department Head, while their branch's Branch Management continues to own them as a person. Distinct axis from the Branch system — operates directly under Exco, not under a Branch Manager. A volunteer may belong to several Departments at once, one interest not excluding another; each Head's authority reaches only their own portfolio's work, so two Heads never contend over the same task. Finance is not a Department — see Finance Manager.
_Avoid_: hub (a Department is an org unit; Hub is a nav section — a Department typically owns a Hub, but they aren't the same concept)

**Department Head**:
Owns, grows, and organises one Department at national scale. MOU authored directly by Exco (no Branch Manager in the loop). Gives Exco in-person or online updates at set intervals, and routes any Exco-discussion requests through the General Liaison. Becomes the national owner of the existing Hub matching their portfolio where one already exists (e.g. the Media Department Head owns the Media Hub — this absorbed the former CMO/Marketing remit, which is no longer a separate Exco seat or department). Holds real authority over their Department's work: sees what its members are doing, assigns it, and has a say in it; onboards them with the tools the portfolio needs, advises them, and mobilises them. That authority stops at the work — **the Department Head owns the work, Branch Management owns the person**, so acceptance onto the platform, dormancy review, deactivation and reactivation are never theirs. They may mark a member inactive *within the Department*, which is a per-Department status and touches nothing about the account. Authors Objectives and Key Results for any unit rather than only their own Department, OKR authorship being the one thing in the model that is not unit-scoped; the Pillars remain Exco's to add or edit. One Head per Department and one Department per Head. Where a branchless volunteer in their Department seeks Project Lead status, the Head works the MOU up with them and passes it to the General Liaison. The Tech Department Head additionally carries Super Admin by seat.
_Avoid_: finance manager, branch lead

**Project Lead**:
Not a distinct account type — a status/upgrade on a Volunteer, granted via an MOU authored at the lowest supervising level above them and signed off by Exco. Owns the resulting Project. A **standing** status, not a time-boxed one: a Project does not end, so neither does leading one. Held **once per accepted MOU** — a person holding three signed-off MOUs may create three Projects and no more, which is what the MOU record on the platform exists to enforce (see MOU). Sees the volunteers working under them and their assigned tasks; admits, invites and removes them (see Project Team); edits the Project's eligibility, its active/inactive flag and the Pillars, Objectives and Key Results it names; and keeps its log of Updates. Joining a Project Team confers nothing — only an accepted MOU makes a Project Lead. **Ends only two ways:** a successor signs an MOU for the Project — which may be the same MOU the outgoing Lead hands over, run through the normal route — or **National Exco remove the Lead outright**, the sign-off having been theirs. Branch Management, who author the MOU, do not revoke the status. Carries **OKR authorship**: writes Objectives and Key Results for any unit, on the same terms as Branch Management and Department Heads — the one place the status reaches outside its own Project — held **while the status is held and not a moment after**, so leading a Project once does not confer organisation-wide OKR write for life. A Project flagged inactive changes none of this: the flag reports momentum and carries no permission, or nobody would ever set it.

**Project**:
An initiative owned by a Project Lead, born when a volunteer's idea is written up as an MOU and signed off by Exco. **A Project does not end** — there is no completion, no closing and no archive; one that has run its course is flagged **inactive**, the same active/inactive momentum signal a Branch carries, set by its Lead or by National Exco and saying nothing about whether the Project exists. Its **home is its Lead's branch**, read off the Lead rather than chosen, and null where the Lead is branchless. It attaches to no Department: a Department Head already owns their members' work wherever that work happens, and a tag would add a second claimant without adding a power.

**Created by its Lead**, through a form on the platform, once the MOU behind it is signed off and recorded — one Project per accepted MOU. The form sets a name, what the Project is for, the MOU it comes from, and its **eligibility**: the set of branches whose volunteers may join, plus whether branchless volunteers may. One branch in the set is a branch-scoped Project; every branch is an open one. KPIs and targets are not set here — they live on the MOU, and tracking them is the work of a project tracker.

**Names the ladder it serves** — any number of Pillars, Objectives and Key Results, all optional, and a Project that names none is unremarkable. Set on the creation form and changed at any time by its Lead, with National Exco as backstop. The choice **cascades**: naming a Key Result carries in its Objective and that Objective's Pillar, naming an Objective carries in its Pillar. Because these are sets rather than single slots, naming a Pillar alongside an Objective standing on a different one is not a contradiction — it is a Project serving two things. The link is **inert**: it moves no progress bar, since an Objective's figure stays the derived mean of its Key Results, and it confers nothing on the Key Results named — no ownership, no acceptance, no duty. Deleting a named Objective or Key Result, or retiring a named Pillar, simply drops it from the set; a Project pointing at something never blocks its deletion, and the Project shows as unlinked, which everyone can see. **The same commitment may legitimately appear twice** — as a Key Result on the ladder and as a KPI in the project tracker — and the link is what makes the duplication visible rather than what prevents it: the two answer to different documents, the ladder and the MOU.

Carries a log of **Updates**, kept by its Lead.

Every Project is visible to every member of the organisation, whether or not they may join it: eligibility gates joining, never seeing.
_Avoid_: programme, campaign, initiative (use for the idea before it is signed off, not the Project), time-boxed (a Project has no end)

**Project Team**:
The volunteers helping on a Project, plus the Project Lead who owns it. Team members remain ordinary volunteers under their own Branch Management; sitting on a team grants no status and no authority.

**The Project Lead is the only gate.** An eligible volunteer asks to join and the Lead approves — their own Branch Management has no say, since the branch owns the person and the Lead owns the work. A request **never lapses**: it stands as a duty on the Lead's Dashboard until answered, because a request that quietly expires lets a Lead refuse someone without ever saying no. The Lead may also **invite** a volunteer, who must **accept** — being added to a team silently creates an expectation of work nobody agreed to. There is **no cap** on a team's size or on how many join from one branch; the Lead's approval already does that job.

A member may **leave** at any time and the Lead may **remove** one — deliberately unlike a Key Result's owners, who have no exit, because membership grants nothing there is any reason to make sticky. Narrowing a Project's eligibility **does not eject** anyone already on the team: the set governs new joins, and removing someone is a named act, not a side effect of editing a list.
_Avoid_: project members, crew, squad

**MOU**:
An offline, signed agreement between a role and its supervising structure (Branch Manager ↔ Project Lead, Exco ↔ Department Head) setting responsibilities, expectations, deliverables, and KPIs/targets.

A Project Lead MOU is authored at the **lowest supervising level above the applicant** and routed upward through the General Liaison to Exco for sign-off; where the applicant already sits at or above a level, that level is skipped. So: a branch-onboarded volunteer's MOU is authored with their **home** Branch Manager whatever the project's reach, and Department membership does not divert it; a branchless volunteer in a Department works it up with that Department Head; a branchless volunteer in no Department works it up with the General Liaison; the General Liaison authors their own and goes straight to Exco; an Exco member authors their own and presents it to the rest of Exco. Not a UI workflow — referenced in the OKRs hub (KPI/target display only) and uploaded to the Compliance Hub for record-keeping.

The platform nonetheless holds a **record** of each signed-off Project Lead MOU — who holds it, and when Exco signed it off — entered by the **General Liaison**, with Exco as backstop, at the moment of sign-off. The record is not the document and does not run the drafting, the review or the sign-off, all of which stay offline; it exists because a Project is created from an accepted MOU and one MOU makes exactly one Project. KPIs and targets stay on the document, not in the record.

**Resolution**:
A formal decision of National Exco, recorded in the Compliance Hub, carrying the **CEO's approval** — with exactly one exception: a Resolution **removing the CEO** is approved by the remaining Exco instead, since a CEO whose own approval were required could never be removed. It is the instrument that moves the seats at the top of the organisation: removing an Exco member, placing a retiring one on the Advisory board or taking them off it, a CEO's own stepping down (which may name a successor), removing a CEO who will not step down, and appointing the next CEO where no successor was named. Unlike an MOU, which is offline and describes a working relationship, a Resolution is held on the platform and changes who holds what.
_Avoid_: motion, vote, minutes, decision (too general — a Resolution is the recorded instrument, not any decision taken)

**Volunteer**:
The base layer every account holds. There is one door into Save7 OS (below), so everyone with an account is a Volunteer and every seat and status layers on top of it — Branch Management is a Volunteer holding that seat, and Project Lead and Super Admin layer the same way. Recruited at one branch, or at none. A volunteer normally enters through a branch and may transfer to another if they change university; a volunteer recruited outside any university has no branch at all — a small population in a student-driven organisation, owned by the General Liaison instead of Branch Management, and following the same paths into Departments and Projects as anyone else. Has a **Dashboard** — the same surface every member of the organisation has, not a consolation for holding no seat — plus the **OKRs Hub** and the **Projects Hub** — the two hubs open to every member of the organisation, so that the ladder from day-to-day work up to the mission is visible to the people doing that work, and so that every Project is visible to everyone who might join one. No other hub appears in their nav. **Owning a Key Result is the one write a plain Volunteer holds anywhere in the model** — its progress, their own acceptance of it, its health, its done tick, its Updates and its supporting units, on that Key Result alone; everywhere else, write comes from a seat. Can simultaneously belong to a branch, be a member of one or more Departments, sit on one or more Project Teams, and hold Project Lead status for one or more initiatives — these are not mutually exclusive. Accounts are not created on the platform: a prospective volunteer signs up and qualifies at learn.save7.org, receives a Save7 email address, and is then *accepted* onto the platform by their branch's Branch Management. Graduating does not end volunteering — accounts persist, and deletion is a genuine last resort rather than routine cleanup, reserved to Exco: an account costs a completed course and a Save7 email address to obtain, so destroying one is not a branch-level act. **Contribution is recorded as work owned and done, never as time spent** — nothing on the platform measures duration, and no hours are logged, verified or counted anywhere in the model. What a volunteer has contributed is legible from the Key Results they own, the Projects they sit on and the Action Points they close; proof of that contribution to somebody outside Save7 is written by a person, offline.

**Hub**:
A named, role-gated section of the site's navigation (OKRs, Projects, Finance, Compliance, Media, Operations, Branch Managers, Departments — the last with a sub-hub per department). **Where a hub sits on screen is deliberately not part of the model** — top nav, sidebar group or anything else is a rendering decision, left to the work that builds the Shell. What the model fixes is which hubs exist, who may open each, and that the nav is **one level deep** everywhere except Departments: a hub's internal pages — the Meetings section of the Compliance Hub, for instance — are pages within it, not hubs and not sub-hubs. A hub is hidden entirely from a role's nav if that role can't access it, rather than shown disabled. A Dashboard is not a hub either. **Two hubs are the exception to that gating** — the **OKRs Hub**, since a ladder nobody can see fails at its only job, and the **Projects Hub**, since every member is a potential member of every Project open to them and a directory that hides what you cannot join cannot tell you what Save7 is running. Both are open to every member of the organisation, Volunteers included. What a member may *edit* there still comes from their seat, with one exception: a Volunteer made an owner of a Key Result sets its progress, their own acceptance of it, its health, its done tick, its Updates and its supporting units.
_Avoid_: top nav, sidebar section, tab (each names a placement the model does not fix)

**Shell**:
The frame around whatever page a person is looking at — the navigation, and the chrome that says who they are and what they can reach. **There is one Shell, and every person gets it**: what appears in it is assembled from the seats and statuses that person holds, so someone holding several seats sees the union of what those seats reach, and a plain Volunteer sees their Dashboard, the OKRs Hub and the Projects Hub. There is no second frame chosen at sign-in, and **navigating never changes what a person is** — opening a page shows that page and nothing else. This is the one thing the model requires of the Shell; everything else about it, placement included, belongs to the work that builds it.
_Avoid_: admin view, member view, the admin half / member half (the model has no halves)

**Season**:
A chapter of Save7's mission and strategy, declared by National Exco and written by them as free-form prose. Has **no fixed length** — not a calendar year, an academic year, or a cycle of any set duration; a new season begins when Exco decides there is a new one to write. Scopes the mission & strategy write-up **and nothing else**: Pillars, Objectives and Key Results carry no season and never roll over, so there is no archiving, carry-forward or closing-off anywhere beneath it. Seasons accumulate rather than overwrite and all stay visible, so that any member can see how the organisation's mission has changed over time; the latest is marked current.
_Avoid_: cycle, term, year, phase, quarter, sprint

**Pillar**:
An organisation-wide focus area defined by National Exco from the mission and strategy — standing examples: Public awareness, Help hospital systems, Help patients, Nimble operations. A **theme, not a commitment**: a Pillar has no owner, no Key Results and no progress of its own, and exists so that every Objective can be seen to serve something. Several stand at a time. Standing, not seasonal — revised in place rather than rewritten each Season. Retiring one **archives** it: removed from the list new Objectives may choose from, still displayed for Objectives already tagged to it. **Displays the Projects serving it**, rolled up from every rung beneath — the one piece of content a Pillar carries, and a directory rather than a commitment, so it remains true that a Pillar has no Key Results and no progress of its own.
_Avoid_: theme, focus area, goal, category, strategic priority

**Objective**:
A unit's commitment, standing on exactly one Pillar — the tag is mandatory, and an Objective that fits no Pillar means Exco needs a new Pillar, not that it should go untagged. Belongs to a **branch**, a **Department**, or the **nation**; the three are structurally identical and differ only in what they hang off. Belongs to a unit, never to a person, so **no Objective has an owner**: it displays **who created it**, and accountability sits one layer down, on its Key Results' owners. A branchless volunteer needs no Objective of their own — they reach work by being made an owner of a Key Result. Written by anyone holding a seat or Project Lead status, **for any unit** — authorship is not confined to the unit you supervise, which makes the OKRs Hub the one place the edit-own/read-others ceiling does not apply, with the creator stamp carrying the accountability that scoping would otherwise enforce. Plain Volunteers do not write Objectives. **Bigger than any one champion**, which is what separates it from a Project, born bottom-up from one volunteer's MOU. Carries a **progress** bar of its own — the unweighted mean of its Key Results' progress, **derived and uneditable**, because an Objective has no owner to set it. Unowned Key Results count at zero, so an Objective whose work nobody has taken reads low however well its owned Key Results are going; that sag is the point, not a defect, since it puts the organisation's work-discovery signal one layer up where the people who can act on it are looking. Carries **no health status**: health is a reading an owner makes, and an Objective has none. **Displays the Projects serving it** — those that named the Objective itself and those that named any of its Key Results, which roll up. The list is a directory and nothing more: a Project named here changes no figure and owes the Objective nothing.
_Avoid_: goal, OKR, initiative, target, project

**Key Result**:
One of the several commitments that move an Objective's needle; the Objective is met when its Key Results are. Written by the same people who write Objectives — anyone holding a seat or Project Lead status — who then attach its **owners** from a drop-down of anyone in the organisation, themselves included. Owners are a **flat set**: one is usual, several are allowed, none is primary, and there is no co-owner concept. A Key Result may also sit **unowned**, displayed as such, which is how work advertises that it needs picking up; an unowned one may be taken up by the person themselves. Carries a **progress** bar, an **acceptance** per owner, a **health** status, an optional **target date**, a **done** tick, a log of **Updates**, and its **supporting units**.

**Progress** is one figure from 0 to 100 that an owner slides. It is a **claim, not a measurement**: nothing computes it and nothing can contradict it, which is what makes the latest updates the evidence behind it rather than a diary beside it. Key Results differ wildly in kind — a plan either written or not, a session held twice a month, a satisfaction percentage held above a threshold — and one sliding claim is the only figure that fits all of them and stays comparable across them.

**Acceptance** is the handshake between one owner and the work. It is held **per owner**, never per Key Result, because it cannot be given on anybody's behalf: attaching a new owner to a Key Result others have accepted leaves that person unaccepted, and the Key Result displays how many of its owners have accepted. There is **no decline** — not accepting is the decline, visible as such, and only someone holding a seat or Project Lead status can remove an owner. Nothing times out.

**Health** is one of *On track*, *At risk*, *Off track* or *Paused*, shared across every owner with the last write standing; two owners who disagree settle it in the latest updates, not in the schema. *Paused* suspends the target date.

A **target date** is optional — the standing commitments that never end take none, and none is imposed by a Season, which scopes nothing below the mission write-up. A Key Result whose date has passed shows **overdue, in red**, on its owners' Dashboards, exactly as an Action Point does, and nothing carries it forward. The **done** tick is what clears the red: set by an owner, it puts progress at 100 and takes the Key Result off the Dashboard while leaving it on the ladder. There is deliberately **no *Missed*** — an owner must not be able to clear their own red by declaring failure — so a Key Result that was genuinely missed stays red until somebody holding a seat rewords, redates or deletes it.

An **unowned** Key Result has no acceptance and no health, its bar is pinned at zero and immovable, and it can never be ticked done. Where it carries a target date that passes, the red falls to whoever created it.

An owner sets exactly those six things — progress, their own acceptance, health, the done tick, the Updates and the **supporting units** — and may not reword, reassign or delete the Key Result, nor remove themselves as an owner; all of which belong to anyone holding a seat or Project Lead status, **none of whom may set the first five**. Supporting units are the one field both sides write, because the person who discovers that help is needed is the one doing the work, and a tag that obliges nobody hands an owner nothing over the unit named. Nobody overrules an owner's reading: a Key Result claiming *On track* with no recent update is visibly stale, and staleness is a better signal than a drop-down two people are fighting over.

A Key Result that **cannot move until another unit acts** is said in the fields that already exist — health *At risk*, that unit named in supporting units, and a dated Update saying what is stuck. There is deliberately **no *Blocked*** health value: recording that a unit is blocking something raises the question of what that unit owes and when it clears, which is precisely the obligation supporting units do not carry — and since *Paused* stops the clock, a *Blocked* that did the same would let an owner clear their own red by pointing at somebody else.

A Key Result displays the Projects that have named it; those Projects also appear on its Objective and on that Objective's Pillar.
_Avoid_: task, action point (discrete and binary with a mandatory date, where a Key Result is a standing commitment carrying progress with an optional one — the dates do not distinguish them, the progress does), KPI, target, milestone

**Supporting Units**:
The parts of the organisation whose help a Key Result needs — **never the people doing the work**, who are its owners. A **readout, not a claim**: naming a unit obliges it to nothing, queues nothing, creates no request to accept or decline, and puts nothing on anybody's Dashboard. The model had already settled that a unit cannot owe anything — an Action Point's owners are people, or a branch's Branch Management, while Departments and National Exco are explicitly not assignable as groups — and a Department Head's authority over their members' work is not by itself a reason for them to owe something here (see Duty). Where the answer really is *"Tech should do this"*, the model already has the instrument: attach someone from Tech as an **owner**.

Optional and multi-select, drawn from: each **Department**, each **branch**, each branch's **Branch Management** as an entry of its own, and **National Exco**. A branch and its Branch Management are separate entries because needing a branch's volunteers and needing its three managers are different asks. **Not** in the list: individuals (that is what owners are), a Department Head separately from their Department (the Head is already who the Department reaches), the **Advisory board** (retained working capability, not a body with rights of its own to be leaned on), and a **Project** — a Project already names the ladder it serves, under its Lead's control, and a tag pointing the other way would give one pair of things two overlapping links.

Set by whoever wrote the Key Result **and by its owners**. Displayed on the Key Result and available as a **filter in the OKRs Hub, open to every member**, read there being flat; it does not roll up to the Objective. A tagged unit is never told — it finds out by looking — so the field records who the work leans on rather than starting anything.
_Avoid_: team (Save7 OS already uses it for a branch, and a Project Team is people), team tag, stakeholder, collaborator, assignee, contributor

**Update**:
One dated entry in a log — a **title**, a **date** and a body of free text. The title is always present, even when it says no more than *General check-in*, so that a log renders one way rather than two; a notable entry titles itself accordingly.

**One mechanism, used in two places.** A Key Result carries a log of Updates written by its **owners**; a Project carries one written by its **Lead**, with National Exco as backstop. A Project Team member does not write to a Project's log — sitting on a team grants no authority, and a log anyone may post to is a discussion thread rather than a record; a team member's own work surfaces through the Key Results they own.

**The weight differs with what it hangs off, and deliberately so.** On a Key Result, progress is a claim nothing computes and nothing can contradict, which makes the Updates the **only evidence** behind the bar. A Project has no progress bar, so its log is a **record** — what happened and when — carrying no evidential load.
_Avoid_: check-in, note, comment, diary, latest updates (the log is of Updates; "latest updates" names the display, not the thing)

**Dashboard**:
The surface a person lands on when they sign in — **everyone has one**, whatever they hold, and it is the control centre for that account. Shows what is theirs: their open and overdue Action Points, **every Key Result they own**, the work assigned to them, who they are across the organisation (branch, Departments, Project Teams, Project Lead statuses), what they must act on by virtue of a seat, **volunteers waiting to join a Project they lead**, and announcements from Exco. **Per seat**, so what appears on it follows the person's duties — a Volunteer's and an Exco member's are the same surface carrying different things. Distinct from a Hub, which is organisational and shared; a Dashboard is personal. An Action Point is ticked done from here by its owner, as is a Key Result. For many plain Volunteers the Key Results they own will be the only thing on the surface, which is the same reasoning that opened the OKRs Hub to them.
_Avoid_: home page, my work, personal hub, landing page (it is the landing page, but the term names the surface)

**Duty**:
Anything a person owes — the single word for what a Dashboard lists, covering an Action Point raised on someone, work assigned to them on a Project, and an obligation falling to them by virtue of a seat. Two kinds. A **raised duty** is a stored record with owners and one done status, ticked off by an owner; Action Points, assigned Project work and **an owned Key Result** are raised — a Key Result carrying a target date goes overdue in red exactly as an Action Point does, and its done tick is what clears it. A **standing duty** is not stored and not addressed to anyone — it is computed from a state somebody is responsible for, so it appears wherever that responsibility sits, disappears when the state does, and has nothing to tick. It **aggregates**: a branch with three volunteers awaiting acceptance produces one duty, not three, and a Project Lead with three volunteers waiting to join produces one duty, not three — the same shape, one level down. Whoever creates a Key Result carries two of them — owners who have not yet accepted, and Key Results left unowned past their target date — kept separate rather than merged, because one asks them to chase a person and the other to find one. That is also why joint responsibility needs no machinery — Branch Management's three seats each see the same standing duty because the state is theirs collectively, any one of them acting clears it for all three, and no assignment, claiming or division of labour is required. A duty sits with the **lowest level that can act on it**, being explicitly named overrides, and holding the authority to do something is never by itself a reason to owe it. Vocabulary of the model, not of the interface: nothing on screen says "duty".
_Avoid_: obligation, commitment, responsibility, to-do, item, task (assigned Project work is one kind of duty, not the word for all of them)

**Meeting**:
A recorded sitting of **National Exco**, the only meeting the platform keeps — weekly, though nothing depends on the cadence. Carries a title, a date and **attendance**, and holds Agenda Points. Attendance may include **guests** from elsewhere in the organisation, attached by Exco or the General Liaison; being named on attendance grants a guest no access to anything — not the minutes, not the agenda, not the record. Logged by the General Liaison, with Exco as backstop, and kept in the **Meetings section of the Compliance Hub**. Read by National Exco, the General Liaison and the advisory board; explicitly **not** by Super Admins. Branch, Department and Project meetings are not modelled.
_Avoid_: minutes (a Meeting holds them rather than being them), session, sitting

**Agenda Point**:
One item of business at a Meeting — a heading plus a free-text note of what was discussed or decided. Several per Meeting, each separate. Holds zero or more Action Points; an Agenda Point that produces none is ordinary, since plenty is settled in the room.
_Avoid_: agenda item, topic, minute

**Action Point**:
A piece of work somebody owes, with **owners**, a **due date**, and one **done** status. Owners are one person, several people, or a branch's **Branch Management**, which resolves to its three members at the moment of assignment. Shown **overdue**, in red, once the due date passes; nothing carries it forward and nothing copies it — it stays outstanding until it is done. Ticked done by an owner from their Dashboard, or by the General Liaison or any Exco member from the tracker, and the change reads the same everywhere at once. Reaches its owners on their Dashboard wherever they sit in the organisation, including people who may read nothing else about where it came from; whether an owner outside Exco sees the Agenda Point behind it is a per-Action-Point choice made by whoever raised it. **Has a source but does not require one** — a Meeting's Agenda Point is one source and a key result is expected to be another; an Action Point may stand alone.
_Avoid_: task (work assigned on a project board is a different thing), to-do, follow-up, item
