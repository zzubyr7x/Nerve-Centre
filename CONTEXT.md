# Save7 OS

Internal ops platform for Save7, a student-run organisation operating across university branches. This glossary defines the roles, org units, and navigation concepts that gate access throughout the app.

## Language

**Save7 OS**:
The product — this platform, live at `os.save7.org`, commissioned by Save7's Exco from the user and Gilbert Lieb jointly. **One system, one name**, before and after the restructuring currently being designed: there is no second product, and nothing in this glossary describes a rival to it. Where a sentence needs to separate the system as it stands from the model being designed for it, that is a difference of **version, not of system** — say "Save7 OS today" and "the target model" rather than reaching for a second name.
_Avoid_: Save7 Operations OS, Save7OS, the OS, Gilbert's system, Nerve Centre (which names the effort, not the product)

**Nerve Centre**:
The planning effort that designs Save7 OS's organisational structure, and the repo it lives in — its wayfinding map, the decision tickets under it, and the spec they become. Names **work, never software**: Nerve Centre holds no application code, and nothing a volunteer signs into is ever called this. So "the Nerve Centre role model" means the model this effort designs, while "the Save7 OS role model" means the one the product carries. Named after the retired Coda nerve-centre document that hub content is drawn from; spelled British, like the rest of this glossary.
_Avoid_: Nerve Center, the Nerve Centre app / platform / system, a second Save7 OS

**Branch**:
A Save7 chapter at one university (e.g. UCT, Stellenbosch, Wits, UP, UJ), and the **entry point** through which a volunteer joins the organisation. A volunteer recruited at a branch works under that branch's team, and every project they take on stays under that branch's Branch Management regardless of where the project itself runs. A branch is not compulsory — a volunteer recruited outside any university has no branch (see Volunteer). Open-ended table managed by National Exco alone — added, edited, or deleted outright, on the Operations Hub's Branches page; no admin status confers this. Carries a separate active/inactive status reflecting current activity/momentum, not whether it still exists — an inactive branch is still fully editable, just flagged as needing a push to get moving again.
_Avoid_: location, chapter, campus

**National Exco**:
Org-wide leadership seats — CEO, COO, CGO (Chief Growth Officer — field of responsibility still TBD), CFO. Full access to every hub across every branch and department. Provides overall oversight, alignment, and accountability across both the Branch and Department systems. Authors Department Head MOUs directly; reviews and signs off on Branch-Manager-authored Project Lead MOUs — routed to them by the General Liaison. Super Admins by default and irrevocably. Exco alone assigns every seat in the organisation — Exco seats, General Liaison, Department Heads and Branch Management — grants or revokes Super Admin status, adds branches and departments, and is the only seat that can delete a volunteer account outright — all of it worked from the **Users** hub, which Exco open with the General Liaison and nobody else. Exco also hold every branch-level power across every branch, and every Project-level power across every Project: they edit any Project's eligibility, its status, its overview, its project tracker and the ladder it names, write to its log, and they alone remove a Project Lead where no successor is taking over, the MOU sign-off having been theirs. No one outside Exco, whatever admin tier they hold, can create or alter an Exco seat or override an Exco decision. The four seats hold the **same access** — each sees and does everything Exco can — and the same acts, save for a short list reserved to the CEO alone (see CEO) and **editing the Operations Hub's own content**, which the CEO and the COO hold with the General Liaison while the CFO and CGO read it (see Operations Hub). **The Finance Hub is not divided that way**: all four seats post to the ledger, mark Claims paid, record Section 18A certificates, set Budgets and keep its settings alike, and what makes it the CFO's is that its national duties name the CFO (see CFO). Where the CEO seat is vacant, those acts fall to the remaining Exco acting together; there is no second-in-line and no deputy. In the OKRs Hub, Exco alone writes the Mission & vision, the Strategy and the Pillars, and may author an Objective onto any unit — national, any branch, any Department. Their weekly meeting is the one meeting the platform records (see Meeting); Exco read every Meeting and the whole Action Point tracker, may log a meeting themselves as backstop to the General Liaison, and may tick any Action Point done.
_Avoid_: leadership, management, executives, exec team, CMO (no longer an Exco seat — see Department Head)

**CEO**:
The National Exco seat carrying the greatest authority in the organisation. Holds the same access as the other three seats, plus a short list of acts that are the CEO's alone — an **open list**, added to as the model grows, presently: **approving a Resolution**; **returning an Advisory board member from Observing to Advising**; and **naming a successor** when stepping down. One more act the CEO holds without holding it alone: **editing the Operations Hub's own content**, shared with the COO and the General Liaison and not held by the CFO or CGO. Everything else National Exco holds, all four seats hold equally — including assigning seats, granting Super Admin, adding branches and departments, deleting an account, and inviting an Advisory board member to a meeting. Most of what makes the CEO the CEO happens off the platform, and the model says so rather than manufacturing powers to justify the title.

**Does not leave by removal in the ordinary way.** A CEO **steps down** by Resolution — their own act, carrying their own approval — which may name their successor; naming one installs them, and reaches the CEO seat only, every other seat staying National Exco's collective appointment. A CEO who will not go is **removed** by a Resolution approved by the **remaining National Exco instead of by the CEO** — the single Resolution the CEO does not approve, and the only reason the seat has any involuntary exit at all. Where no successor was named, the remaining Exco appoint the next CEO by Resolution rather than by ordinary seat assignment, every movement of this seat being a Resolution matter.

Shown first and labelled wherever the Exco seats are listed, so that the authority is visible to everyone and not only to the person holding the buttons.
_Avoid_: chief executive, president, chair, head of Exco, managing director, boss

**COO**:
The National Exco seat the **Operations Managers report to** — the operations line runs from each branch's Operations Manager to the COO, as the finance line runs from each Finance Manager to the CFO — and the owner of the **Operations Hub**. Holds the same access as the other three Exco seats, and one act the CFO and CGO do not: **editing the Operations Hub's own content**, which the COO shares with the CEO and the General Liaison. Beyond that, what the seat adds is that **national operations duties sit with the COO by name** — approving what Operations Managers submit, chasing an Agreement past its renewal date — rather than with National Exco as a whole, since a duty sits with whoever is explicitly named and otherwise with the lowest level that can act on it — and holding Exco's authority over operations is not by itself a reason for all four seats to owe it. Along with the Branch Manager and the Operations Manager, funnels volunteers with a specialised interest toward the matching Department.
_Avoid_: head of operations, operations admin (Save7 OS today's job title for an account carrying finance permissions, not this seat)

**CFO**:
The National Exco seat the **Finance Managers report to** — the finance line runs from each branch's Finance Manager to the CFO with no national Finance Department between them — and the owner of the **Finance Hub**. Holds **no finance permission the other three Exco seats lack**: all four write there alike. What the seat adds is that **national finance duties sit with the CFO by name** — Claims approved and awaiting payment, Claims a Finance Manager cannot decide (their own, and those with no branch), Section 18A certificates outstanding, a Budget period lapsed with no replacement, and the budget plan of a Project with no home branch — rather than with National Exco as a whole, holding Exco's authority over money being no reason for all four to owe it. Whether the CFO is a **co-signatory** is a matter of the bank mandate, not of the seat (see Co-signatory).
_Avoid_: finance admin (Save7 OS today's role carrying every finance write, which no seat in the model inherits whole), treasurer, head of finance

**Advisory board**:
Retained Exco capability, held by people who have served on National Exco. Not an honorary title and not a consulted body with rights of its own — an Advisory board member keeps working access to the organisation, in one of two states. **Advising**, the default, is full edit across every hub, every branch and every department — everything a sitting Exco member does day to day — minus the acts reserved to the sitting Exco: assigning seats, creating or altering an Exco seat, granting or revoking Super Admin, adding branches or departments, and deleting a volunteer account. Like the CFO and CGO, an Advising member reads the Operations Hub's own content rather than editing it. Stepping down surrenders the power to decide who runs Save7 next; it surrenders nothing else. **Observing** is read everything, change nothing. Neither state opens the **Users** hub, where access to Save7 OS is decided — that is the sitting Exco's and the General Liaison's. Close to Super Admin in shape, but reached by seat rather than by admin status and so not identical to it — the Meetings page of the Operations Hub's Compliance section is read by the advisory board and explicitly not by Super Admins. A member may step themselves down to Observing at any time; returning to Advising needs the CEO. Entry is never automatic — a Resolution places a retiring Exco member on the board, so an Exco member removed for non-performance does not reach it. Membership has no expiry but is not irrevocable: a Resolution removes it. Closed to anyone who has not served on National Exco. Members hold no standing place in Exco's meeting — any Exco member invites them and the General Liaison routes the invitation — but they read its archive: the record, not the room. They do not read the Action Point tracker (see Meeting, Action Point). Underneath the seat they remain a Volunteer with their original branch, like everyone else.
_Avoid_: board, board of directors, advisory council, emeritus, former Exco (names the history, not the seat), advisory role

**General Liaison**:
Single org-wide seat, distinct from National Exco. The routing point between Exco and everyone reporting up to it: Branch-Manager-authored Project Lead MOUs pass through them on the way to Exco sign-off, and Department Heads' interval updates and any Exco-discussion requests (including meeting invites) go through them too. Full hub access across every branch and department, same as National Exco, but doesn't author or sign off MOUs — and **reads the Finance Hub without writing to it**, no money routing through the seat. May author an Objective or Key Result onto any unit — national, any branch, any Department — though this is no longer exceptional, OKR authorship having been opened to everyone holding a seat or Project Lead status; the seat's organisation-wide reach across every *hub* remains its own. Neither extends to the Mission & vision, the Strategy or the Pillars, which are Exco's alone. Owns the lifecycle of volunteers who have no branch — acceptance, dormancy, deactivation, reactivation — since there is no Branch Management to hold it, with Exco as backstop, and helps a branchless volunteer with no Department author their Project Lead MOU. **Records each Project Lead MOU** the moment Exco sign it off — the platform record that gates Project creation, one Project per accepted MOU (see MOU), with Exco as backstop — on the MOUs page of the Operations Hub. **Edits the Operations Hub's own content** alongside the COO and the CEO, the two seats working closely enough that the General Liaison was given the COO's edit there. **Keeps the record of National Exco's meetings** — logs the Meeting, writes its Agenda Points, raises the Action Points off them, and works the organisation-wide Action Point tracker; Exco can do all of this too, as backstop. **Opens the Users hub** with National Exco: invites people in without the course, may answer any access request and answers every branchless one — but assigns no seat, grants no Super Admin and deletes no account.
_Avoid_: secretary general, secretary, exco liaison

**Users**:
The hub where **access to Save7 OS** is granted, changed and taken away, opened by **National Exco and the General Liaison** and nobody else — not Super Admins, not the Advisory board, not Branch Management. It holds **User approval**: one page in four tabs — **Pending**, every **access request** not yet answered; **Approved**, everyone who is in, with their basic details and the **switches** on their account; **Rejected**; and **Deleted** — with a search across all of them. From here National Exco and the General Liaison **invite** someone in without the course (see Volunteer) and may answer any access request in the country, though a branch's own are its Branch Management's to answer, from Branch Centre, and a branchless person's the General Liaison's. **The switches** on an approved account are five, each flipped only by its owner: **seats**, **Super Admin** and **delete**, National Exco's alone; **active / deactivated**, the person's Branch Management's, or the General Liaison's where they have no branch, with Exco on anyone; and **branch**, moving someone when they change university, Exco's and the General Liaison's, since a move sits above either branch. Department membership and Project Lead status are not switches — the first is the Head's, the second comes from an MOU record. A **rejected** request may carry a reason, shown to the person, who may ask again and lands in Pending afresh. **Deleted** is a record — who, when, and by whom — of an account that is gone for good and cannot be restored, or deleting would be deactivation under another name. **An invited person arrives already approved**: sending the invitation is the approval, so they never wait in Pending, and their row shows who invited them. It skips the course and nothing else — the invitation goes to a Save7 email address the inviter has arranged, everyone signing in on the same kind of account. Replaced the **Admin Dashboard**, whose contents were split by what each actually was — access came here, the master volunteer tracker went to the Operations Hub, the branch list to its Branches page and the department list to the Departments directory.
_Avoid_: Admin Dashboard (retired), admin, admin panel, control panel, back office, user approval (the page inside it, not the hub)

**Access request**:
A person's ask to be let into Save7 OS, which they may send **only once they have finished the course** at learn.save7.org — finishing it lets nobody in by itself. Names **their branch, or none**, chosen by them, which is what routes it: to that branch's Branch Management, answering from Branch Centre, or to the General Liaison where they chose none; National Exco and the General Liaison see every one in the **Users** hub and may answer any. Waits in *Pending* until **approved** or **rejected**, a reason optional on a rejection, and never lapses; someone rejected may ask again. Stands as a duty, aggregated per branch, on its Branch Management. The one way in besides it is an invitation, which skips the course (see Volunteer).
_Avoid_: submission, application, sign-up, registration (registering at learn.save7.org comes first and is not this)

**Super Admin**:
The only admin status there is — a flag layered on a seat, not a seat of its own, and never held by a Volunteer. Confers **read** access to every hub across every branch and department — with **two carve-outs**, neither of which a Super Admin reads: the **Meetings page** of the Operations Hub's Compliance section, and the **Users** hub. Maintaining the site is not a reason to sit in on Exco's deliberations, nor to decide who gets in, so both are reached by seat — National Exco and the General Liaison, and for Meetings the advisory board — and never by admin status. It confers no edit rights, which still come from the underlying seat. The "Super" is measured against National Exco rather than a lesser admin: a Super Admin sees everything and changes nothing structural. One who is not Exco cannot grant or revoke Super Admin, assign any seat, add a branch or department, create or alter an Exco seat, or override an Exco decision. Exco hold it by default and irrevocably, and the Tech Department Head holds it by seat, since maintaining the site requires seeing everything; otherwise granted at Exco's discretion, in practice to seats already carrying organisation-wide responsibility such as the General Liaison. Account upkeep is not theirs: it is done in the Users hub, which a Super Admin does not open.
_Avoid_: admin, superuser, root, owner

**Branch Manager**:
Runs one branch. Edit access to their own branch across hubs; read-only access to other branches — **money excepted**: in the Finance Hub they read their own branch in full and every other branch in totals, and write nothing, the finance line running from the branch's Finance Manager to the CFO. Authors MOUs (responsibilities/expectations/deliverables) for that branch's Project Leads — routed through the General Liaison for Exco review and sign-off. Appointed by Exco. With the Operations Manager and Finance Manager, accepts new volunteers onto the platform — a power that comes with the seat itself, not from any admin status. Authoring a branch volunteer's Project Lead MOU is the Branch Manager's alone, and it is their whole part in making a Project Lead: the status is conferred by the record of Exco's sign-off, not by any branch seat (see MOU). Branch Management own their branch's volunteers for the whole of that lifecycle: reviewing dormancy, deactivating, and reactivating. Deleting an account outright is Exco's alone. Any one of the three, acting alone, may author an Objective or a Key Result — no quorum, and not confined to their own branch, since OKR authorship is the one thing in the model that is not unit-scoped.

**Operations Manager**:
Runs one branch's operations team. Branch-scoped, same edit-own/read-others pattern as Branch Manager. Appointed by Exco; shares Branch Management's powers over their branch's volunteers — see Branch Manager. **Reports directly to the COO** — the operations line runs to the COO the way the finance line runs from each Finance Manager to the CFO — while working alongside their branch's Branch Manager. Oversees all volunteers in the branch, all Project Leads, and new-volunteer onboarding. **Overseeing a Project Lead is looking after them as a person** — they are one of the branch's volunteers — and reading their Projects as any seat-holder does; it confers nothing over the Project, which is its Lead's. Drafts, offline, the branch's **Agreements** with outside organisations and its **SOPs**, and briefs the branch's volunteers from them; never drafts a Project Lead MOU, which is the Branch Manager's alone. Along with the Branch Manager and COO, funnels volunteers with a specialised interest toward the matching Department.

**Finance Manager**:
Runs one branch's finance function. Reports directly to the CFO — there is no national Finance Department standing between them. Appointed by Exco; shares Branch Management's powers over their branch's volunteers — see Branch Manager. **The one seat below Exco that writes in the Finance Hub**, and only over their own branch: they **decide their branch's Claims** — approve, reject or query a fuel claim or a Branch spend line — and **review the budget plan** of every Project homed at their branch, accepting it or returning it with a reason. They do not mark a Claim paid, since the money leaves the organisation's account and no branch holds one, never decide their own Claim, and post nothing to the ledger by hand. They read their own branch's money in full and every other branch's in **totals** — Budget against spend, spend by category — never another branch's individual Claims, which name a person and an amount. **Money duties sit with them alone**, not with Branch Management jointly.
_Avoid_: branch function head, finance head, department head, branch lead

**Branch Management**:
The three branch seats **collectively** — Branch Manager, Operations Manager, Finance Manager — one set per branch. Not a seat of its own and not a fourth appointment: the name for the three acting as the branch's leadership, which is how the model refers to them whenever the point is the branch owning its people rather than one manager's particular remit. They accept volunteers — answering their branch's **access requests** from Branch Centre, while National Exco and the General Liaison see every request in the Users hub — and own their branch's volunteers through dormancy, deactivation and reactivation. They do **not** grant Project Lead status — the Branch Manager alone authors a branch volunteer's MOU, and the status comes from the record of its sign-off (see MOU). Over a Project homed at their branch they hold no write at all: the branch owns the person, the Lead owns the work. **Money is not held jointly**: the branch's Claims and budget plans are its Finance Manager's alone. Addressable as a unit — an Action Point may be assigned to a branch's Branch Management, which lands on all three.
_Avoid_: branch trio (the previous term, renamed), trio, branch leadership, branch exec

**Department**:
A national/organisation-level portfolio for specialised work: Media, Tech, Research, Education. Open-ended list managed by National Exco alone, extendable the same way Branch is — Departments are added and edited on the Departments directory. A **second supervisory axis**, not a label: joining a Department places a volunteer's work in that portfolio under its Department Head, while their branch's Branch Management continues to own them as a person. Distinct axis from the Branch system — operates directly under Exco, not under a Branch Manager. A volunteer may belong to several Departments at once, one interest not excluding another; each Head's authority reaches only their own portfolio's work, so two Heads never contend over the same task. Finance is not a Department — see Finance Manager.

**Joined by asking, and admitted by its Head.** A volunteer asks to join from the Department's front page, sees the request pending there and may withdraw it; the Department Head approves or declines, a reason optional, and someone declined may ask again. A request **never lapses** — it stands on the Head's Dashboard until answered, since a request that quietly expired would let a Head refuse someone without ever saying no. The Head may also **invite** a volunteer, who must **accept**: membership places a person's work under somebody, and that cannot be agreed on their behalf. Branch Management has no say — the branch owns the person, the Head owns the work — and the funnelling the Branch Manager, Operations Manager and COO do toward a matching Department is advice that points a volunteer at the Departments Hub, never an act on the platform.

**Leaving, removal and the inactive flag.** A member may leave at any time. The Head may **remove** a member — for someone who should not be there — who may then ask again. **Inactive** is the state between, for someone who has gone quiet and is expected back: still a member, still able to enter the Department's hub and read it, unable to write content, and cleared by the Head alone. Membership survives a change of seat and a deactivated account alike. **A Department with no Head** shows as having none and is **closed to requests and invitations**, there being nobody to answer them — the rule a Project with no Lead follows. Its members stay and may leave, requests already waiting pass to the next Head, an inactive flag stays until one arrives, and nobody carries a duty for it. National Exco do not admit members in a Head's place: admitting someone is taking on their work.
_Avoid_: hub (a Department is an org unit; Hub is a nav section — a Department typically owns a Hub, but they aren't the same concept)

**Department Head**:
Owns, grows, and organises one Department at national scale. MOU authored directly by Exco (no Branch Manager in the loop). Gives Exco in-person or online updates at set intervals, and routes any Exco-discussion requests through the General Liaison. Owns their Department's **hub**, which sits under Departments rather than at the top level — the Media Department Head owns the Media Hub, absorbing the former CMO/Marketing remit, which is no longer a separate Exco seat or department. Holds real authority over their Department's work: sees what its members are doing, assigns it, and has a say in it; onboards them with the tools the portfolio needs, advises them, and mobilises them. That authority stops at the work — **the Department Head owns the work, Branch Management owns the person**, so acceptance onto the platform, dormancy review, deactivation and reactivation are never theirs. They may mark a member inactive *within the Department*, which is a per-Department status and touches nothing about the account — its one consequence is to suspend that member's write over content in the Department's hub (see Volunteer). **The two axes do not reach into each other**: marking someone inactive within a Department never feeds their Branch Management's dormancy review, and dormancy at their branch never marks them inactive in a Department — a volunteer may be quiet in one and busy in the other, and letting either switch the other off would have one supervisor judging the other's half of the person. The one exception is **deactivation**, which belongs to the account rather than to either axis: a deactivated account can do nothing, but its Department memberships survive it and return on reactivation, the Head seeing that member as deactivated and unable to undo it. **Holding the seat makes them a member** of their Department, doing everything its members do — entering its hub, writing content, opting in to its work — so they cannot be marked inactive, nobody in the Department sitting above them (a Head who goes quiet is Exco's to reassign), and they do not leave it, stepping down from the seat instead; someone who stops being Head stays an ordinary member unless they leave. What the Head holds beyond membership is its **management**, which lives on their **Dashboard** rather than in the Department's hub: volunteers waiting to join, the invitations they have sent, the member list and the inactive flags. They admit, invite, remove and mark inactive from there (see Department). Authors Objectives and Key Results for any unit rather than only their own Department, OKR authorship being the one thing in the model that is not unit-scoped; the Mission & vision, the Strategy and the Pillars remain Exco's alone. **One Head per Department, but a person may head several** — the authority being scoped to each Department separately, heading two needs nothing but two of everything. The seat sits alongside any other: an Exco member, the General Liaison or a Branch Manager may head a Department too. Where a branchless volunteer in their Department seeks Project Lead status, the Head works the MOU up with them and passes it to the General Liaison. The Tech Department Head additionally carries Super Admin by seat.
_Avoid_: finance manager, branch lead

**Project Lead**:
Not a distinct account type — a status/upgrade on a Volunteer, granted via an MOU authored at the lowest supervising level above them and signed off by Exco, and **conferred on the platform by the record of that sign-off** (see MOU) — held from the moment the record exists, before any Project does. Owns the resulting Project, which they create from the Projects Hub; until they do, the unused record sits on their Dashboard as a standing duty. A **standing** status, not a time-boxed one: it lasts as long as the Project does. Held **once per accepted MOU** — a person holding three signed-off MOUs may create three Projects and no more, which is what the MOU record on the platform exists to enforce (see MOU). Sees the volunteers working under them and their Tasks; admits, invites and removes them, assigns them Tasks and owns the columns of the Project's Board (see Project Team); edits the Project's eligibility, its status and the Pillars, Objectives and Key Results it names; writes its overview, its project tracker and its attached documents, and submits its budget plan to the Finance Hub; and keeps its log of Updates. Joining a Project Team confers nothing — only an accepted MOU makes a Project Lead. **Ends three ways:** a successor signs an MOU for the Project — which may be the same MOU the outgoing Lead hands over, run through the normal route; **National Exco remove the Lead outright**, the sign-off having been theirs; or the Project is **completed or cancelled**. Whoever authored the MOU does not revoke the status. Carries **OKR authorship**: writes Objectives and Key Results for any unit, on the same terms as Branch Management and Department Heads — the one place the status reaches outside its own Project — held **while the status is held and not a moment after**, so leading a Project once does not confer organisation-wide OKR write for life. A Project that is Inactive or On ice changes none of this: those statuses report momentum and carry no permission, or nobody would ever set them. Completing or cancelling one does end the status for it, and with it the OKR authorship it carried unless its holder leads something else — which is why National Exco may set those statuses too, so that a Project is not kept open just to keep a permission.

**Project**:
An initiative owned by a Project Lead, born when a volunteer's idea is written up as an MOU and signed off by Exco — so there is no Project waiting on a sign-off, and nothing before *Active*. Carries one **status**, set by its Lead or by National Exco: **Active**, where every Project starts; **Inactive**, dormant — it has lost momentum and needs a push, the same signal a Branch carries; **On ice**, parked on purpose to be picked up later, a decision rather than a stall; and **Completed** or **Cancelled**, which are final. Only the final two carry any consequence.

**Not time-boxed, but able to end.** A Project runs until its Lead or Exco say it has finished, and many never will; nothing about a date closes one. A completed or cancelled Project is closed to requests and invitations, and its Board, team and Updates become read-only — save that its Lead may still attach its **reflection and close** document. The platform stamps the date it ended. Ending one **closes whatever was still waiting** — requests to join and invitations, each person seeing that the Project ended rather than their ask quietly vanishing, and every open Task, which leaves its owners' Dashboards and stays on the Board as left undone. Only National Exco reopen one, to correct a mistake. Its MOU stays used, so running the same thing again takes a new MOU and a new Project. Its **home is its Lead's branch**, read off the Lead rather than chosen, and null where the Lead is branchless; a Project left with no Lead keeps the home its last Lead gave it until a successor arrives, its people and its history still being there. It attaches to no Department: a Department Head already owns their members' work wherever that work happens, and a tag would add a second claimant without adding a power.

**Created by its Lead**, through a form in the Projects Hub that appears only to someone holding an unused MOU record, once the MOU behind it is signed off and recorded — one Project per accepted MOU. The form sets a name, what the Project is for, the MOU it comes from — chosen by its code from the Lead's own unused ones — and its **eligibility**: the set of branches whose volunteers may join, plus whether branchless volunteers may. One branch in the set is a branch-scoped Project; every branch is an open one. KPIs and targets are not set here — they live on the MOU, and tracking them is the work of a project tracker. A Project carries a **start date** and an optional **target end date**, both set by its Lead and both informational: nothing is computed from them, so no Project is ever shown as late or as having slipped.

**Names the ladder it serves** — any number of Pillars, Objectives and Key Results, all optional, and a Project that names none is unremarkable. Set on the creation form and changed at any time by its Lead, with National Exco as backstop. The choice **cascades**: naming a Key Result carries in its Objective and that Objective's Pillar, naming an Objective carries in its Pillar. Because these are sets rather than single slots, naming a Pillar alongside an Objective standing on a different one is not a contradiction — it is a Project serving two things. The link is **inert**: it moves no progress bar, since an Objective's figure stays the derived mean of its Key Results, and it confers nothing on the Key Results named — no ownership, no acceptance, no duty. Deleting a named Objective or Key Result, or retiring a named Pillar, simply drops it from the set; a Project pointing at something never blocks its deletion, and the Project shows as unlinked, which everyone can see. **The same commitment may legitimately appear twice** — as a Key Result on the ladder and as a KPI in the project tracker — and the link is what makes the duplication visible rather than what prevents it: the two answer to different documents, the ladder and the MOU.

Carries a log of **Updates**, kept by its Lead.

Every Project is visible to every member of the organisation, whether or not they may join it: eligibility gates joining, never seeing. Every member reads its **page** — its **overview** (what it is for, its goals and its audience), its Lead, its status, its eligibility stated as it applies to the reader, the ladder it names, its Updates, its team, and its **MOU code**, which opens the signed MOU only for those allowed to read it (see MOU). Its **Board** of Tasks is read only by its team and its Lead, and by the seats that read everything: the invitation is open, the working surface belongs to the people doing the work. Its **project tracker** and **attached documents** are read by its Lead, its team and anyone holding a seat — except the **budget plan** and the Project's **spend**, which only the Lead, the seats that read everything and the plan's reviewer — its home branch's Finance Manager, in the Finance Hub — see. The overview, the tracker and the documents are written by the Lead alone, with National Exco as backstop.

**A Project with no Lead** — its Lead removed by Exco and no successor signed off — shows as having none and is **closed to requests and invitations**, there being nobody to answer them. Its team stays, and may leave; requests already waiting pass to the successor; its status falls to Exco; and nobody carries a duty for it, since nobody owes a successor — a Project nobody will lead is Exco's to cancel.

**Deleting** a Project is National Exco's alone and corrects a mistake — a Project created against the wrong MOU, or created twice — returning its MOU record to unused. It is never a way of ending one; that is what Completed and Cancelled are for.
_Avoid_: programme, campaign, initiative (use for the idea before it is signed off, not the Project), time-boxed (a Project ends when someone says so, not at a set term), pending, planned (there is no Project before its MOU is signed off)

**Project Team**:
The volunteers helping on a Project, plus the Project Lead who owns it. Team members remain ordinary volunteers under their own Branch Management; sitting on a team grants no status and no authority over the Project. Its **one write** is on the Project's **Board**: a member writes their own Tasks, moves cards and ticks their own Tasks done, while the Lead also assigns Tasks to any member, edits or deletes any, and owns the columns. An assigned Task needs no acceptance — joining was the handshake, and a member who will not do it says so or leaves. The team is visible to every member of the organisation, each member with an optional **role** on it — *Logistics*, *Social media* — set by the Lead, which labels what they do there and grants nothing.

**The Project Lead is the only gate.** An eligible volunteer asks to join from the Project's page, sees the request pending there and may withdraw it; the Lead approves or declines from the same page — their own Branch Management has no say, since the branch owns the person and the Lead owns the work. A decline may carry a reason and need not, a Lead choosing their team owing nobody an account of it; someone declined may ask again. A request **never lapses**: it stands as a duty on the Lead's Dashboard until answered, because a request that quietly expires lets a Lead refuse someone without ever saying no. The Lead may also **invite** a volunteer, who must **accept** — being added to a team silently creates an expectation of work nobody agreed to — and the invitation stands on the invitee's Dashboard until they accept or decline it, the Lead being free to withdraw it. **An invitation never overrides eligibility**: the set is what everyone reads as who may join, so a Lead who wants one volunteer from another branch adds that branch and answers its requests. Waiting requests and invitations are seen only by the Lead and the person concerned. There is **no cap** on a team's size or on how many join from one branch; the Lead's approval already does that job.

A member may **leave** at any time and the Lead may **remove** one — deliberately unlike a Key Result's owners, who have no exit, because membership grants nothing there is any reason to make sticky. Narrowing a Project's eligibility **does not eject** anyone already on the team: the set governs new joins, and removing someone is a named act, not a side effect of editing a list.
_Avoid_: project members, crew, squad

**MOU**:
An offline, signed agreement between a role and its supervising structure (Branch Manager ↔ Project Lead, Exco ↔ Department Head) setting responsibilities, expectations, deliverables, and KPIs/targets.

A Project Lead MOU is authored at the **lowest supervising level above the applicant** and routed upward through the General Liaison to Exco for sign-off; where the applicant already sits at or above a level, that level is skipped. So: a branch-onboarded volunteer's MOU is authored with their **home** Branch Manager whatever the project's reach, and Department membership does not divert it; a branchless volunteer in a Department works it up with that Department Head; a branchless volunteer in no Department works it up with the General Liaison; the General Liaison authors their own and goes straight to Exco; an Exco member authors their own and presents it to the rest of Exco. Not a UI workflow: the drafting, the review and the sign-off stay offline. A Project Lead MOU's KPIs and targets are followed in its Project's **project tracker**, never in the OKRs Hub. A Department Head's MOU is kept in the **Operations Hub**, which holds every signed MOU. Not to be confused with an **Agreement**, which Save7 makes with an outside organisation even where the paper is titled a memorandum of understanding.

The platform nonetheless holds a **record** of each signed-off Project Lead MOU — who holds it, and when Exco signed it off — entered by the **General Liaison**, with Exco as backstop, at the moment of sign-off, on the **MOUs** page of the Operations Hub's Compliance section, which keeps every signed MOU — Department Head MOUs beside Project Lead ones. **The record is what confers Project Lead status** — no branch seat grants it. It is entered either **unattached**, making a new Project possible, or **against an existing Project**, making its holder that Project's successor Lead, which is how a handed-over MOU is recorded. The record does not run the drafting, the review or the sign-off; it exists because a Project is created from an accepted MOU and one MOU makes exactly one Project. KPIs and targets stay on the document, not in the record.

Each record carries a **code** — `#MOU-0042` — which the platform issues at entry: unique, never reused and never typed, so that no two can collide. It is how a Project names the MOU it comes from, and how the paper and the record point at each other. A Lead's creation form offers their own unused codes and no one else's. The **signed copy is uploaded to the record** by the General Liaison as part of entering it, and is required, since a record with no document asserts a sign-off nobody can check. It is read by its holder, whoever authored it with them, the General Liaison, National Exco and the seats that read everything; every other member sees the code on the Project's page and cannot open it. A successor's record carries a code of its own — the Project shows its current Lead's, the earlier ones staying listed for those readers. The General Liaison or Exco may **withdraw** a record no Project has been created from, which is how an idea dropped before its Project exists comes off its holder's Dashboard.

**Agreement**:
A signed agreement between **Save7 and an outside organisation** — a hospital, a university, another NGO — whatever the paper calls itself: the Tygerberg Life Pod transplant-shadowing memorandum of understanding is an Agreement, not an MOU. Drafted and negotiated **offline**, by a branch's Operations Manager for that branch's arrangements or nationally; the **signed copy** is kept on the **Agreements** page of the Operations Hub's Compliance section, which groups them by partner. An Operations Manager **submits the draft** from their own hub for the COO's approval **before it is signed** — approval after a signature could undo nothing — and the signed copy is then filed against the approved draft without a second approval; a draft may be returned with a reason, and never lapses. Names its **partner organisation**, the **branch** it serves or none where it is national, and may carry a **renewal date**: once that passes it shows **overdue, in red, on the COO's Dashboard**, and clears when the renewed copy is filed or the Agreement is marked ended. The Operations Manager does the renewing, offline, but cannot file it, so the red sits with the COO, who chases them. A renewal date of its own, rather than a warning computed ahead of an end date, keeps the one rule the model already has: a dated duty goes red when its date passes. It confers nothing on the platform and gates nothing, which is what separates it from an MOU — an MOU is internal, sits between a role and the level above it, and its record makes a Project Lead.
_Avoid_: MOU (the paper may carry that title; in this glossary an MOU is internal), contract, partnership, deal

**SOP**:
A **standard operating procedure** — Save7's written way of doing something that recurs, such as running a campus stall or conducting a hospital visit. Drafted by Operations Managers and the COO and kept in the **Operations Hub**, read by everyone holding a seat or Project Lead status. An Operations Manager **submits** one from their own hub in Branch Centre; it waits as a duty on the **COO's** Dashboard — one card however many are waiting — until **approved**, which publishes it, or **returned with a reason**, and it never lapses. The CEO and the General Liaison may approve one too, but the duty is the COO's. **Not visible to plain Volunteers**: an SOP is for the people who run the work, and an Operations Manager briefs their branch's volunteers from it rather than handing it over.
_Avoid_: policy, guideline, playbook, manual, procedure document

**Resolution**:
A formal decision of National Exco, recorded in the Operations Hub's **Compliance section**, carrying the **CEO's approval** — with exactly one exception: a Resolution **removing the CEO** is approved by the remaining Exco instead, since a CEO whose own approval were required could never be removed. It is the instrument that moves the seats at the top of the organisation: removing an Exco member, placing a retiring one on the Advisory board or taking them off it, a CEO's own stepping down (which may name a successor), removing a CEO who will not step down, and appointing the next CEO where no successor was named. Unlike an MOU, which is offline and describes a working relationship, a Resolution is held on the platform and changes who holds what.
_Avoid_: motion, vote, minutes, decision (too general — a Resolution is the recorded instrument, not any decision taken), board resolution (the same instrument under another name — there is no separate board passing its own)

**Volunteer**:
The base layer every account holds. There are **two doors** into Save7 OS (below), and everyone who comes through either is a Volunteer, every seat and status layering on top of it — Branch Management is a Volunteer holding that seat, and Project Lead and Super Admin layer the same way. Recruited at one branch, or at none. A volunteer normally enters through a branch and may transfer to another if they change university — a move National Exco or the General Liaison make; a volunteer recruited outside any university has no branch at all — a small population in a student-driven organisation, owned by the General Liaison instead of Branch Management, and following the same paths into Departments and Projects as anyone else. Has a **Dashboard** — the same surface every member of the organisation has, not a consolation for holding no seat — plus the **OKRs Hub** and **National Centre** with everything beneath it: the **Projects Hub**, the **Departments Hub** and the front page of all four Department hubs, what lies behind one opening to them when they join it. So the ladder from day-to-day work up to the mission is visible to the people doing that work, every Project is visible to everyone who might join one, and every Department is too. **Operations and Branch Centre do not appear in their nav** — save that holding **Project Lead status** opens the Operations Hub to them, to read — and of the **Finance Hub** they reach only **Your money**: their own Claims, their own Claude seat and their own documents. **A plain Volunteer holds exactly four writes anywhere in the model.** The first is **owning a Key Result** — its progress, their own acceptance of it, its health, its done tick, its Updates and its supporting units, on that Key Result alone. The second is **membership of a Department**, which carries write over **content** in that Department's own hub and nothing else — never its membership, never its permissions, never another Department's hub — because the Department axis exists to place a volunteer's work under a Head, and a media volunteer with no way to publish a template cannot do the work they joined to do. That write is what the per-Department **inactive** status suspends. The third is **membership of a Project Team**, which carries write over that Project's **Board** — writing their own Tasks, moving cards, ticking their own Tasks done — and nothing else about the Project, for the same reason: a team member who cannot put a card on the board cannot do the work they joined for. The fourth is **lodging their own Claims** — a fuel claim, or a Branch spend line for their own branch — and withdrawing one while it is still pending; once it has been decided, changing it is a finance correction rather than theirs, since the person driving to a hospital event is exactly who is owed the money. Everywhere else, write comes from a seat. Can simultaneously belong to a branch, be a member of one or more Departments, sit on one or more Project Teams, and hold Project Lead status for one or more initiatives — these are not mutually exclusive. Accounts are not created on the platform: a prospective volunteer signs up and completes the course at learn.save7.org, receives a Save7 email address, and only then may **ask for access** — finishing the course lets nobody in by itself; the request waits, *Pending*, until it is *accepted* onto the platform by hand, by their branch's Branch Management. The **second door** is an **invitation**, for the people Save7 wants in **without the course**: National Exco or the General Liaison send someone a registration link, and the course is the one thing it skips. Graduating does not end volunteering — accounts persist, and deletion is a genuine last resort rather than routine cleanup, reserved to Exco: an account costs a completed course and a Save7 email address to obtain, or an invitation from the top of the organisation, so destroying one is not a branch-level act. **Contribution is recorded as work owned and done, never as time spent** — nothing on the platform measures duration, and no hours are logged, verified or counted anywhere in the model. What a volunteer has contributed is legible from the Key Results they own, the Projects they sit on and the Action Points they close; proof of that contribution to somebody outside Save7 is written by a person, offline.

**Hub**:
A named, role-gated section of the site's navigation. **Six sit at the top level** — OKRs, Finance, Operations, **National Centre**, **Branch Centre** and **Users** — and nine sit beneath the two Centres (see their entries). **Where a hub sits on screen is deliberately not part of the model** — top nav, sidebar group or anything else is a rendering decision, left to the work that builds the Shell. What the model fixes is which hubs exist, how they nest, who may open each, and what state each is in.

**Nesting runs three levels along two spines and nowhere else** — National Centre → Departments → a Department's hub, and Branch Centre → a seat's hub. Everywhere else the nav is one level deep: a hub's internal pages are pages within it, not hubs and not sub-hubs, and a **section** — a named group of pages inside one hub, such as the Operations Hub's **Compliance** section and the Meetings page within it — is not a hub either. Compliance was a top-level hub until it was folded into Operations. **A Department's hub is reached under Departments and never stands at the top level**, whatever that Department's size or reach — Media carried a top-level hub in the pre-Departments nav and loses it for the same reason branch Media Head went, the Departments layer having arrived after that nav was drawn.

**A hub that holds only other hubs has no page of its own**, because that is a menu rather than a place — true of National Centre and Branch Centre, and of neither anywhere else. **Departments is the counter-case and holds a page deliberately:** a directory of the four Departments, what each is for and what it is working on, and how to join one. Nothing else in the model tells a volunteer the Departments exist. See Departments Hub.

A hub is hidden entirely from a role's nav if that role can't access it, rather than shown disabled. A Dashboard is not a hub either. **Open to every member of the organisation, Volunteers included:** the **OKRs Hub**, since a ladder nobody can see fails at its only job; the **Projects Hub** — which the user refers to informally as *All Projects* — since every member is a potential member of every Project open to them and a directory that hides what you cannot join cannot tell you what Save7 is running; **National Centre, Departments and all four Department hubs** — every Department is visible to everybody through its **front page**, and what lies behind that page is for those who belong to it (see Departments Hub); and the **Finance Hub**, of which a plain Volunteer reaches only **Your money**, since everyone may be owed a reimbursement and nobody without a seat needs the books. **A page inside a hub may be gated more tightly than the hub itself** — the Meetings page of the Operations Hub's Compliance section, which a Super Admin does not read, and everything behind a Department's front page, which only its members, its Head and the seats that read everything reach: for Media, its content scheduler, tracker, tasks and calendar. What a member may *edit* there still comes from their seat, save for the four writes a plain Volunteer holds (see Volunteer): owning a Key Result, content in a Department they belong to, the Board of a Project whose team they sit on, and lodging their own Claims.

A hub may be **under construction** — it appears in the nav for whoever may reach it and its page says it is not built yet. See that entry; it is not a property of hubs alone. **Tech, Research and Education carry it**, behind their front pages.
_Avoid_: top nav, sidebar section, tab (each names a placement the model does not fix); sub-hub (a nested hub is a Hub like any other)

**National Centre**:
A Hub holding the **Projects Hub** and the **Departments Hub**, and nothing else. It has no page of its own — clicking it opens what is under it. A **grouping in the navigation, not an organisational unit**: nobody heads it, it confers no permission, and it makes no claim that what it holds is national — the Projects Hub is the loosest fit of all, a Project's home being its Lead's branch. Open to every member, Volunteers included, because both of its children are. Named against the alternative of leaving Projects and Departments at the top level, to shorten the top row; the cost is that both sit one click further away for the members who use them most.
_Avoid_: Nerve Centre (the effort and its repo, never a surface in the product), National Exco (a body of seats, not a place in the nav)

**Projects Hub**:
The hub holding every Project, under National Centre and open to every member: a **directory**, and each Project's **page** with its Board and its project tracker. The directory is one flat list, searched by name and filtered by **home** branch, by **status** — Active and Inactive Projects showing unless the reader asks for those On ice, Completed or Cancelled — and to the Projects **open to the reader**; not grouped, and not filtered by Pillar, since a Pillar in the OKRs Hub already lists the Projects serving it. A Project's page is read by everyone, its Board by its team, its tracker by its team and anyone holding a seat (see Project). It holds **no money** for anyone but a Project's Lead and the seats that read everything; spend against a Project is the Finance Hub's to show. It holds no MOU records: those are entered on the Operations Hub's MOUs page, which keeps every signed MOU, and a Project's page shows only its MOU code.
_Avoid_: All Projects (the user's informal name for it), project portfolio, projects page

**Project tracker**:
A Project's built-in record of how it is doing against its MOU — one per Project, a page of it in the Projects Hub, reached by its Lead from their Dashboard too. Holds **KPIs**, each a current figure against a target; **milestones**, each named and dated with a done tick; **deliverables**, planned against actual; and a **budget plan**, begun from a template, filled in line by line and **submitted to the Finance Hub**, where it is accepted or returned with a reason (see Budget) — revisable and resubmittable, every submission kept. The Lead adds their own KPIs and milestones, and writes all of it, with National Exco as backstop. **Nothing in it is computed or chased**: an overdue milestone shows its date and nothing goes red, nothing reaches a Dashboard, and no check-in is kept. What the tracker does not hold is an **attached document** — a file the Lead uploads to the Project — for motivation and sustainability, the risk register and stakeholders, and the reflection and close sign-off. Not the MOU: the MOU sets the targets and the tracker follows them.
_Avoid_: tracker sheet, full proposal, check-in, Ops Mentor (Save7 OS today's second writer of the tracker, with no counterpart in the model)

**Departments Hub**:
The hub holding the four Department hubs, under National Centre and open to every member — and, unlike the two Centres, holding a page of its own: a **directory** of the Departments. Each Department's row gives its name, and whether it is under construction; its **Head**, or *No Head yet*; **what it is for**, a short standing description written by the Head with National Exco as backstop; **what it is working on**, which is read rather than written — the Department's own Objectives from the OKRs Hub, and a way into the Key Results naming it as a supporting unit — since a hand-written account goes stale and the ladder already holds the answer; and the reader's own standing in it: a member, inactive, a request pending, or none. Membership is not managed here; that is the Head's, from their Dashboard.

**Every Department's hub opens on a front page** — its signpost, open to every member, and built for every Department whether or not the rest is. It carries the directory's row at full size, the way in for someone who is not a member (asking to join, or closed where there is no Head), and whatever the Department offers everyone: for Media, its brand kit, templates, coverage log, photo and video library and the Content Request intake. **Behind it is the Department's own work**, which its members, its Head and the seats that read everything **enter** — National Exco, and the Advisory board while Advising, writing there as backstop to the Head; Branch Management does not enter, the Head owning the work. On an under-construction hub the notice is what lies behind the front page, so a Head runs their Department from the front page, their Dashboard and the OKRs Hub until the rest is built. The member list is on no page of the hub: the Head holds it, and fellow members see one another only on the work they share.
_Avoid_: Departments group (it holds a page, so it is more than a group), department list (Exco add Departments on this directory, but it is a page for every member, not an admin list), lobby, landing page

**Operations Hub**:
The **COO's** hub, national in scope: where Save7 keeps track of its people, its branches and its paperwork. Holds the **Volunteers** page — the **master volunteer tracker**, one row per volunteer in every branch, giving their branch, seats, Departments, Projects and Project Lead status, filterable by how far onboarding has got, each row opening that person's **Profile** and carrying **the same switches as the Users hub**, each reader seeing only those they own on the rows they own — Branch Management deactivate and reactivate their own branch's people from here as well as from Branch Centre, flipping a switch being an act on a person rather than an edit to the hub; the **Branches** page — where National Exco add, edit and delete branches — one row per branch with its Branch Management, its active/inactive status, how many volunteers it has and how many await acceptance, and its latest **branch report** with the date it was written, so that a quiet branch is visible without anything going red; the **Events** calendar; the **SOPs**; and the **Compliance** section — **Resolutions**, **Agreements**, the **MOUs** page holding every signed MOU, **Templates** (the Resolution template, under construction; MOU and SOP templates; indemnity and consent forms), **Meetings**, and an **Incident log**, under construction.

**Read by everyone holding a seat or Project Lead status**, and by no plain Volunteer — an Operations Manager briefs their volunteers rather than handing them the hub. **Some pages are gated tighter**: the master tracker and Profiles are read by National Exco, the General Liaison, the Advisory board, Super Admins, every Branch Management seat (every branch, not only their own) and the Department Heads, and not by Project Leads; Meetings by National Exco, the General Liaison and the Advisory board; a signed MOU by those its record names. **Its own content is edited by the COO, the CEO and the General Liaison, and nobody else** — not the CFO or CGO, not an Advising member of the Advisory board, not Branch Management. Acts the model gives to someone keep their owner wherever their page sits: the General Liaison keeps the Meetings record, enters MOU records and runs the lifecycle of branchless volunteers from the master tracker, and National Exco's collective acts stay theirs. Branch Management read here and act on their own branch's volunteers from Branch Centre. An Operations Manager reaches the hub by **submission**: what they draft goes to the COO from their own hub in Branch Centre, and their branch reports are written there and shown here.
_Avoid_: ops hub, operations dashboard, Compliance Hub (folded into this hub as its Compliance section)

**Finance Hub**:
The **CFO's** hub, national in scope, holding Save7's **one ledger** — a single set of books in which a branch is a dimension on a line, never a book of its own, and a line with no branch is the organisation's. **Save7 OS's finance build carried across whole**, the model deciding who holds each power and the build deciding what the records are and how they move. Its pages: **Overview** — cash on hand, income against spend, the action queue; the **Financial tracker** — the ledger itself, where a manual outgoing payment awaits its co-signatures; **Fuel claims**; **Branch spend**; **Claude seats** — subscriptions Save7 pays for on people's own accounts and recovers from them unless it funds the seat outright; **Section 18A** — the tax certificates issued to donors, and the donors themselves; the **Receipt vault**; **Budgets**, with the **budget plans** Projects submit; and its settings — the chart of accounts, opening cash, the fuel rate, the financial year, the certificate issuer and who may co-sign. And **Your money**, the one page group every member reaches: their own Claims, their own Claude seat and their own documents.

**Opens to every member, gated by page.** A plain Volunteer reaches Your money and nothing else. **National Exco**, and the Advisory board while **Advising**, read and write all of it, the four Exco seats alike; the **General Liaison**, **Super Admins** and the Advisory board while **Observing** read all of it and write none. A branch **Finance Manager** reads their own branch in full and every other branch in totals, and writes only the decisions on their branch's Claims and the reviews of their branch's budget plans; the **Branch Manager** and **Operations Manager** read the same and write nothing. A Project's **Lead** reads its budget plan and its spend. Approving a Claim and paying it are kept apart — the Finance Manager decides, Exco pays — and **nobody decides their own Claim**. A record a flow has posted is never deleted as a side effect of deleting something else, and **nothing here blocks spend**: a Budget reports, a missing co-signature flags, and neither refuses.
_Avoid_: the books (one page of it), money hub, finance admin (Save7 OS today's role, not a place)

**Claim**:
Money a person asks to be reimbursed or accepted as spent — a **fuel claim**, the distance driven at the fuel rate frozen onto the claim so that a later change never restates it, or a **Branch spend** line, which carries its receipt. **Lodged by the person themselves**, as *pending*, and withdrawable by them only while it is pending. Decided by the claimant's branch **Finance Manager** — approved, rejected, or **queried**, a query going back to the claimant to answer before the decision is taken — and by National Exco, the CFO by name, where the claimant is that Finance Manager or has no branch. An approved fuel claim is then **paid** by Exco, which posts its line to the ledger. A Branch spend line belongs to **a branch or the organisation**, and may also name the **Project** it was for, which is how a Project's spend reaches the Finance Hub. Nothing caps a Claim: the Finance Manager's decision is the check on each one and the branch's Budget reports the total.
_Avoid_: reimbursement request (the user's informal term), team spend (Save7 OS today's name, where a team means a branch), expense, invoice

**Co-signatory**:
A person who may sign a **manual outgoing payment** on the ledger, two signatures from two different people being what authorises one. **Held by the person, not by any seat**, mirroring who the bank mandate names — which a seat cannot express — and granted or withdrawn by National Exco alone. A payment awaiting its signatures still counts in the books and is flagged rather than held back; the duty to sign it stands on every co-signatory who has not, shared the way a Department's members share an unanswered Content Request, and clears at the second signature.
_Avoid_: approver, signatory (the bank's word, for the mandate this mirrors)

**Budget**:
An amount allotted to a **branch** or to a **category** of spend for a **period** — by default a financial year running March to February, the SARS tax year, though any period may be set and two for the same target never overlap. **Set by National Exco**, and read by the branch's Finance Manager and Branch Management for their own branch. **Reports and never blocks**: spend over it shows, and no Claim is refused because of it. **Never follows a Season**, an irregular chapter declared by Exco being the wrong clock for money. A Project's money is a separate view rather than a Budget: its **budget plan**, submitted from its project tracker, is reviewed by its home branch's Finance Manager, or by the CFO by name where it has no home, and **accepted or returned with a reason, never left to lapse** — and an accepted plan is the Project's budget, shown against its spend. Budgets, category Budgets and Project budget plans are three views of the same money and are never added together. A Department has no Budget of its own: its spend is booked to the organisation under a category until it holds money of its own.
_Avoid_: allocation, budget line (one line of a Project's budget plan)

**Branch Centre**:
A Hub holding one hub per seat of the branch trio — **Branch Managers**, **Branch Operations Managers**, **Branch Finance Managers**. No page of its own, the same as National Centre. It is the branch counterpart at the top level, and its existence is what lets "National" in National Centre name an axis instead of describing contents loosely. **The Operations Hub and the Finance Hub are national** — the COO's and the CFO's — and hold no branch half for Branch Centre to take: each seat hub holds that seat's own work for its branch, distinct from both, and the Branch Finance Managers hub keeps **no second set of books**. Who may open Branch Centre and whose branch they see is open.
_Avoid_: Branch Management (the three seats acting jointly over a branch's people — an authority, not a place in the nav)

**Under construction**:
A **declared state, not an omission**: the thing exists in the model, appears wherever it belongs, and says plainly that it is not built yet. It is what lets the platform show the **whole shape of the organisation** while the content arrives afterwards, and what stops *"not yet decided"* from rendering to a member as *"does not exist"*. On a **Hub**, it means the hub is in the nav for whoever may reach it and its page carries the notice rather than content — carried today by **Tech**, **Research** and **Education**. On a Department's hub the notice sits **behind its front page**, which every Department has whether built or not — so joining, the directory's account of it and the Head's management of its members are live in a Department whose own work is not.

It has a **second job, on the effort rather than the product**: it is the tag for anything whose build may land **either in this map or after the merge**, that being a question deliberately left open rather than answered early. Anything under construction is therefore **parked, never closed** — it is in scope, and the only open question is when. This is what separates it from work ruled out of scope, which does not come back.
_Avoid_: coming soon, TODO, out of scope (a scope ruling, and it never returns — under construction is in scope and merely unbuilt)

**Shell**:
The frame around whatever page a person is looking at — the navigation, and the chrome that says who they are and what they can reach. **There is one Shell, and every person gets it**: what appears in it is assembled from the seats and statuses that person holds, so someone holding several seats sees the union of what those seats reach, and a plain Volunteer sees their Dashboard, the OKRs Hub and National Centre with everything beneath it. There is no second frame chosen at sign-in, and **navigating never changes what a person is** — opening a page shows that page and nothing else. This is the one thing the model requires of the Shell; everything else about it, placement included, belongs to the work that builds it.
_Avoid_: admin view, member view, the admin half / member half (the model has no halves)

**Mission & vision**:
Save7's standing statement of what it exists to do — the top rung of the OKR ladder, written by National Exco as free-form prose. **Standing, never seasonal**: Exco edit it in place and the platform keeps no chapters of it, because a mission that accumulated versions would be a Strategy. It is the fixed thing each Season's Strategy moves against, which is what lets a member read the hub as *this is who we are*, then *this is what we are doing about it now, and what we did before*. **One write-up, not two fields** — mission and vision are one statement here.
_Avoid_: mission statement, purpose, values, mission & strategy (the old fused layer — see Strategy)

**Strategy**:
How Save7 pursues its Mission & vision right now, written by National Exco as free-form prose. **One Strategy per Season, and the Season is what chapters it**: a new Strategy is written when Exco declare a new Season, and it changes as the scope of what the organisation becomes changes. Sits below the Mission & vision and above the Pillars — Exco define the Pillars from both — but **holds nothing**: no Pillar, Objective or Key Result hangs off a Strategy, so a Season turning over moves nothing beneath it. **Not several named strategies standing side by side**; that shape is a Pillar's, and two rungs of the same shape one above the other would be a redundant rung.
_Avoid_: strategic plan, roadmap, strategic priority, pillar, mission & strategy (the old fused layer — see Mission & vision)

**Season**:
A chapter of Save7's **Strategy**, declared by National Exco. Has **no fixed length** — not a calendar year, an academic year, or a cycle of any set duration; a new Season begins when Exco decide there is a new Strategy to write. Scopes the Strategy **and nothing else**: the Mission & vision stands above it, and Pillars, Objectives and Key Results carry no Season and never roll over, so there is no archiving, carry-forward or closing-off anywhere beneath it. Seasons accumulate rather than overwrite and all stay visible, so that any member can see how the organisation has changed over time; the latest is marked current, and past Seasons stay editable — nothing is frozen.
_Avoid_: cycle, term, year, phase, quarter, sprint

**Pillar**:
An organisation-wide focus area defined by National Exco from the Mission & vision and the current Strategy — standing examples: Public awareness, Help hospital systems, Help patients, Nimble operations. A **theme, not a commitment**: a Pillar has no owner, no Key Results and no progress of its own, and exists so that every Objective can be seen to serve something. Several stand at a time. Standing, not seasonal — revised in place rather than rewritten each Season. Retiring one **archives** it: removed from the list new Objectives may choose from, still displayed for Objectives already tagged to it. **Displays the Projects serving it**, rolled up from every rung beneath — the one piece of content a Pillar carries, and a directory rather than a commitment, so it remains true that a Pillar has no Key Results and no progress of its own.
_Avoid_: theme, focus area, goal, category, strategic priority

**Objective**:
A unit's commitment, standing on exactly one Pillar — the tag is mandatory, and an Objective that fits no Pillar means Exco needs a new Pillar, not that it should go untagged. Belongs to a **branch**, a **Department**, or the **nation**; the three are structurally identical and differ only in what they hang off. Belongs to a unit, never to a person, so **no Objective has an owner**: it displays **who created it**, and accountability sits one layer down, on its Key Results' owners. A branchless volunteer needs no Objective of their own — they reach work by being made an owner of a Key Result. Written by anyone holding a seat or Project Lead status, **for any unit** — authorship is not confined to the unit you supervise, which makes the OKRs Hub the one place the edit-own/read-others ceiling does not apply, with the creator stamp carrying the accountability that scoping would otherwise enforce. Plain Volunteers do not write Objectives. **Bigger than any one champion**, which is what separates it from a Project, born bottom-up from one volunteer's MOU. Carries a **progress** bar of its own — the unweighted mean of its Key Results' progress, **derived and uneditable**, because an Objective has no owner to set it. Unowned Key Results count at zero, so an Objective whose work nobody has taken reads low however well its owned Key Results are going; that sag is the point, not a defect, since it puts the organisation's work-discovery signal one layer up where the people who can act on it are looking. Carries **no health status**: health is a reading an owner makes, and an Objective has none. **Displays the Projects serving it** — those that named the Objective itself and those that named any of its Key Results, which roll up. The list is a directory and nothing more: a Project named here changes no figure and owes the Objective nothing.
_Avoid_: goal, OKR, initiative, target, project

**Key Result**:
One of the several commitments that move an Objective's needle; the Objective is met when its Key Results are. Written by the same people who write Objectives — anyone holding a seat or Project Lead status — who then attach its **owners** from a drop-down of anyone in the organisation, themselves included. Owners are a **flat set**: one is usual, several are allowed, none is primary, and there is no co-owner concept. A Key Result may also sit **unowned**, displayed as such, which is how work advertises that it needs picking up; an unowned one may be taken up by the person themselves. Carries a **progress** bar, an **acceptance** per owner, a **health** status, an optional **target date**, a **done** tick, a log of **Updates**, and its **supporting units**.

**Progress** is one figure from 0 to 100 that an owner slides. It is a **claim, not a measurement**: nothing computes it and nothing can contradict it, which is what makes the latest updates the evidence behind it rather than a diary beside it. Key Results differ wildly in kind — a plan either written or not, a session held twice a month, a satisfaction percentage held above a threshold — and one sliding claim is the only figure that fits all of them and stays comparable across them.

**Acceptance** is the handshake between one owner and the work. It is held **per owner**, never per Key Result, because it cannot be given on anybody's behalf: attaching a new owner to a Key Result others have accepted leaves that person unaccepted, and the Key Result displays how many of its owners have accepted. There is **no decline** — not accepting is the decline, visible as such, and only someone holding a seat or Project Lead status can remove an owner. Nothing times out.

**Health** is one of *On track*, *At risk*, *Off track* or *Paused*, shared across every owner with the last write standing; two owners who disagree settle it in the latest updates, not in the schema. *Paused* suspends the target date.

A **target date** is optional — the standing commitments that never end take none, and none is imposed by a Season, which scopes nothing below the Strategy. A Key Result whose date has passed shows **overdue, in red**, on its owners' Dashboards, exactly as an Action Point does, and nothing carries it forward. The **done** tick is what clears the red: set by an owner, it puts progress at 100 and takes the Key Result off the Dashboard while leaving it on the ladder. There is deliberately **no *Missed*** — an owner must not be able to clear their own red by declaring failure — so a Key Result that was genuinely missed stays red until somebody holding a seat rewords, redates or deletes it.

An **unowned** Key Result has no acceptance and no health, its bar is pinned at zero and immovable, and it can never be ticked done. Where it carries a target date that passes, the red falls to whoever created it.

An owner sets exactly those six things — progress, their own acceptance, health, the done tick, the Updates and the **supporting units** — and may not reword, reassign or delete the Key Result, nor remove themselves as an owner; all of which belong to anyone holding a seat or Project Lead status, **none of whom may set the first five**. Supporting units are the one field both sides write, because the person who discovers that help is needed is the one doing the work, and a tag that obliges nobody hands an owner nothing over the unit named. Nobody overrules an owner's reading: a Key Result claiming *On track* with no recent update is visibly stale, and staleness is a better signal than a drop-down two people are fighting over.

A Key Result that **cannot move until another unit acts** is said in the fields that already exist — health *At risk*, that unit named in supporting units, and a dated Update saying what is stuck. There is deliberately **no *Blocked*** health value: recording that a unit is blocking something raises the question of what that unit owes and when it clears, which is precisely the obligation supporting units do not carry — and since *Paused* stops the clock, a *Blocked* that did the same would let an owner clear their own red by pointing at somebody else.

A Key Result displays the Projects that have named it; those Projects also appear on its Objective and on that Objective's Pillar.
_Avoid_: task, action point (discrete and binary with a mandatory date, where a Key Result is a standing commitment carrying progress with an optional one — the dates do not distinguish them, the progress does), KPI, target, milestone

**Supporting Units**:
The parts of the organisation whose help a Key Result needs — **never the people doing the work**, who are its owners. A **readout, not a claim**: naming a unit obliges it to nothing, queues nothing, creates no request to accept or decline, and puts nothing on anybody's Dashboard. The model had already settled that a unit cannot owe anything — an Action Point's owners are people, or a branch's Branch Management, while Departments and National Exco are explicitly not assignable as groups — and a Department Head's authority over their members' work is not by itself a reason for them to owe something here (see Duty). Where the answer really is *"Tech should do this"*, the model already has the instrument: attach someone from Tech as an **owner**.

Optional and multi-select, drawn from: each **Department**, each **branch**, each branch's **Branch Management** as an entry of its own, and **National Exco**. A branch and its Branch Management are separate entries because needing a branch's volunteers and needing its three managers are different asks. **Not** in the list: individuals (that is what owners are), a Department Head separately from their Department (the Head is already who the Department reaches), the **Advisory board** (retained working capability, not a body with rights of its own to be leaned on), and a **Project** — a Project already names the ladder it serves, under its Lead's control, and a tag pointing the other way would give one pair of things two overlapping links.

Set by whoever wrote the Key Result **and by its owners**. Displayed on the Key Result and available as a **filter in the OKRs Hub, open to every member**, read there being flat; it does not roll up to the Objective. A tagged unit is never told — it finds out by looking — so the field records who the work leans on rather than starting anything.
_Avoid_: team (Save7 OS already uses it for a branch, and a Project Team is people), team tag, stakeholder, collaborator, assignee, contributor

**Update**:
One dated entry in a log — a **title**, a **date** and a body of free text. The title is always present, even when it says no more than *General check-in*, so that a log renders one way rather than two; a notable entry titles itself accordingly.

**One mechanism, used in three places.** A Key Result carries a log of Updates written by its **owners**; a Project carries one written by its **Lead**, with National Exco as backstop; and a **branch** carries one written by its **Operations Manager** — its **branch reports**, the operations line's account to the COO, written from the Branch Operations Managers hub and shown on the Operations Hub's Branches page. A branch report owes no date and nothing goes red when one is late: the Branches page shows when each branch last reported. A new one sits on the **COO's** and the **CEO's** Dashboards until each has opened it. A Project Team member does not write to a Project's log — sitting on a team grants no authority, and a log anyone may post to is a discussion thread rather than a record; a team member's own work surfaces through the Key Results they own.

**The weight differs with what it hangs off, and deliberately so.** On a Key Result, progress is a claim nothing computes and nothing can contradict, which makes the Updates the **only evidence** behind the bar. A Project has no progress bar, so its log is a **record** — what happened and when — carrying no evidential load; a branch's is the same.
_Avoid_: check-in, note, comment, diary, latest updates (the log is of Updates; "latest updates" names the display, not the thing), monthly report (a branch report has no cadence)

**Dashboard**:
The surface a person lands on when they sign in — **everyone has one**, whatever they hold, and it is the control centre for that account. Shows what is theirs: their open and overdue Action Points, **every Key Result they own**, the work assigned to them, **their dated Tasks**, **any Content Request they submitted that is still live**, who they are across the organisation (branch, Departments and any inactive flag in one, Project Teams, Project Lead statuses), what they must act on by virtue of a seat, **volunteers waiting to join a Project they lead** or **a Department they head**, **an invitation to join a Project or a Department awaiting their answer**, **an accepted MOU they hold with no Project yet**, any **request to join a Project or a Department they are still waiting on**, **any Claim of theirs still waiting on Finance**, and announcements from Exco. The COO's also carries the **SOPs and Agreement drafts submitted for approval** and any **Agreement past its renewal date**, and the COO's and the CEO's carry **branch reports they have not yet opened**. A Department Head's also carries their Department's **management** — its member list and inactive flags, and the invitations they have sent — which is run from here rather than from the Department's hub. **Per seat**, so what appears on it follows the person's duties — a Volunteer's and an Exco member's are the same surface carrying different things. Distinct from a Hub, which is organisational and shared; a Dashboard is personal. An Action Point is ticked done from here by its owner, as is a Key Result. For many plain Volunteers the Key Results they own will be the only thing on the surface, which is the same reasoning that opened the OKRs Hub to them.
_Avoid_: home page, my work, personal hub, landing page (it is the landing page, but the term names the surface)

**Profile**:
One page per person showing **who they are across the organisation** — their branch, their seats, their Departments, the Project Teams they sit on and the Projects they lead — opened from the **master volunteer tracker** in the Operations Hub or from the **Users** hub, and read only by **those who read that tracker**: National Exco, the General Liaison, the Advisory board, Super Admins, every Branch Management seat and the Department Heads. Not a Dashboard: a Dashboard is what a person sees of their own duties, a Profile what the people who look after volunteers see of them.
_Avoid_: account, user page, bio, member card

**Duty**:
Anything a person owes — the single word for what a Dashboard lists, covering an Action Point raised on someone, work assigned to them on a Project, and an obligation falling to them by virtue of a seat. Two kinds. A **raised duty** is a stored record with owners and one done status, ticked off by an owner; Action Points, assigned Project work, **a dated Task** and **an owned Key Result** are raised — a Key Result carrying a target date goes overdue in red exactly as an Action Point does, and its done tick is what clears it. A **standing duty** is not stored and not addressed to anyone — it is computed from a state somebody is responsible for, so it appears wherever that responsibility sits, disappears when the state does, and has nothing to tick. It **aggregates**: a branch with three volunteers awaiting acceptance produces one duty, not three, and a Project Lead or a Department Head with three volunteers waiting to join produces one duty, not three — the same shape, one level down. An invitation to join a Project or a Department stands on its invitee the same way until answered, and an accepted MOU with no Project yet stands on its holder until the Project is created or the record is withdrawn. Whoever creates a Key Result carries two of them — owners who have not yet accepted, and Key Results left unowned past their target date — kept separate rather than merged, because one asks them to chase a person and the other to find one. That is also why joint responsibility needs no machinery — Branch Management's three seats each see the same standing duty because the state is theirs collectively, any one of them acting clears it for all three, and no assignment, claiming or division of labour is required. A duty sits with the **lowest level that can act on it**, being explicitly named overrides, and holding the authority to do something is never by itself a reason to owe it. Vocabulary of the model, not of the interface: nothing on screen says "duty".
_Avoid_: obligation, commitment, responsibility, to-do, item, task (assigned Project work is one kind of duty, not the word for all of them)

**Meeting**:
A recorded sitting of **National Exco**, the only meeting the platform keeps — weekly, though nothing depends on the cadence. Carries a title, a date and **attendance**, and holds Agenda Points. Attendance may include **guests** from elsewhere in the organisation, attached by Exco or the General Liaison; being named on attendance grants a guest no access to anything — not the minutes, not the agenda, not the record. Logged by the General Liaison, with Exco as backstop, and kept on the **Meetings page of the Operations Hub's Compliance section**. Read by National Exco, the General Liaison and the advisory board; explicitly **not** by Super Admins. Branch, Department and Project meetings are not modelled.
_Avoid_: minutes (a Meeting holds them rather than being them), session, sitting

**Agenda Point**:
One item of business at a Meeting — a heading plus a free-text note of what was discussed or decided. Several per Meeting, each separate. Holds zero or more Action Points; an Agenda Point that produces none is ordinary, since plenty is settled in the room.
_Avoid_: agenda item, topic, minute

**Action Point**:
A piece of work somebody owes, with **owners**, a **due date**, and one **done** status. Owners are one person, several people, or a branch's **Branch Management**, which resolves to its three members at the moment of assignment. Shown **overdue**, in red, once the due date passes; nothing carries it forward and nothing copies it — it stays outstanding until it is done. Ticked done by an owner from their Dashboard, or by the General Liaison or any Exco member from the tracker, and the change reads the same everywhere at once. Reaches its owners on their Dashboard wherever they sit in the organisation, including people who may read nothing else about where it came from; whether an owner outside Exco sees the Agenda Point behind it is a per-Action-Point choice made by whoever raised it. **Has a source but does not require one** — a Meeting's Agenda Point is one source and a key result is expected to be another; an Action Point may stand alone.
_Avoid_: task (now a term of its own — a Task hangs off a parent and has an optional due date, where an Action Point stands alone and its due date is mandatory), to-do, follow-up, item

**Board**:
A named set of columns holding cards, moved between them by hand. **One mechanism, several domains** — the project board, the research board and the Media Hub's **content tracker** are instances of it, and what sits on a board is domain-specific: Tasks on a Project's, questions on Research's, accepted Content Requests on Media's. Columns belong to the board rather than to the model, which fixes only that a card sits in exactly one column at a time and that moving it is somebody's deliberate act, never a computed one. A card may carry a **blocked** flag with a free-text note; it stays in its column and reads as blocked. The flag **never names a unit** — naming one would raise the question of what that unit owes and when it clears, which is precisely the obligation the model declined to create when it refused a *Blocked* health value on Key Results.
_Avoid_: kanban, swimlane, pipeline, workflow, column (names one part of it)

**Task**:
A piece of work somebody owes **under a parent** — a Project, or an accepted Content Request — carrying **owners** that default to whoever created it, an optional **due date**, a **done** status, and an **importance** of one to three set by its creator alone, which orders the list and does nothing else. Shows its creator's name. Appears on its owners' Dashboards **when it carries a due date**, and not otherwise: an undated Task is a note to self rather than a duty.

**Renders differently in different places and is one concept regardless** — a card on a Board under a Project, a list item under a Content Request. In the Media Hub **nobody assigns a Task**: a member opts in to a Content Request and then writes their own, so creator and owner coincide by construction, and importance is that member's ordering of their own work.
_Avoid_: action point (which stands alone, has a mandatory due date and hangs off no parent), to-do, ticket, card (names the display, not the thing)

**Content Request**:
An ask for something to be made — the Media Hub's intake, open to **every member of the organisation** through the hub's Content section, which is an invitation rather than a workspace. Carries a tagline, a description, the **date wanted**, a type (post, reel, long-form) and the **channels** it is for (the website, Facebook, YouTube, Instagram, TikTok, LinkedIn and so on).

**Answered, never left to lapse.** It stands as a standing duty shared across the Media Department's members until one of them **accepts** it or **declines** it with a reason; any member may answer, and answering clears it for all of them. A request that quietly expired would let the Department refuse somebody without ever having to say no.

Accepting sets the **scheduled publish date** — the Department's promise, kept alongside the requester's date wanted rather than overwriting it, so that a date which has slipped stays visible. The accepted request becomes a card on the **content tracker** (Not started, In progress, Done, Published) and may carry Tasks. Members **opt in** freely to work on one — no approval, the Head's power to mark a member inactive within the Department being gate enough — and the opted-in members plus the Department Head are who may move its card.

**Overdue** once its scheduled publish date passes with the card short of Published: red on **everyone opted in**, or on **whoever accepted it** where nobody has — the same fallback the model uses for an unowned Key Result whose target date passes. **Published is set by hand** after posting; the platform posts to no channel and integrates with none.

The requester follows their own request from their **Dashboard** rather than from the hub, whose operational pages they cannot open, and it clears when the content is published or the request is declined. A member of the Media Department may also **create content directly**, which enters the tracker already accepted with no requester attached.
_Avoid_: brief, job, ticket, suggestion (the feature-suggestion box is a different thing), content idea (names the invitation, not the record)
